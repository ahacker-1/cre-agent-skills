# Retail Lender Criteria

Last updated: 2026-09-01

Scope: U.S. retail acquisition, refinance, and recapitalization financing fit across grocery-anchored, power, strip / unanchored, lifestyle, mall, mixed-use retail, pad, and single-tenant net lease (STNL) formats. This is an educational underwriting reference, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Confirm live lender appetite, quotes, covenants, reserves, and loan documents with active lenders and counsel. Formulas are not restated here - see [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md) and [Risk Scoring](knowledge/risk-scoring.md).

---

## Current Context

- The OCC's Semiannual Risk Perspective, Spring 2026 (published 2026-05-07) states that "Retail remains a bright spot. The vacancy rate in retail properties remains low, with little space available for lease in most markets," while flagging CRE refinance risk as warranting continued attention.
- CBRE reported (2026-07-29) U.S. retail availability unchanged at 4.9% in Q2 2026, with average asking rent of $24.79 PSF, up 2.4% year over year, on four consecutive quarters of positive net absorption.
- Trepp data reported 2026-08-11 put July 2026 retail CMBS delinquency at 6.96%, against 11.91% for office, 1.13% for industrial, and 7.86% all-property. The retail figure is concentrated in legacy mall and outlet collateral, not open-air necessity centers.
- MBA reported (2026-08-06) that Q2 2026 retail originations rose 61% year over year - the largest property-type gain - with CMBS volume up 68% and depositories up 61%, while life company volume fell 27%.
- The Federal Reserve's July 2026 Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey (released 2026-08-03) found moderate net shares of banks eased standards on nonfarm nonresidential loans, while also reporting those standards remain at relatively tight levels.

Underwriting implication: retail has more executions available than it did in 2023-2024, but the sector-level headline does not underwrite a deal. Anchor, format, trade area, lease structure, and recovery leakage decide proceeds. All figures above are directional and dated - refresh them before using them in a live credit conversation.

## Lender Lane Matrix

| Lender Type | Best Fit | Likely Friction |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship bank | Local or regional sponsor, grocery-anchored or necessity strip, moderate leverage, deposits on the table | CRE concentration position, recourse, conservative debt yield, small-balance pricing |
| Life company | Best-in-class stabilized grocery-anchored, credit-tenant, or dominant power center, low leverage, long hold | Selective on sponsor and quality; MBA volume data shows life company originations down 27% YoY in Q2 2026, so availability cannot be assumed |
| CMBS / conduit | Larger stabilized center with durable in-place cash flow, clean structure, and a defensible anchor | Cash management, tenant concentration, reserve sizing, legacy retail perception, limited flexibility post-close |
| Debt fund / bridge | Anchor backfill, redevelopment, pad splits, lease-up, discounted basis, credible business plan | Higher coupon, extension tests, milestone covenants, heavy reserves and holdbacks |
| Credit tenant lease (CTL) | STNL with an investment grade tenant and a bondable or near-bondable long lease | Requires both a qualifying credit and a qualifying lease document; larger minimum sizes |
| SBA 504 / owner-user | An operating business buying its own storefront or pad | Owner-occupancy rules; explicitly not available for investment or rental retail |

## Core Sizing Tests

Use all of these. The tightest test controls, and in retail the tightest test is frequently sustainable NOI rather than leverage.

| Test | Stabilized Retail | Transitional Retail |
|---|---|---|
| LTV | Practitioner-published life company ranges run roughly 60%-75%, general retail up to about 75% (directional, 2026-09-01) | Sized to as-is value plus reserves, not to a stabilized pro forma |
| DSCR | Practitioner-published ranges run roughly 1.25x-1.35x (directional) | Must survive anchor downtime, free rent, and leasing capital |
| Debt yield | Practitioner-published life company ranges run roughly 8%-10% (directional); run it on in-place NOI, not stabilized | Run on current NOI plus a credible stabilized case |
| Sustainable NOI | Expect a haircut to broker or issuer NOI; KBRA reported KNCF 10.4% below issuer cash flow in one pool with 43.0% retail exposure | Same, plus explicit downtime and cost-to-stabilize |
| Anchor rollover | Anchor expiring inside the loan term reduces proceeds absent a signed renewal or reserve | Reserve, holdback, or milestone required |
| Co-tenancy exposure | Screen for triggers, cure periods, and remedies before sizing | Assume the trigger fires in the downside case |
| Loan PSF | No published durable band by format - case-by-case against replacement cost and market comps | Same, judged against as-is basis |
| Recovery leakage | Underwrite billed-to-recoverable gap into NOI | Same, plus post-plan expense structure |

Loan-per-square-foot caps by retail format are intentionally left case-by-case: no primary or standards-body source publishes durable PSF bands, and basis varies too widely by market and anchor structure to default.

## How Lenders Read Retail Deterioration

CREFC's Investor Reporting Package, Version 8.4 (published 2024-03-13) defines the Servicer Watchlist triggers CMBS master servicers apply. These are surveillance thresholds rather than origination limits, but they are the clearest published statement of what institutional retail lenders treat as trouble - and a useful pre-screen. An asset that would trip several of these on a plausible anchor loss is not a clean permanent-loan candidate.

| Trigger | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Fixed rate DSCR (1E) | NCF DSCR below 1.10x (1.20x for healthcare and lodging) |
| DSCR vs underwriting (1F) | Current NCF DSCR below 75% of underwritten NCF DSCR while under 1.40x |
| Floating rate DSCR (1G) | DSCR below 1.00x and below 90% of in-place NOI as of underwriting |
| Occupancy decrease (4A) | Below 80% of underwritten occupancy (fixed rate); below 90% of in-place tenants as of underwriting (floating rate) |
| Major tenant rollover (4C) | Single tenant or any tenant above 30% of NRA expiring within 12 months for loans >= $30 million, within 6 months for loans under $30 million, or on notice of non-renewal. Fully amortizing CTL loans excluded where the lease is co-terminus with the loan |
| Top-three rollover (4D) | Top three tenants each at least 5% of NRA and cumulatively above 30% of NRA expiring within 6 months |
| Tenant bankruptcy (4E) | Bankruptcy of licensee, franchisor, or a top-three combination each at least 5% of NRA and cumulatively above 30% of NRA |
| Dark or defaulted major tenant (4F) | Major tenant lease in default, terminated, or dark above 30% of NRA |
| Operational (1B / 1C / 1D) | Non-escrowed taxes 60+ days past due; insurance 60+ days past due or force-placed; servicing advances above $10,000 aggregate for 30+ days |
| Maturity (5A) | Pending maturity or ARD with balloon balance inside 90 days |

The 4C through 4F cluster is the anchor and go-dark test. 4A is the practical co-tenancy proxy.

## Retail-Specific Credit Questions

Ask before sizing debt:

- Who is the anchor, is it a lease or an owned pad, and is there an REA or OEA the borrower does not control?
- When does the anchor expire relative to loan maturity, and are renewal options at, below, or above market?
- Which inline leases carry co-tenancy triggers, and what are the trigger thresholds, cure periods, and remedies - rent reduction, alternative rent, or termination?
- Does the anchor or any major tenant hold a go-dark right, and does going dark break the co-tenancy chain?
- What exclusives and use restrictions constrain the backfill pool for the anchor and the largest inline boxes?
- What are reported tenant sales PSF and occupancy cost ratios by tenant, and how many tenants are above a healthy range?
- How much of NOI is percentage rent, specialty and temporary income, or signed-not-open rent?
- What is the CAM recovery structure - pro rata, caps, base year, gross-up, admin fee, capital recovery, anchor carve-outs - and what is the billed-to-recoverable gap?
- What is the cash cost to reach underwritten occupancy, including TI, LC, downtime, and anchor-box demising or capital work?

## Reserves, Holdbacks, and Recourse

- Reserve sizing for TI, LC, replacement, anchor rollover, and co-tenancy cure is deal-specific and sized by lenders to the actual rent roll, lease documents, and property condition report. State the mechanism, not a number.
- Common retail structures include upfront TI/LC reserves, springing anchor reserves that fund on non-renewal notice, free-rent and gap-rent holdbacks disbursed monthly, and cash management or lockbox activation on a DSCR or tenant trigger.
- Recourse posture is case-by-case. CMBS and CTL are typically non-recourse with standard carve-outs, but the carve-out schedule is deal-specific; bank, debt fund, and life company recourse varies with sponsor, market, and leverage.
- The interagency Policy Statement on Prudent Commercial Real Estate Loan Accommodations and Workouts (FIL-23-2023, issued 2023-06-29) tells examiners that prudent modifications for borrowers with demonstrated repayment capacity are not criticized, and that a modified loan is not adversely classified solely because collateral value fell below the balance. That is the regulatory room a bank has to extend rather than force a maturity default - it is not a reason the refinance math works.

## Lane Constraints Worth Knowing Before You Call

| Constraint | Detail |
|---|---|
| Bank CRE concentration | The 2006 interagency guidance (OCC Bulletin 2006-46) screens institutions where construction, land, and land development loans reach 100% or more of total risk-based capital, or total CRE reaches 300% or more of capital with 50%+ growth over the prior 36 months. Exceeding those criteria triggers supervisory analysis, not a hard cap - but it is why a clean center can still be declined |
| Percentage rent | Lenders generally give no credit toward debt service. The practitioner formulation is direct: "You can spend the percentage rent we just won't let you borrow against it." Treat overage rent as equity upside and as a tenant-health signal |
| Sustainable NOI | Rating agency practice divides sustainable net cash flow by an asset-specific cap rate rather than accepting appraised value; KBRA reported values 40.0% below third-party appraisals in one pool with 43.0% retail exposure. Expect the same directional discipline from a credit committee |
| CTL for STNL | One published program (PGIM Private Capital) offers up to 100% LTV based on the present value of lease obligations, $25 million to $100+ million, and 20+ year maturities, targeting investment grade tenants with below-investment-grade case by case. Both the credit and the lease document must qualify |
| SBA 504 | Up to $5 million, or up to $5.5 million for certain projects, and explicitly ineligible for "speculation or investment in rental real estate." Occupancy practice commonly cited by CDCs is 51% of an existing facility or 60% immediately for new construction; verify against the current SOP with the CDC |

## Red Flags

- Anchor expiring before loan maturity with no signed renewal, no reserve, and no backfill plan.
- Anchor dark but paying: rent continues, traffic collapses, inline sales fall, and co-tenancy clocks may already be running.
- Anchor rent well below market, so in-place NOI looks strong and the renewal or replacement case is punitive.
- Co-tenancy triggers concentrated in the same inline leases that carry the best credit, creating a single-event cascade.
- NOI materially dependent on percentage rent, specialty income, or signed-not-open leases.
- Exclusives or use restrictions that shrink the realistic backfill pool for the anchor or a major box.
- A recovery structure with caps, base years, or anchor carve-outs that leaves a permanent billed-to-recoverable gap not reflected in underwritten NOI.
- Site plan, parking, or redevelopment rights controlled by an REA or OEA the borrower does not control.
- Occupancy cost ratios above a healthy range across a meaningful share of inline GLA, signaling renewal risk that a current rent roll hides.
- CMBS execution assumed for an asset whose story needs active lender flexibility after close.
- SBA proposed for an investor-owned center - a structural disqualification, not a negotiation point.
- CTL assumed for a below-investment-grade tenant or a lease that is not bondable.
