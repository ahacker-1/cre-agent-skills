# Retail Benchmarks

Last updated: 2026-09-01

Scope: U.S. retail acquisition, refinancing, and asset review across grocery-anchored, power, strip / unanchored, lifestyle, mall, outlet, mixed-use, pad, and single-tenant net lease (STNL) assets. This is an educational underwriting reference, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Every market figure below is directional and dated. Confirm current trade-area comps, actual rent roll terms, reported tenant sales, and live lender pricing before relying on any number here. For formulas, use [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md); for scoring, use [Risk Scoring](knowledge/risk-scoring.md).

---

## Current Context

- CBRE reported U.S. retail availability unchanged at 4.9% in Q2 2026, average asking rent of $24.79 PSF (up 2.4% year over year), a fourth consecutive quarter of positive net absorption, and historically low construction completions.
- Cushman & Wakefield reported national shopping-center vacancy at 6.0% in Q2 2026, up 3 bps quarter over quarter and below the 7.4% historical average, with asking rents up 2.2% year over year to $25.65 PSF, 2.3 million SF delivered in the quarter, and a pipeline under 0.3% of existing inventory.
- JLL reported Q2 2026 net absorption of 10.2 million SF across 11.8 billion SF of inventory after a negative Q1 2026 (-4.5 million SF), with general retail leading at roughly 7 million SF and malls and neighborhood centers turning positive; national rent growth eased to 1.7% year over year, ranging from Charlotte at +6.0% to Los Angeles at -1.0%.
- The U.S. Census Bureau put e-commerce at 17.1% of total retail sales in Q2 2026 (seasonally adjusted), up from 16.0% in Q1 2025; e-commerce grew 12.2% year over year against 6.7% for total retail sales.
- JLL reported H1 2026 U.S. retail investment volume of $33.0 billion, up 14% year over year and the strongest first half since 2022, with cap rates compressing across retail sub-types. CBRE's H1 2026 Cap Rate Survey (completed late June 2026) found average cap rates broadly stable, with neighborhood retail compressing more than other sectors.

Underwriting implication: national vacancy is tight and supply is thin, but absorption is carried by freestanding and general retail, not by shopping centers. Underwrite the trade area and the rent roll, not the national headline.

## Format Definitions

Ranges below are the ICSC U.S. shopping-center classification as published. Anchor GLA share is the cleanest structural separator between formats.

| Format | Typical GLA | Anchors | Anchor GLA Share | Trade Area | Underwriting Driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Super-regional mall | 800,000+ SF | 3+ | 50%-70% | 5-25 miles | Sales PSF, anchor commitments, REA/OEA control, redevelopment basis |
| Regional mall | 400,000-800,000 SF | 2+ | 50%-70% | 5-15 miles | Occupancy cost headroom, anchor vacancy, capital plan |
| Community center | 125,000-400,000 SF | 2+ | 40%-60% | 3-6 miles | Anchor credit and term, shop depth, co-tenancy |
| Neighborhood center (typically grocery-anchored) | 30,000-125,000 SF | 1+ (supermarket) | 30%-50% | 3 miles | Grocer sales and term, shop occupancy, CAM recovery |
| Strip / convenience (unanchored) | Under 30,000 SF | None or convenience anchor | n/a | Under 1 mile | Tenant granularity, rollover depth, credit thinness |
| Power center | 250,000-600,000 SF | 3+ | 70%-90% | 5-10 miles | Big-box release risk, dark-box supply, go-dark and co-tenancy |
| Lifestyle | 150,000-500,000 SF | 0-2 | 0%-50% | 8-12 miles | Merchandising, F&B share, high shop capital load |
| Factory outlet | 50,000-400,000 SF | n/a | n/a | 25-75 miles | Sales PSF, short terms, tourism dependence |

ICSC does not define grocery-anchored, mixed-use retail, pad, or STNL as separate formats. Grocery-anchored is a tenancy description that usually sits in the neighborhood or community band. Pad and STNL are ownership and lease-structure descriptions: underwrite them on lease term, credit, rent-to-market, and residual use, not on center metrics.

## Occupancy and Leasing Reference Points (FY2025 public filings)

These are large-portfolio, institutional-quality figures disclosed for the year ended December 31, 2025. Definitions differ by issuer and do not reconcile across companies. Use them as a ceiling reference, not as a target for a single Class B asset.

| Format | Reference Point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Grocery-anchored / open-air | 96.1% leased portfolio-wide; 98.0% anchor space, 93.2% shop space; total ABR $25.91 PSF; rent spreads +10.8% | Regency Centers 10-K |
| Grocery-anchored / open-air | 96.6% leased consolidated (458 centers, 79.5M SF); ABR per leased SF $20.36 to $21.05; average center 177,308 SF | Kimco 10-K |
| Mixed-use / community | 96.1% leased vs 94.1% occupied (gap attributed to redevelopment, improvement, and permitting) | Federal Realty 10-K |
| Mall / premium outlet | 96.4% ending occupancy; average base minimum rent $60.97 PSF; average tenant allowance on new leases $63.92 PSF | Simon 10-K |
| Mall | 94.0% leased occupancy; releasing spread +6.7%; ABR $66.92 PSF (spaces under 10,000 SF) | Macerich 10-K |
| Outlet | 98% occupancy; average annual base rent $27.77 PSF | Tanger 10-K |
| STNL | 98.9% leased across 15,511 properties; WALT 8.8 years; rent recapture 103.9%; 1%-4% of portfolio available at any time | Realty Income 10-K |
| STNL | 98.3% of properties leased; WALT 10.2 years; FY2025 acquisitions at 7.4% weighted average cap rate, dispositions at 6.4% | NNN REIT 10-K |

The 400-500 bps spread between anchor occupancy and shop occupancy is structural in open-air centers, not a distress signal on its own. Always request the split before accepting a blended leased rate.

## Leasing Capital (TI/TA and LC)

Regency's FY2025 pro-rata disclosure is the clearest published split of retail leasing capital by space type and deal type.

| Space | Deal | Base Rent PSF | Allowance + Landlord Work PSF | Commissions PSF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor (10,000 SF+) | New | $17.46 | $28.67 | $4.65 |
| Anchor | Renewal | $15.14 | $0.65 | $0.41 |
| Shop (under 10,000 SF) | New | $43.16 | $51.12 | $17.37 |
| Shop | Renewal | $40.89 | $1.45 | $1.30 |
| All leases | Blended | $27.46 | $12.58 | $3.89 |

Two decisions follow. First, renewal capital ran roughly 1% to 4% of new-deal capital in FY2025, so retention assumptions move capital-adjusted cash flow more than rent assumptions do. Second, do not treat any of these as a fixed PSF default: the same table for FY2024 shows anchor new-deal allowance at $61.64 PSF against $28.67 PSF in FY2025. Size leasing capital from actual deal terms and the market's current concession posture, and note the year of any benchmark you cite.

Capex reserve and operating expense PSF by format are not published here. No reviewed primary source supports a national default, so they stay case-by-case and should be built from the property's own operating statements and capital plan.

## Occupancy Cost and Sales PSF

Occupancy cost ratio = (base rent + CAM reimbursement + real estate tax reimbursement + percentage rent) / tenant gross sales. Tanger's disclosed definition also includes insurance and advertising and promotion; confirm which components a reported ratio includes before comparing it to anything.

| Reference | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mall occupancy cost, consolidated | 11.8% of sales in 2025 (8.1% minimum rent, 0.6% percentage rent, 3.1% recoveries); 11.8% in 2024, 12.1% in 2023 | Macerich 10-K |
| Mall occupancy cost, JV centers | 11.6% of sales in 2025 | Macerich 10-K |
| Outlet occupancy cost | 9.7% in 2025, 9.5% in 2024, 9.3% in 2023, 8.6% in 2022, 8.1% in 2021 | Tanger 10-K |
| Mall tenant sales PSF (under 10,000 SF) | $881 TTM at 12/31/2025 vs $837 a year earlier; comparable sales +1.2%, traffic flat | Macerich 10-K |

Category-level thresholds (grocery, restaurant, apparel, fitness, service) are deliberately not published here. No primary or quasi-primary source reviewed publishes them, and the strongest practitioner treatment available states that a healthy ratio depends on the retail type, the tenant, and how the store compares to that tenant's other locations. Get the tenant's reported sales, compute the ratio, and compare it to that tenant's own fleet. Sales PSF for grocery, power-center, and STNL tenants is not publicly benchmarked and must come from sales reports, estoppels, or lease reporting covenants.

## Recoveries, Percentage Rent, and Expense Load

Derived from Regency's FY2025 same-property pro-rata disclosure (base rent $1,130.0M, recoveries $404.3M, percentage rent $15.5M, total real estate revenue $1,586.2M; operating and maintenance $265.6M, real estate taxes $205.7M, ground rent $15.0M, total operating expenses $486.4M):

- Recoveries equaled about 86% of operating and maintenance plus real estate taxes. A stabilized grocery-anchored center recovering materially less deserves a structural explanation (caps, exclusions, gross leases, anchor fixed-CAM deals, or vacancy leakage).
- Total real estate operating expenses equaled about 31% of total real estate revenue.
- Percentage rent equaled about 1.0% of total real estate revenue.

Percentage rent is material only in mall and outlet formats, where Macerich reports it at 0.6% of tenant sales. Realty Income reports percentage rent at less than 1% of rental revenue in STNL. Do not underwrite percentage rent growth in strip, unanchored, or STNL models.

## E-Commerce Exposure and Absorption

Census data shows e-commerce share rising roughly 80 to 110 bps per year (16.0% of retail sales in Q1 2025 to 17.1% in Q2 2026), with the growth concentrated in the non-store channel itself. JLL's Q2 2026 category read (Census-sourced) showed sporting goods, hobby, and books up 13.7% year over year, online and non-store retail up 12.6%, electronics up 8.0%, clothing up 5.6%, and furniture and home furnishings down 1.1%.

- Structurally resilient in physical space: grocery and food retail, quick-service and full-service restaurants, medical and dental, fitness, personal services, pharmacy, off-price and discount, home improvement, pet, and automotive service. These carry the necessity, service, and value demand that Census and JLL both show holding.
- Structurally exposed: categories whose growth is running in the non-store channel, including consumer electronics, media, general soft goods without an experiential or off-price angle, and furniture and home furnishings, which was the one Q2 2026 category in year-over-year decline.
- Absorption and lease-up: JLL reported 10.2 million SF of national net absorption in Q2 2026 after -4.5 million SF in Q1 2026, while Cushman & Wakefield reported only 708,000 SF of shopping-center absorption in the same quarter and -2.7 million SF year to date. Freestanding and general retail is carrying national demand. Lease-up assumptions for shop space in a shopping center should be built from local comps and signed LOIs, not from the national absorption headline.
- Supply is thin but uneven: JLL reported a national average of 16.4 SF under construction per 100 residents against 95.9 in Dallas-Fort Worth. Sun Belt lease-up needs a local pipeline check.

## Red Flags

- A strong blended leased rate that hides shop-space weakness. Ask for the anchor-versus-shop split every time.
- A wide leased-to-occupied gap presented as benign. Confirm whether it is redevelopment and permitting or signed-not-open leases that will not pay rent for several quarters.
- Occupancy cost ratios built on unaudited self-reported sales. Simon's own risk disclosure notes that curbside pickup, ship-from-store, and in-store online returns can distort reported store sales.
- Anchor replacement modeled as a leasing assumption. Anchor new-deal allowance can exceed several years of that anchor's base rent, as in Regency's FY2024 disclosure of $61.64 PSF allowance against $20.06 PSF base rent.
- Percentage rent growth underwritten in a strip, unanchored, or STNL model, where the disclosure shows it is immaterial.
- A national vacancy or rent figure used as a submarket proxy. JLL's published market rent growth ranged from +6.0% to -1.0% in Q2 2026.
- Institutional REIT portfolio occupancy applied to a single Class B asset in a secondary trade area.
- A retail cap rate asserted without a comp. This file publishes no cap-rate matrix by format; the only figures stated are portfolio-level STNL transaction evidence (NNN acquisitions at 7.4% and dispositions at 6.4% in FY2025; Realty Income investments at a 7.3% initial cash yield), which are not market cap rates for a specific asset.
