# Rescue Capital Comparator

Compare preferred equity, mezzanine debt, JV equity, bridge debt, note purchase, discounted payoff, and sale alternatives.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when a CRE asset has a refinance gap, maturity shortfall, operating deficit, capex need, or broken capital stack that may require new third-party capital.

---

## What You'll Need to Provide

- Current capital stack and ownership structure
- Existing loan payoff, maturity, and consent requirements
- Refinance gap or capital need
- Current and stabilized NOI
- Business plan, capex, leasing, and exit assumptions
- Sponsor contribution and desired control economics
- Indicative terms from rescue capital providers, if available

---

## Mission

Rank the viable rescue-capital alternatives by certainty, cost, control, dilution, consent risk, and exit feasibility.

---

## Strategy

### Step 1: Define the Capital Need

Break the need into:

- loan paydown
- reserve requirement
- capex
- TI/LC or lease-up funding
- operating deficit
- transaction costs
- contingency

### Step 2: Identify Viable Capital Sources

Screen:

- sponsor equity
- third-party common equity
- preferred equity
- mezzanine debt
- bridge or stretch senior debt
- note purchase
- discounted payoff capital
- sale

### Step 3: Compare Economics

For each source, identify:

- amount
- rate, preferred return, or target return
- current pay vs accrual
- fees
- minimum multiple
- redemption date
- upside participation
- dilution
- control rights

### Step 4: Compare Execution Risk

Score:

- time to close
- senior-lender consent
- intercreditor or recognition agreement
- appraisal or valuation dependency
- legal complexity
- sponsor alignment
- exit path

### Step 5: Recommend Capital Strategy

Rank alternatives and state:

- best overall path
- fastest path
- least dilutive path
- highest certainty path
- paths to reject

---

## Quality Checks

- Separates paydown need from operating and capex need
- Scores consent and intercreditor risk
- Flags sponsor dilution and control changes
- Includes exit feasibility
- Does not recommend preferred equity or mezzanine without senior-lender consent analysis

---

## Red Flags & Dealbreakers

- Rescue capital has no realistic repayment or redemption path
- Control triggers are likely to be tripped immediately
- New money only delays default
- Sponsor dilution removes execution incentive
- Required senior-lender consent is unlikely
- Total cost of capital consumes remaining asset value

---

## When Data is Missing

- If term sheets are missing, compare structures qualitatively
- If senior-loan consent rights are unknown, mark subordinate capital as conditional
- If valuation is missing, stress outcomes under low-value case
- If sponsor preferences are unknown, show control and dilution consequences clearly

---

## Confidence Scoring

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| HIGH | Capital need, existing documents, value, business plan, sponsor preferences, and indicative terms are available |
| MEDIUM | Need and business plan are available, but capital terms or consent rights are incomplete |
| LOW | High-level gap only; no term sheets or consent details |
