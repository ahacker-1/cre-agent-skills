# Workout and Extension Structures

Reference guide for commercial real estate loan extensions, modifications, forbearance, discounted payoffs, deeds-in-lieu, and lender-facing workout packages.

---

## Decision Frame

A workout is not one structure. It is a negotiated path that should solve a specific problem:

- Time problem: the asset is viable but maturity arrived too early
- Cash-flow problem: NOI cannot cover debt service during transition
- Value problem: current value cannot support the payoff
- Control problem: lender wants more oversight before granting relief
- Exit problem: borrower needs time to sell, refinance, lease, or recapitalize

The right structure should match the problem. A short extension does not solve a value gap. A forbearance does not solve an asset with no credible exit.

---

## Common Structures

| Structure | What It Does | Best Fit | Main Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short extension | Moves maturity date | Active refinance, sale, or closing delay | Fees, paydown, tighter reporting |
| Performance extension | Extends if milestones are met | Lease-up, sale process, capex completion | Milestone default risk |
| Loan modification | Changes economics or covenants | Viable asset needs revised terms | Lender approvals and legal cost |
| Forbearance | Lender agrees not to enforce for a period | Default exists but plan is credible | Does not waive default permanently |
| A/B note split | Separates supportable A note from hope-value B note | Value impairment with upside path | Complex documents and control |
| Discounted payoff | Borrower pays agreed discounted amount | Lender wants clean exit | Requires capital and release clarity |
| Note sale | Lender sells debt to third party | Lender wants liquidity | New creditor may be more aggressive |
| Deed-in-lieu | Borrower transfers collateral to lender | No viable refi or sale, clean handover | Tax, guaranty, release, title risk |
| Receivership / foreclosure | Court or lender enforcement path | Failed consensual resolution | Expensive, slow, value-destructive |

---

## Extension Terms to Negotiate

- Extension fee
- Paydown amount
- Default interest waiver or accrual
- Covenant reset
- Cash sweep
- Lockbox or cash-management trigger
- Tax, insurance, capex, leasing, or debt-service reserves
- Required reporting cadence
- Milestone tests
- Updated appraisal or broker opinion
- Guarantor support
- Additional collateral
- Release of claims
- Expense reimbursement

Each term should be tied to a specific lender concern. Avoid random concessions.

---

## Workout Package Contents

Minimum lender package:

1. Executive summary and ask
2. Current loan facts and maturity timeline
3. Current collateral status
4. T-12, budget, rent roll, and occupancy trend
5. NOI bridge from underwriting to current performance
6. Sponsor liquidity and contribution plan
7. Business plan with dates and responsible parties
8. Capital budget and reserve request
9. Refinance, sale, recap, or payoff path
10. Proposed structure and lender protections
11. Sensitivities and downside plan
12. Document checklist and requested decision date

---

## Lender Concerns to Answer

- Is the asset viable or just being delayed?
- Is the borrower contributing real capital?
- Is the current value below the payoff?
- Is a sale or refinance realistic?
- Are taxes, insurance, and life-safety items current?
- Are guarantors cooperative and solvent?
- Is cash being trapped or leaking?
- Is the borrower asking early enough?
- Does the proposed relief maximize recovery compared with enforcement?

---

## Borrower Protections to Track

- Confirm whether default is waived, cured, or merely forborne
- Define exactly which defaults are covered
- Avoid overbroad releases unless counsel approves
- Preserve property-level operating authority where possible
- Understand tax effects of discounted payoff or debt forgiveness
- Confirm guaranty exposure and carveout triggers
- Confirm future cure rights
- Confirm whether lender can sell the note during the standstill

---

## Red Flags

- Borrower asks for time without a funded plan
- Plan depends on a speculative sale price
- Guarantor refuses financial disclosure
- Taxes, insurance, or payroll are unpaid
- Collateral has unresolved life-safety or environmental issues
- Lender fees and legal costs are not budgeted
- Requested structure requires consent from parties not yet identified
- Workout burns cash but leaves maturity unresolved
