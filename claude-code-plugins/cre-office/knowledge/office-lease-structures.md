# Office Lease Structures

Last updated: 2026-06-16

Scope: U.S. office leases for acquisition, lender diligence, lease abstraction, rollover analysis, and asset management. This is an issue-spotting reference, not legal advice. Confirm all lease interpretation with counsel.

---

## Common Office Lease Forms

| Structure | Who Pays Operating Costs | Office Use Case | Main Diligence Issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-service gross | Landlord pays most operating costs from base rent | Multi-tenant office, especially larger CBD assets | Whether "full-service" still has exclusions, after-hours HVAC, parking, or special charges |
| Modified gross / base-year | Landlord covers base-year expenses; tenant pays increases over the base | Very common in multi-tenant office | Base-year reset, gross-up, controllable expense caps, exclusions |
| Expense stop | Landlord covers costs up to a dollar amount; tenant pays excess | Older or smaller office assets | Whether stop reflects real operating cost level |
| Triple net | Tenant pays taxes, insurance, maintenance, and operating costs | Single-tenant office, medical office, owner-user style assets | Capital repairs, roof/structure, replacement obligations |
| Percentage / hybrid | Rare for office, sometimes mixed-use retail component | Mixed-use projects | Allocate office vs retail expenses separately |

## Base-Year and Expense Pass-Through Checklist

For every office lease, abstract:

- Base year or expense stop.
- Tenant pro rata share and denominator.
- Whether expenses are grossed up to stabilized occupancy.
- Controllable vs uncontrollable expense caps.
- Exclusions from operating expenses.
- Treatment of capital repairs, code compliance, energy upgrades, and replacements.
- Real estate tax reassessment language.
- Audit rights and audit window.
- Administrative or management fee add-ons.
- After-hours HVAC, overtime services, and special cleaning/security charges.

Red flags:

- Base year is artificially high or low.
- Capital items can be passed through without useful-life amortization.
- Expense denominator excludes vacant space in a way that shifts vacancy cost to tenants.
- Mixed-use building expenses are not allocated by use.
- Landlord cannot recover rising taxes, insurance, utilities, or security costs.

## Rentable Area, Usable Area, and BOMA

Office lease economics depend on area measurement.

Required checks:

- Confirm rentable square feet (RSF), usable square feet (USF), and load factor.
- Confirm the measurement standard, commonly ANSI/BOMA for office.
- Confirm whether the lease locks the stated rentable area or allows remeasurement.
- Compare quoted rent on a rentable basis to tenant occupancy planning on a usable basis.
- For government tenants, check GSA or agency measurement conventions.

Useful formulas:

```text
Load Factor = Rentable Area / Usable Area
Usable Area = Rentable Area / Load Factor
Annual Base Rent = Rentable Area x Stated Rent PSF
```

Do not compare office rents without confirming whether figures are stated on RSF or USF.

## Tenant Improvements and Work Letters

Office leases often include a work letter. Abstract:

- TI allowance per RSF.
- What costs are eligible: hard costs, soft costs, cabling, security, permits, design, project management.
- Whether the allowance is landlord-controlled, tenant-controlled, reimbursement-based, or direct-paid.
- Disbursement conditions, lien waivers, default restrictions, percentage-of-completion requirements.
- Use-it-or-lose-it vs credit for unused allowance.
- Who owns the improvements for accounting and removal.
- Substantial completion definition and rent commencement.
- Punch list, delays, force majeure, and tenant delay rules.

Red flags:

- Rent starts before usable completion due to tenant-delay dispute.
- TI allowance is below fit-out cost, but excess cost funding is unclear.
- Spec suite or prebuilt assumptions do not match tenant requirements.
- Landlord capital obligation exceeds available liquidity.

## Options and Control Rights

Office options have major value impact. Abstract:

- Renewal options: notice window, term, rent-setting method, conditions.
- Expansion rights: ROFO, ROFR, must-take space, contiguous space.
- Contraction rights: dates, fees, restoration, conditions.
- Termination rights: dates, fees, free-rent/TI payback.
- Assignment and sublease: consent standard, recapture, profit sharing, affiliate transfers.
- Exclusive use or signage rights.
- Parking rights and ratios.
- Antenna, generator, data room, riser, storage, and after-hours HVAC rights.

Red flags:

- Major tenant can terminate before debt maturity.
- Expansion option blocks lease-up of adjacent vacancy.
- Contraction rights create hidden vacancy.
- Sublease rights allow direct competition at discounted rent.
- Renewal option is below market or uses tenant-friendly fair market rent language.
