# Construction Contracts and Draw Controls

Last updated: 2026-09-01

Scope: U.S. ground-up development and heavy redevelopment. This reference covers owner-contractor contract structure, the money mechanics inside those contracts, and the monthly draw controls that owners, owner representatives, and construction lenders rely on. It is an educational decision-support reference, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Lien law, retainage caps, prompt-payment rules, and bond requirements are state-specific and change; confirm the governing law, the executed contract language, and the lender's own draw requirements with counsel and the deal team. AIA form section numbers are used as common vocabulary, but nearly every executed form is modified by supplementary conditions, so read the signed document.

---

## Current Context

- California SB 61 added Civil Code section 8811, capping retention on private construction contracts at 5% for contracts entered into on or after January 1, 2026, requiring negotiated reductions to flow down to subcontractors, and making the cap non-waivable with a mandatory attorney fee award ([Stoel Rives](https://www.stoel.com/insights/publications/new-california-law-limits-retention-on-private-construction-projects-to-5), accessed 2026-09-01).
- Texas runs the other direction on the owner side: Property Code section 53.101 requires an owner to reserve 10% of the contract price or the value of work completed during the work and for 30 days after completion ([Texas Property Code 53.101](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_53.101), accessed 2026-09-01).
- HUD's MAP Guide (4430.G, March 2021 revision), Chapter 12, requires a 10% holdback on construction items until 50% completion for FHA-insured multifamily construction loans, with a permitted step-down to 5% at 50% and 2.5% at 75% held to Final Endorsement, conditioned on no identity of interest above 5%, prior written surety consent, and clean performance ([HUD MAP Guide](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/OCHCO/documents/4430GHSGG.pdf), accessed 2026-09-01).
- FAR 28.102-1 (FAC 2026-01, effective 2026-03-13) requires Miller Act performance and payment bonds on federal construction contracts over $150,000, with alternative payment protections from $35,001 to $150,000 ([Acquisition.gov](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/28.102-1), accessed 2026-09-01).
- AIA's contingency guidance cites 5%-10% as a common construction contingency and 5%-10% of construction cost as a common design contingency, and states that unused contingency reverts to the owner ([AIA](https://www.aia.org/resource-center/managing-the-contingency-allowance), accessed 2026-09-01).

## Contract Families and What Each Allocates

Delivery method (who builds and when they join) and contracting format (how they are paid) are separate choices. CMAA's owner guidance treats them as independent variables, and conflating them is a common source of confusion in development files.

| Form | Relationship and Basis of Payment | Cost Overrun Sits With | Owner Gets | Watch For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A101 | Owner and contractor, stipulated sum | Contractor | Price certainty | Only works with complete, tightly defined design; savings stay with the contractor |
| A102 | Owner and contractor, cost of the work plus a fee with a GMP | Contractor above the GMP; owner below it | Cost cap plus open-book visibility | Definition of Cost of the Work, contingency location, savings split |
| A133 | Owner and construction manager as constructor, cost plus fee with a GMP | Contractor above the GMP | Preconstruction input, then a GMP Amendment (Exhibit A) that binds the CM to build at or below the GMP | Design completeness at GMP; qualifications and assumptions attached to the GMP |
| A134 | Same CMc relationship, no GMP | Owner | Speed and flexibility | No cap; needs strong cost controls |
| A201 | General conditions adopted by the agreement | n/a | Uniform allocation of duties among owner, contractor, architect | Supplementary conditions that rewrite it |
| B101 | Owner and architect | n/a | Design scope and construction-phase administration | Whether the architect certifies pay apps and inspects for completion |

Per AIA's GMP guidance, if the Cost of the Work plus the contractor's fee exceeds the GMP, the contractor pays the excess from its own funds. AIA's A133-2019 instructions describe the two-phase structure: preconstruction advice first, then a construction phase that begins when the owner accepts the GMP.

## Money Mechanics Inside the Contract

| Item | What It Is | Default Behavior | Question to Ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allowance | Placeholder for scope not yet selected; A201 section 3.8 puts allowances in the contract sum and requires the owner to select items with reasonable promptness | Covers materials and equipment plus related costs; other contractor costs stay in the contract sum | What is in and out of each allowance, and who eats the overage? |
| Contractor's contingency | Contractor-controlled reserve for unforeseen conditions inside the GMP | Contractor draws it; unused amount reverts to the owner per AIA guidance | Is a draw on contingency shown separately from a change order? |
| Owner's contingency | Owner-controlled reserve outside the contract, in the development budget | Funds scope change and owner-side risk | Is it sized against remaining scope, not against elapsed time? |
| Unit prices | Pre-agreed rates for quantities that cannot be fixed at signing; A201 9.1.2 and 7.3 | Applied to actual measured quantity | Are the assumed quantities realistic, and is there a quantity band? |
| Savings / buyout | Difference between GMP and final cost of the work plus fee | Negotiated: some agreements return all savings to the owner, others split | Where do buyout savings go, and can they refill contingency? |
| Liquidated damages | Stipulated daily delay damages | Enforceable only if damages were hard to estimate at signing and the amount is a reasonable estimate | Does the clause allow apportionment for owner-caused delay? |

Contingency ranges of 5%-10% (construction) and 5%-10% of construction cost (design) come from AIA guidance and are directional starting points, not required numbers. Savings-split percentages, contractor fee percentages, general conditions as a percentage of cost, and liquidated damages dollar amounts are negotiated and market-specific; this reference does not carry a default for any of them.

## Changes: Change Order vs Construction Change Directive

| Instrument | A201 Section | Who Must Agree | Price and Time Set? | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Change Order | 7.2 | Owner, contractor, and architect | Yes | Adjusts contract sum and contract time |
| Construction Change Directive | 7.3 | Owner and architect; contractor may or may not agree | No, priced later | Contractor must proceed promptly (7.3.6); interim payment governed by 7.3.9 |
| Minor change | 7.4 | Architect orders it | No change | No adjustment to sum or time |
| Unauthorized work | 7.1.4 | n/a | n/a | Except in emergency, changes made without prior authorization are at contractor risk |

A Construction Change Directive is the pressure valve when work cannot wait for agreement on price. It is also where cost silently accumulates: a directive open for months means the contract sum, the cost to complete, and the schedule in the draw package are all stale.

## Completion, Retainage, and Correction

| Milestone | A201 Section | What It Triggers |
|---|---|---|
| Substantial Completion | 9.8.1 | Work sufficiently complete for the owner to occupy or use for its intended purpose |
| Certificate of Substantial Completion | 9.8.4 | Fixes the date; allocates security, maintenance, heat, utilities, damage, and insurance; sets the time to finish punch items |
| Partial occupancy | 9.9 | Owner use of a portion after joint inspection; not by itself acceptance |
| Final Completion and final payment | 9.10 | Final inspection plus evidence that debts and claims are paid or satisfied and liens released |
| Waiver on final payment | 9.10.4, 9.10.5 | Owner waives claims except stated carve-outs; contractor and subs waive claims except those previously identified |
| One-year correction period | 12.2.2.1 | Runs from Substantial Completion; owner must give prompt notice or waives the right to require correction |

Retainage is where state law overrides contract habit. The two common poles are a 5% cap (California private work for contracts on or after 2026-01-01) and a 10% requirement (Texas owner reserve; HUD-insured multifamily to 50% completion). Reductions at milestones are common but, on bonded work, HUD requires prior written surety consent, and private sureties raise the same objection. Confirm the governing state's cap before drafting or accepting a retainage schedule.

## The Draw Package and What Each Piece Proves

| Document | What It Proves | Failure Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule of values (A201 9.2) | The agreed cost breakdown that becomes the basis for every application | Front-loaded lines; general conditions carried as one undifferentiated block |
| G702 application and certificate | Contract sum, change orders, completed and stored to date, retainage, prior payments, current payment due; contractor applies, architect certifies | Certification signed without an inspection behind it |
| G703 continuation sheet | Each schedule of values line with this period, previously completed, stored materials, retainage, and balance to finish | Percent complete asserted rather than measured |
| Invoice summary and supporting invoices | Cost actually incurred, above the lender's backup threshold | Backup missing for soft costs, developer fee, and interest reserve |
| Change order log | Executed and pending changes and their budget impact | Pending directives not shown; contingency draws hidden as change orders |
| Stored materials support (A201 9.3.2) | On-site storage, or off-site with advance owner approval, agreed location, clear title free of liens, and insurance | Off-site material funded with no title evidence or insurance |
| Lien waivers | Release of lien, stop-notice, and bond rights for the work paid | Sequence reversed; wrong statutory form |
| Certificates of insurance | Builder's risk, general liability, workers compensation in force | Coverage lapses mid-project; builder's risk limit below contract sum |
| Third-party inspector report | Independent percentage of completion, photos, stored materials, change order observations | Inspector percentage never reconciled to the pay application |
| Title date-down endorsement | Priority preserved through the date of this advance | Skipped or out-of-sequence date-down |

HUD's process is a useful reference model for lender-side control: interim advances are approved by the lender based on the inspector's approval of the construction amount (form HUD-92403), with completion percentage recorded on the inspector's trip report (form HUD-95379), and HUD will not authorize interim advances exceeding documented percentage of completion less applicable retainage.

## Lien Waivers, Title, Bonds, and Insurance

**Lien waivers.** Twelve states prescribe statutory forms; California is the clearest model, with four forms under Civil Code sections 8132 (conditional on progress payment), 8134 (unconditional on progress payment), 8136 (conditional on final payment), and 8138 (unconditional on final payment), each of which must substantially follow the statute to be effective. A conditional waiver is effective only on actual receipt of payment. An unconditional waiver states the claimant has been paid and binds on signature even if payment never arrives. The practical sequencing rule that follows: conditional waivers accompany the current draw request, unconditional waivers follow proof that the prior draw was funded. California also voids waivers signed in advance of the work; other states differ, so treat advance waiver language as a counsel question.

**Title date-downs.** ALTA Endorsement 32 (with 32.1 and 32.2 variants keyed to who disburses) gives the construction lender mechanic's lien priority coverage in place of the policy's Covered Risk 11(a). ALTA Endorsement 33 is the date-down issued with each subsequent advance, moving the Date of Coverage forward and increasing coverage for that advance. Each ALTA 33 protects only against lien rights accrued as of its Date of Coverage, which is exactly why a skipped date-down or a disbursement made out of sequence opens a priority gap.

**Bonds.** On federal work, FAR 28.102-1 requires performance and payment bonds over $150,000 and alternative payment protections from $35,001 to $150,000; many states impose parallel "little Miller Act" requirements on public work. On private work, bonding is a negotiated credit decision, weighed against a subcontractor default insurance program or a parent guaranty. Whichever is used, confirm the penal sum, the obligee, and whether the lender is named or has a dual-obligee rider.

**Insurance.** Builder's risk is typically an owner-purchased property policy covering the work in place, with off-site and transit coverage added by negotiation. The A201-2017 family moved most detailed insurance requirements into an exhibit to the owner-contractor agreement, so check both the general conditions and that exhibit. A wrap-up program consolidates general liability and workers compensation for the whole project under one policy: an OCIP when the owner sponsors it, a CCIP when the general contractor does. Builder's risk, pollution, and professional coverage are usually bought alongside a wrap rather than inside it. Wrap economics only work at scale and are project-specific.

## Red Flags

- Percentage complete in the pay application exceeds the third-party inspector's observed percentage, and no one has reconciled the two.
- Schedule of values is front-loaded, or general conditions and contractor fee are carried in one line with no detail behind it.
- Unconditional lien waivers are being collected before the corresponding payment has cleared.
- A waiver does not substantially follow the statutory form in a state that prescribes one, or an advance waiver is embedded in the subcontract.
- Retainage is being held above the state cap, or reduced at a milestone on bonded work without prior written surety consent.
- Construction Change Directives have been open and unpriced for multiple draw cycles while the work proceeds.
- Contingency is being drawn through change orders that also increase the GMP, so the cap is no longer a cap.
- Off-site stored materials are funded with no advance owner approval, no evidence of title transfer free of liens, and no insurance.
- Title date-down is missing for a draw, or advances were disbursed out of sequence with the endorsements.
- Substantial Completion has been certified so that the owner can occupy while a long punch list and unresolved base-building items remain, and the one-year correction clock has started running.
- Liquidated damages are stated with no apportionment language, on a project where owner-caused delay is already documented.
- Contractor has an identity of interest with the ownership entity, and pricing, buyout, and retainage release have not been tested at arm's length.
- Final payment is being processed before final lien releases, consent of surety, and the certification of paid debts and claims required by A201 9.10.
