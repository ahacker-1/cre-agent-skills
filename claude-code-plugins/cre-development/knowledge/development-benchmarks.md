# Development Benchmarks

Last updated: 2026-09-01

Scope: U.S. ground-up development and heavy redevelopment across multifamily, industrial, retail, office, and mixed-use. This is an educational decision-support reference, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Stabilized operating assumptions belong to the multifamily, industrial, office, and retail packs. Every cost level, cap rate, and absorption figure below is directional as of its stated date and must be re-validated against current local evidence, live subcontractor pricing, and lender feedback.

---

## Current Context

- Construction cost escalation is positive but no longer spiking, and the indices disagree on magnitude. Turner reported its Building Cost Index at 1552 in Q2 2026, up 1.44% for the quarter and 5.15% year over year. Rider Levett Bucknall reported a national index of 288.58 in Q2 2026 with escalation settling near 1% per quarter. Mortenson reported +1.69% quarterly and +6.77% year over year for Q1 2026.
- Escalation is highly local. RLB's Q2 2026 annual city growth ran from Chicago at 1.42% to Honolulu at 5.93%, and Mortenson's Q1 2026 markets ran from Portland at +2.55% to Denver at +10.37% year over year.
- Metals carry an explicit federal tariff overlay. Proclamation 11032 (signed June 1, 2026, published in the Federal Register June 4, 2026) recites the regime from Proclamation 11021 of April 2, 2026: 50% ad valorem on products made of aluminum, steel, or copper, 25% on derivative products predominantly composed of those metals, and a temporarily reduced 15% on fixed industrial machinery and power equipment.
- Aggregate construction activity is contracting. The Census Bureau reported total construction put in place of $2,157.6 billion SAAR in July 2026, down 0.5% from June and 3.8% year over year, with year-to-date spending down 3.5%.
- Supply is thinning in the two most-developed sectors. CBRE reported Q2 2026 multifamily completions of 77,700 units, down 14% year over year against 167,000 units of net absorption, and industrial completions of 47.9 million sq ft, the lowest quarterly total since 2016, against 85.1 million sq ft of net absorption.

## Development Budget Taxonomy

Build every budget from these groups. GSA's federal cost policy and the capitalized-cost definitions used by AvalonBay and Prologis in their 10-K filings converge on the same stack.

| Group | What Belongs In It | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Land and acquisition | Purchase price, options and extensions, assemblage, closing costs, title, survey, broker fee | Also the residual land value test target |
| Hard costs | Direct construction, general conditions, contractor overhead and profit, escalation to the construction midpoint, sales and use taxes on construction inputs | Escalate to the midpoint of construction, not to the start date |
| Soft costs | Architecture and engineering, permits and impact fees, entitlement and legal, environmental and geotechnical, surveys, testing and inspection, construction management, insurance, taxes during construction, marketing and lease-up | Ratio to hard cost varies too widely to default; build it line by line |
| FF&E and equipment | Furnishings, appliances, amenity fit-out, first-generation tenant improvements and leasing commissions | AvalonBay capitalizes first-generation commercial TI and LC into project cost |
| Financing costs | Origination and lender fees, capitalized interest, legal, appraisal, inspection, rate cap or hedge cost | Sizing and reserve mechanics live in [Construction Lending Criteria](knowledge/construction-lending-criteria.md) |
| Contingency | Design contingency and construction contingency, shown as two separate lines | Never embed contingency inside unit prices or quantities |
| Developer fee | Fee to the sponsor for development services | No sourced market range; treat as deal-specific and disclose whether it is paid, deferred, or subordinated |
| Reserves | Interest reserve, operating deficit reserve, lease-up reserve, replacement reserve at stabilization | Lender-driven; confirm what is funded at close versus drawn |

For-sale residential runs on a different stack. NAHB's 2024 construction cost survey (published January 2025) put the average new single-family sale price at $665,298 split as finished lot 13.7%, construction 64.4%, financing 1.5%, overhead and general expenses 5.7%, marketing 0.8%, sales commission 2.8%, and profit 11.0%. Those shares do not transfer to income-property development.

## Contingency and Escalation

GSA's Public Buildings Service cost policy (PBS 1000.6B / P-120, November 7, 2022) publishes an explicit contingency schedule. It is federal owner policy, not a private-market rule, but it is one of the few published, non-anonymous anchors available. Use it as a reference point and adjust for delivery method, subcontractor market, and design completeness.

| Design Stage | Design Contingency, New Construction (% of direct cost) | Design Contingency, Renovation and Alteration |
|---|---|---|
| Planning and development | 10% | 10% |
| Concept design | 7.5% - 10% | 10% - 15% |
| Design development | 5% - 7.5% | 7.5% - 10% |
| 75% construction documents | 2% - 5% | 3.5% - 7.5% |
| 90% construction documents | 1% - 3% | 1.5% - 3.5% |
| Final construction documents | 0% | 0% |

| Construction Contingency (% of cost of construction at award) | GSA Guideline |
|---|---|
| New capital construction | 7% |
| Modernization | 10% |
| Repair and alteration, limited or complex scope | 10% |

Three rules that travel with these numbers, all from the same policy: allowances and contingencies must always be identified separately; contingency covers unforeseen conditions and design coordination, never scope additions, which require an approved budget revision; and escalation is a separate line estimated to the construction midpoint from a market study, not a rule of thumb.

## Yield on Cost, Development Spread, and Residual Land Value

| Metric | Formula | How to Use It |
|---|---|---|
| Yield on cost | Stabilized NOI / total development cost | Prologis publishes this definition directly: estimated NOI at stabilized occupancy divided by total expected investment |
| Untrended yield on cost | Stabilized NOI at today's rents and expenses / total development cost | The controlling test. If the deal only works trended, say so out loud |
| Trended yield on cost | Stabilized NOI at projected delivery-date rents and expenses / total development cost | A forecast, not a return. Show the rent growth and expense assumptions that create the gap |
| Development spread | (Yield on cost - market cap rate) x 10,000, stated in basis points | Use a live, submarket-specific, quality-matched cap rate, not a national average |
| Development margin | (Stabilized value - total development cost) / total development cost, net of closing costs and taxes where a sale or contribution is planned | Prologis reported an estimated weighted average margin of 25.4% on 2025 stabilizations, up from 19.2% in 2024 |
| Residual land value | (Stabilized NOI / target yield on cost) - all non-land development costs - required developer profit | Answers what land price still clears the target return. Distinct from the appraisal land residual technique of income capitalization |

Reference points, dated. Prologis reported a weighted average stabilized yield of 6.7% on 2025 stabilizations, up from 6.2% in 2024. CBRE's H1 2026 Cap Rate Survey (published August 12, 2026) found the all-property average cap rate essentially flat while the 10-year Treasury peaked at 4.67% in mid-May 2026; a trade summary of that survey reported going-in cap rates near 5.2% industrial, 5.3% multifamily, 6.4% office, and 6.4% retail. Practitioner spread targets published by Realty Capital Analytics (realcapanalytics.com, a modeling and consulting firm, not MSCI Real Capital Analytics) run roughly 150 to 200 bps for multifamily, 175 to 250 bps for industrial, 250 to 350+ bps for office, and 150 to 200 bps for grocery-anchored retail rising above 300 bps for speculative retail, tighter in gateway markets and wider in secondary growth markets. Those are conventions, not measured market data. There is no defensible universal spread threshold; the honest output is the computed spread, the cap rate evidence behind it, and what happens if the exit cap widens 50 to 100 bps.

## Schedule and Lease-Up Duration

Definitions drive these numbers more than markets do. AvalonBay and Prologis define stabilization as the earlier of 90% occupancy or the one-year anniversary of completion. Camden uses 90% occupancy with no backstop. Always state which definition a reported stabilization date uses before comparing projects.

| Interval | Directional Range | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Construction start to construction completion, institutional multifamily | Median 10 quarters, range 4 to 18 | Computed across AvalonBay's 24 under-construction communities, 10-K at December 31, 2025 |
| Construction start to stabilization, institutional multifamily | Median 12 quarters, range 8 to 20 | Same pipeline, using AvalonBay's stabilization definition |
| Construction completion to 90% occupancy, Sunbelt garden and mid-rise | 3 to 4 quarters actual; 4 to 7 quarters forecast on projects still under construction | Camden 10-K, 2025 actual stabilizations and current pipeline |
| Completion to stabilization under a one-year-backstop definition | Median 2 quarters, range 1 to 4 | AvalonBay pipeline; shorter partly because initial occupancy precedes completion and the definition caps the interval |
| Industrial and logistics | Stabilization defined as earlier of one year after completion or 90% occupied | Prologis 10-K definition; no per-project interval disclosed |

Retail, office, and mixed-use ground-up lease-up durations are not benchmarked here. No comparable public-filing disclosure was located; those stay case-by-case against submarket leasing evidence and signed pre-leases.

## Cost Escalation and Tariff Risk

- Do not adopt a single national escalation rate. The published 2026 indices spread from roughly 4% to 7% year over year because they measure different baskets, geographies, and building types. Carry a project-specific rate supported by a market study, current subcontractor pricing, and escalation to the construction midpoint.
- Price the tariff-exposed scopes separately: structural steel, metal panel and decking, aluminum glazing and storefront, copper wiring, switchgear, elevators, and rooftop mechanical equipment. Under the June 2026 Section 232 regime those carry duties of 50%, 25%, or 15% depending on classification.
- Labor is the binding constraint in the 2026 releases, not materials availability. Turner named skilled mechanical and electrical labor specifically, Cumming Group's Q2 2025 analysis reported skilled trade wages up 5% to 7% in high-demand regions, and data center, semiconductor, and advanced manufacturing demand competes directly for the same trades. Delay is therefore a cost line, not only a schedule line: it compounds through carry, escalation to a later midpoint, and lease-up into a different market.

## Red Flags

- Contingency embedded in unit prices or a single blended line instead of separate design and construction contingency lines, or drawn for owner-directed scope additions.
- Renovation or adaptive reuse budgeted at new-construction contingency levels.
- Escalation applied at the construction start date rather than to the midpoint, or omitted entirely on a build longer than 18 months.
- A project that clears the target return only on trended rents, with the trending assumption not disclosed.
- Development spread computed against a national average cap rate, or against an exit cap rate below the current going-in cap rate, with no widening stress case.
- Cost per square foot or per unit imported from a different market, product form, or parking type without adjustment.
- No line for entitlement, impact fees, exactions, or opposition delay in a jurisdiction where those are known to bite.
- Lease-up underwritten from national absorption data instead of submarket delivery concentration and competitive pre-leasing evidence.
- Stabilization dates compared across sponsors without checking whether each uses a 90% occupancy test or a one-year backstop.
- Tariff-exposed scopes carried at pre-2026 pricing, or a fixed-price contract assumed to absorb duty changes without checking its escalation and allowance language.
