# Site and Entitlement Screen

Screen a U.S. development site for zoning fit, entitlement path, fees and exactions, environmental and physical constraints, and political risk, then produce an entitlement risk rating, a critical path, and a go / no-go on pursuit.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill before committing predevelopment capital: when a site is offered, before an LOI or option is signed, before a deposit goes hard, when a broker's "zoned for" claim needs testing, or when an equity partner or construction lender asks what has to be true for this site to become a buildable project. This is educational decision support, not legal, environmental, engineering, tax, or investment advice. Land use is governed locally, so every threshold below must be confirmed against the current municipal code, the state enabling statute, local land use counsel, and licensed environmental, geotechnical, and civil professionals.

This is the pre-entitlement question. For a standing income-producing asset already under contract, the acquisition-diligence equivalents live in other packs and should be used instead: [Environmental Review](skills/due-diligence/environmental-review.md) for Phase I scope, recognized environmental conditions, and remediation cost on an existing building; [Legal & Title Review](skills/due-diligence/legal-title-review.md) for title and survey exceptions heading into a closing; and [Market Study](skills/due-diligence/market-study.md) for submarket demand and comparables. Those skills assume a use that already exists; this one asks whether the intended program can be built here at all.

---

## What You'll Need to Provide

- Site address, parcel numbers (APN), acreage, and a current title report or preliminary title commitment
- Intended program: use, unit count or square footage, stories, parking count, delivery target
- Current zoning district, applicable overlays, specific plan, and the general or comprehensive plan designation
- Any existing survey or ALTA, wetland delineation, Phase I ESA, geotechnical report, or traffic study, plus any recorded covenants, easements, deed restrictions, development or annexation agreements
- Contract or option terms: deposit schedule, when the deposit goes hard, contingency and extension structure; and any local information available such as the adopted fee schedule, hearing calendar, and recent approvals on comparable sites
- The business question: pursue and option, pursue with a longer contingency, renegotiate price, or pass

---

## Mission

Determine whether the intended program can be legally built on this site, what approvals stand between here and a building permit, how long and how uncertain that path is, what it costs in fees and exactions, what physical and environmental conditions could shrink the buildable envelope or blow up the budget, and whether the deal is worth pursuit capital.

---

## Strategy

### Step 1: Establish Site Facts and What Binds the Land

Do not start with zoning. Start with what is recorded.

- Confirm parcel identity, acreage, and legal description against the title report and survey; note any discrepancy between deeded and surveyed area
- Pull every recorded instrument: easements (access, utility, drainage, conservation), rights of way, covenants and restrictions, deed restrictions, reciprocal easement agreements, prior development or annexation agreements
- Confirm legal access to a public street adequate for the program's loading, fire, and turning requirements, and whether any legal nonconforming status of existing structures would be extinguished by demolition or change of use
- Note whether the site is a single parcel, a split-zoned parcel, or an assemblage requiring a lot line adjustment, subdivision, merger, or street vacation, and whether any parcel in the assemblage is not yet under control

A recorded restriction can defeat a use that zoning allows. Anything binding independent of the code is a counsel item.

### Step 2: Test the Program Against the Dimensional Envelope

Read the standards from the current code text, not from a summary table or a listing.

| Standard | What to confirm | Common trap |
|---|---|---|
| Permitted use | Use permitted, conditional, or prohibited in the base district and every overlay | Overlay or specific plan is more restrictive than the base zone |
| Density / FAR | Units per acre or floor area ratio, and exactly which areas count toward it | Definition of gross vs net site area, and what floor area is excluded |
| Height | Feet, stories, or both; measurement datum; rooftop appurtenance rules | Height measured from a different grade than assumed |
| Setbacks, coverage, open space | Front, side, rear and transitional setbacks, maximum coverage, required open space, landscaping, tree preservation | Stepbacks and daylight planes shrink upper floors; open space consumes the surface parking field |
| Parking and loading | Minimum and maximum stalls, bicycle, ADA, EV, loading berths, stacking | Reductions require a discretionary finding |
| Buildable envelope | Site area net of easements, floodway, wetlands, required dedications | Program fits the parcel but not the developable area |

State the result in one line: compliant, compliant with administrative relief, requires variance or conditional use, or requires a legislative change. Then compute the delta - how much density, height, or parking relief the program actually needs.

### Step 3: Classify the Entitlement Path and Build the Critical Path

Classify the approval as ministerial, administrative site plan, variance, conditional or special use permit, rezoning or map amendment, planned unit development, or a density bonus / incentive program, using the pathway matrix in [Entitlement and Site Risk](knowledge/entitlement-and-site-risk.md). For each required approval, capture: the decision maker, the findings they must make, the statutory or code decision clock, the public hearing and notice requirements, the appeal window, and any referendum period. New York Town Law 274-a is a concrete example of the form these clocks take - reasonable conditions directly related to the site plan, 10 days' mailed notice to the applicant, 5 days' published notice, and a decision within 62 days of the close of hearing or of application receipt, extendable only by mutual consent. Read the governing state's and jurisdiction's equivalent; do not assume this one.

Then ask the vesting question explicitly: what freezes, when, and for how long. California Government Code 65866 illustrates the mechanism - under a development agreement the rules governing permitted uses, density, and design are those in effect when the agreement is executed, while non-conflicting later rules may still apply. Vesting doctrine is state-specific; route it to counsel.

Sequence the critical path with dependencies, not as a wish list: environmental review before or concurrent with the discretionary hearing, utility will-serve before civil design sign-off, delineation before site plan, appeal period before the deposit goes hard. This library publishes no national months-to-entitlement default; build the duration from the local code, the published hearing calendar, and comparable recent approvals in the same jurisdiction before the same body.

### Step 4: Price the Fees, Exactions, and Affordability Requirements

- Pull the currently adopted impact fee schedule; confirm the basis (per unit, per square foot, per trip, per fixture unit) and the payment trigger, since payment at permit versus at certificate of occupancy changes carry cost materially
- Identify linkage fees, park or school dedication and fee-in-lieu, utility connection and capacity reservation fees, plan check and inspection fees, and any inclusionary or affordability set-aside (percentage, income depth, term, on-site versus fee-in-lieu, and whether a density bonus or concessions offset it)
- Identify offsite obligations conditioned on approval: road widening, turn lanes, signals, sewer upsizing, lift stations, dedications - and underwrite them as hard cost with their own schedule and bonding, never as contingency
- Flag any exaction lacking a visible connection to the project's impact as a counsel question. Under Sheetz v. County of El Dorado (2024-04-12, unanimous), the Takings Clause does not distinguish legislative from administrative permit conditions, so Nollan nexus and Dolan rough proportionality reach legislatively adopted monetary exactions; the Court expressly left open how individualized a class-wide condition must be. Budget the adopted schedule at face value regardless
- Scale check, directional and dated: NAHB reported on 2026-06-09 that regulation adds $131,734 to an average new single-family home, 26.4% of a $499,500 average price, with $46,795 (9.4%) at the land development stage. Single-family and not transferable to income property, but it is why this line cannot be a placeholder

### Step 5: Screen Environmental and Physical Constraints

Run each trigger and record the answer, the source document, and its date.

- **Phase I ESA.** Confirm it complies with 40 CFR Part 312 All Appropriate Inquiries using ASTM E1527-21 (E2247-23 for forestland or rural). Full AAI must be within 1 year before acquisition, and interviews, government records review, visual inspection, and the lien search within 180 days. Outside those windows the report is stale. Confirm the buyer has reliance. Any recognized environmental condition drives a Phase II scope and a remediation allowance
- **Wetlands and waters.** Confirm a current delineation and whether it is Corps-verified. Section 404 requires avoidance first, then minimization, then compensation. A general (nationwide) permit covers minimal-effect activity; an individual permit brings public notice and public interest review and a materially longer schedule. Test how close the design sits to the applicable nationwide permit limits
- **Flood and stormwater.** Pull the FIRM from FEMA's Flood Map Service Center by address or coordinates. Under 44 CFR 60.3 the community must permit all development in the SFHA, new residential lowest floor including basement must be at or above BFE, and non-residential must elevate or be certified watertight; new development in the regulatory floodway is prohibited absent a demonstrated no-rise. Confirm local freeboard, which commonly exceeds the federal minimum. A construction stormwater permit is required at 1 acre or more disturbed, or below 1 acre within a larger common plan of development or sale reaching 1 acre, and in most states the operative instrument is the state general permit rather than EPA's
- **Listed species and federal nexus.** If a lawful activity could take a federally listed species, a Habitat Conservation Plan supporting an ESA Section 10(a)(1)(B) incidental take permit is required, and roughly two thirds of listed species have some habitat on private land. Separately, federal funding, a loan guarantee, a license, or a Section 404 individual permit can pull in NEPA and Section 106 historic review under 36 CFR Part 800 - screen for it before building the schedule on local approvals alone
- **Geotechnical.** Measure any existing report against the IBC Section 1803 scope. Expansive soil is defined by plasticity index 15 or greater (ASTM D4318), more than 10% passing a No. 200 sieve, more than 10% finer than 5 micrometers, and expansion index above 20 (ASTM D4829), or the expansion index criterion alone. On redevelopment sites budget exploratory work for undocumented fill, prior foundations, abandoned utilities, and buried tanks
- **State environmental review.** Where a CEQA, SEQRA, SEPA, or MEPA-type regime applies, classify the action and add its own clock. NYSDEC's SEQR process, for example, contemplates 30 days to designate a lead agency, 20 days for the determination of significance, 60 days to a final scope, 45 days for draft EIS adequacy, a 30-day minimum comment period, and findings 30 days after the final EIS - statutory minimums for one state, not a schedule
- **Utilities and offsite.** Confirm current will-serve letters for water, sewer, power, gas, and fiber. A capacity statement is not a reservation: check expiration, reservation fees, and whether the commitment survives an entitlement extension. Confirm right of way for offsite pipes, cables, and access

### Step 6: Assess Community and Political Risk

- Identify the decision makers by name and their voting record on comparable projects, plus organized neighborhood groups, prior opposition campaigns, and the litigation history of this jurisdiction and this neighborhood
- Identify the election calendar, any pending moratorium, comprehensive plan update, code rewrite, or ballot measure that could change the rules mid-process, and who has standing to appeal or force a referendum
- Size the exposure by project profile, not by base rate. Litigation is rare per project but concentrates on large, visible, discretionary ones - Holland and Knight's Hernandez research reported 47,999 housing units targeted by CEQA lawsuits filed in 2020, roughly half of that year's California production. A big infill project belongs to the litigated population

### Step 7: Rate, Compare, and Decide on Pursuit

Rate three axes separately and let the worst one set the entitlement risk rating. Never average them. Use the LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / CRITICAL scale in [Risk Scoring](knowledge/risk-scoring.md) so the rating carries directly into the risk table in [Development IC Memo Writer](skills/development/development-ic-memo-writer.md); reserve CRITICAL for an axis that is a dealbreaker on the current record, such as a use the code prohibits outright or a recorded restriction that defeats the program.

| Axis | LOW | MEDIUM | HIGH |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dimensional fit | Program is by-right under current standards | Needs administrative relief or minor variance | Needs density, height, or use relief a body can deny |
| Procedural path | Ministerial or staff-level with a statutory clock | Quasi-judicial with defined findings and one hearing body | Legislative change, PUD negotiation, or full state environmental review |
| Political exposure | No organized opposition, supportive record | Mixed record, some opposition, appealable | Organized opposition, hostile record, litigation history, election risk |

Pull 3 to 5 comparable entitlement outcomes in the same jurisdiction: application date, approval date, vote, conditions imposed, appeals filed, and how the approved program differed from the application. Comparable outcomes, not comparable sales, are the evidence for the schedule.

Close with a pursuit decision and the structure it requires: predevelopment capital at risk, the contingency period and extension rights the contract must carry, when the deposit may go hard relative to the appeal or referendum window, and the fallback program if the primary entitlement fails. Hand the fee, exaction, offsite, and predevelopment cost lines to [Development Budget and Yield on Cost Analyst](skills/development/development-budget-and-yield-on-cost-analyst.md), the entitlement critical path to [Schedule and Delivery Risk Tracker](skills/development/schedule-and-delivery-risk-tracker.md), the entitlement status and conditions of approval to [Construction Loan Sizing and Structure](skills/development/construction-loan-sizing-and-structure.md), and the rating, comparables, and open items to [Development IC Memo Writer](skills/development/development-ic-memo-writer.md).

## Quality Checks

- Dimensional standards are quoted from the current code text with section numbers, not from a listing or a broker summary, and buildable area is net of easements, floodway, delineated wetlands, and required dedications
- Every approval in the critical path has a named decision maker, required findings, a clock, and an appeal window, and the appeal and referendum periods sit inside the schedule rather than after it
- Fee amounts come from the currently adopted schedule with the payment trigger identified
- Every environmental item names the document relied on and its date, and the Phase I is tested against both the 1-year and 180-day AAI windows
- Comparable entitlement outcomes are from the same jurisdiction and the same decision body
- The rating is set by the worst axis with the reason stated, and anything requiring legal, environmental, or engineering judgment is flagged as a referral rather than answered

---

## Red Flags & Dealbreakers

- Deposit goes hard before the discretionary approval and its appeal or referendum period runs, or the pro forma treats a rezoning, PUD, or variance as if it were by-right with no probability weighting and no fallback program
- Recorded covenant, easement, or prior development agreement prohibits or constrains the intended use independent of zoning
- Any part of the buildable area sits in the regulatory floodway, where the no-rise standard applies
- Phase I is outside the 1-year or 180-day AAI windows at closing, has no reliance letter for the buyer, or identifies a recognized environmental condition with no Phase II and no remediation allowance budgeted
- Wetland delineation is stale or unverified, or the design sits close enough to the nationwide permit limits that a minor change forces an individual permit
- No geotechnical investigation on a redevelopment site with prior structures, or a report that omits expansive soil, liquefaction, or fill findings
- Will-serve letters are missing, expired, or describe general adequacy rather than committing capacity to this project, or offsite improvements sit in contingency instead of the hard cost budget with their own schedule and bonding
- Federal nexus discovered after the schedule was built on local approvals alone, or fee exposure underwritten from a prior year's schedule or assumed payable at certificate of occupancy without confirming the code
- California density bonus, concession, waiver, or streamlining assumptions imported into a state with no comparable statute, or legal nonconforming status assumed to survive demolition or a change of use
- Entitlement duration taken from a national rule of thumb rather than the local calendar and comparable approvals

## When Data is Missing

- If the title report is missing, treat the screen as preliminary; recorded restrictions can defeat a compliant program
- If the code text is unavailable, do not infer standards from a zoning map or a listing; state the standard as unconfirmed
- If no Phase I exists, do not assume the site is clean; carry an assessment cost and hold the deposit soft. If no delineation exists, assume potential waters until one says otherwise and treat an individual permit as a live schedule case
- If no geotechnical work exists, carry an exploratory allowance and flag foundation cost as unpriced; if will-serve letters are absent, treat capacity as unconfirmed and utility upsizing as an open cost
- If no comparable approvals can be found, say so and widen the schedule range rather than publishing a single date, and if fee schedules cannot be obtained, show the fee line as unpriced rather than zero

## Confidence Scoring

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| HIGH | Current code text, title report, survey, Phase I within AAI windows, delineation, geotech, will-serve letters, adopted fee schedule, and 3 or more local comparable approvals are all in hand and reconciled |
| MEDIUM | Zoning and program tested against current code, but one or more of Phase I, delineation, geotech, will-serve, or fee schedule is missing or stale, and comparables are thin |
| LOW | Zoning or program taken from a summary rather than the code, no environmental or geotechnical work, no title review, or no local comparable outcomes |
