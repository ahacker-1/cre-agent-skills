# Development IC Memo Writer

Synthesize site and entitlement, budget and yield on cost, capital stack, contract and schedule, and lease-up work into one U.S. ground-up or heavy-redevelopment investment committee memo with a decision table, scored risks and mitigants, a go / no-go recommendation, dated conditions, and open items. This is educational decision support, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice; every cost level, cap rate, spread convention, and market figure referenced is directional as of its stated date.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when a development deal has to go in front of an investment committee, a credit committee, an equity partner, or a joint venture board, and the underlying analysis already exists in pieces. It also works as a gap audit on a draft package: run it and it names what the committee will ask for and cannot find. It covers ground-up and heavy redevelopment across multifamily, industrial, retail, office, and mixed-use, and does not re-underwrite stabilized operations, which come from the sector packs. For the acquisition of a standing asset, use [IC Memo Writer](skills/underwriting/ic-memo-writer.md) or the sector equivalents ([Office IC Memo Writer](skills/office/office-ic-memo-writer.md), [Retail IC Memo Writer](skills/retail/retail-ic-memo-writer.md), [Industrial IC Memo Writer](skills/industrial/industrial-ic-memo-writer.md)); this memo carries the entitlement, cost-to-complete, contract, and delivery risk those do not.

---

## What You'll Need to Provide

- Project identity: address, product type, unit or bay count, gross and net rentable area, parking, phasing
- Site and entitlement status: zoning, approval pathway, approvals obtained and outstanding, appeal windows, conditions of approval, environmental and geotechnical reports, will-serve letters
- Land control: deed, option, conditional contract, ground lease, or contribution, with deposit-hard and outside dates
- Development budget, sources and uses, design stage, and the return set: untrended and trended yield on cost, spread, margin, project and equity IRR, equity multiple
- Debt: term sheet or commitment, sizing tests, guaranties, reserves, extension and take-out terms
- Contract and schedule: delivery method, contract form, GMP or contract sum, contingency, allowances, liquidated damages, milestone dates, float, long-lead items
- Lease-up plan: absorption curve, concessions, competitive deliveries, pre-leases or build-to-suit commitments
- Sponsor and partner facts: comparable deliveries, guarantor liquidity and net worth, JV governance and promote
- Third-party reports: appraisal, market study, cost review, Phase I, geotechnical, survey, title
- Business question: approve, approve with conditions, defer for diligence, or decline

---

## Mission

Produce a memo a committee can vote on: one recommendation, the evidence behind it, the risks scored and paired with funded mitigants, the conditions that must be satisfied before capital moves, and an honest list of what is still unknown.

## Strategy

### Step 1: Assemble the Inputs and Name Every Gap

A memo is a synthesis, so its weakest input governs its confidence. Pull site, entitlement, and environmental findings from [Site and Entitlement Screen](skills/development/site-and-entitlement-screen.md), budget and returns from [Development Budget and Yield on Cost Analyst](skills/development/development-budget-and-yield-on-cost-analyst.md), debt terms from [Construction Loan Sizing and Structure](skills/development/construction-loan-sizing-and-structure.md), contract terms from [GC Contract and Change Order Reviewer](skills/development/gc-contract-and-change-order-reviewer.md), milestones and float from [Schedule and Delivery Risk Tracker](skills/development/schedule-and-delivery-risk-tracker.md), and the absorption curve from [Lease-Up and Stabilization Pro Forma](skills/development/lease-up-and-stabilization-pro-forma.md). For a project already under construction, add cost to complete and in-balance status from [Construction Draw and Cost-to-Complete Reviewer](skills/development/construction-draw-and-cost-to-complete-reviewer.md). Then check the package against the documentation set regulators expect in a construction credit file: appraisal plus the documented review of it, feasibility study, business plan, construction budget separating land development, hard, and soft costs, plans and specifications, executed construction contracts, soil reports, survey, title, permits, insurance, take-out commitments, and financial statements for borrower, builder, prime tenant, take-out lender, and guarantors (FDIC, *Examination Modules: Construction and Land Development*, 10/25; OCC, *Comptroller's Handbook: Commercial Real Estate Lending*, Version 2.0). Where no one with the expertise to review plans, budget, and third-party reports has done so, say that. List every missing document by name; silence reads as satisfaction.

### Step 2: Fix Definitions So the Numbers Are Comparable

State these once, before any figure appears:

- **Stabilization definition.** Say which test governs: 90% occupancy, or the earlier of 90% occupancy and the one-year anniversary of completion. AvalonBay and Prologis use the second, Camden the first. Never compare a stabilization date or yield across sponsors without checking this.
- **Three valuation bases, never blended.** As-is, prospective market value as-completed, and prospective market value as-stabilized, each with its effective date and its stated point of reference to market conditions and time frame (Interagency Appraisal and Evaluation Guidelines, December 2010, citing USPAP Statement 4 and Advisory Opinion 17). For a proposed or partially leased rental development the appraisal must apply deductions and discounts for leasing commissions, rent loss, tenant improvements, and entrepreneurial profit, and must consider absorption of unleased space. The sum of retail sales for a proposed development is not market value.
- **Return definitions.** Yield on cost is stabilized NOI divided by total development cost; value creation is stabilized value above total cost; margin is value creation less closing costs and taxes on anything to be sold or contributed, divided by total cost (Prologis FY2025 Form 10-K). Also fix the cost scope, the unit denominators, and whether returns are levered and at what level.

### Step 3: Write the Thesis and Test It Against the Feasibility Rule

The thesis is three to five sentences: what is being built, for whom, why here, why now, and what the exit is. Then apply the governing test before anything else: construction costs that closely approach or exceed the expected value supported by the project's income generally indicate the project is not feasible (OCC handbook). If as-stabilized value is not comfortably above total development cost, say so in the recommendation line. Frame market context with dated evidence, not sentiment alone. Directional as of this writing: ULI and PwC's *Emerging Trends in Real Estate 2026* found investment prospect ratings rose for 16 of 27 subsectors while development prospect ratings fell for 18, with traditional office development rated lowest; Prologis reported 60.9% of 2025 development starts by total expected investment were build-to-suit against 28.6% in 2024, a statement about how much demand risk institutional developers carried into a start.

### Step 4: Build the Section Set the Committee Expects

Cover these in order. The sequence follows the narrative structure HUD publishes for new construction applications, one of the few non-anonymous published models of a development decision narrative (HUD MAP Guide, Handbook 4430.G, March 2021, Appendix 4):

1. **Sponsor, general contractor, and partners**: comparable completed deliveries, guarantor liquidity and net worth, and JV governance, including who controls budget increases, scope changes, and capital calls.
2. **Site and entitlement**: approval pathway, approvals in hand versus outstanding, conditions of approval priced into hard cost, appeal and referendum windows, offsite obligations, will-serve status, environmental and geotechnical findings. Use [Entitlement and Site Risk](knowledge/entitlement-and-site-risk.md); never treat a rezoning or PUD as by-right.
3. **Budget and yield on cost**: untrended yield first, trended second with the growth assumption disclosed, spread against a dated submarket cap rate comparable, margin, and residual land value against the contracted land price.
4. **Capital stack and construction loan**: which sizing test binds, contributed capital and how much is new cash, interest and carry reserves, guaranty burn-off tests, extension conditions, and take-out posture. Test take-out validity, including the take-out lender's capacity, marketability without a take-out, and whether the agreement extends automatically for delays beyond the builder's control (FDIC module). Use [Construction Lending Criteria](knowledge/construction-lending-criteria.md).
5. **Contract and schedule risk**: delivery method and contract form, when the GMP was set relative to design completeness, savings sharing, open allowances, liquidated damages, long-lead items, float, and delivery quarter. AIA distinguishes A101 (stipulated sum), A102 (cost plus a fee with a GMP), A103 (cost plus a fee, no GMP), A133 (construction manager as constructor with a GMP), and A134 (construction manager as constructor, no GMP); say which governs.
6. **Lease-up and takeout**: absorption curve, concessions, competitive deliveries in the same submarket and quarter, pre-leases or build-to-suit commitments, and the refinance or sale case.
7. **Returns**, base and stressed, at both the project and the equity level.

### Step 5: Score the Risks and Pair Each With a Funded Mitigant

Use the LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / CRITICAL scale and the escalation and dealbreaker logic in [Risk Scoring](knowledge/risk-scoring.md). Score at minimum entitlement and regulatory, environmental and site condition, cost and contract, schedule and delivery, market and lease-up, financing and capital, sponsor and counterparty, and partnership and governance. Anchor the categories in what actually breaks development projects; AvalonBay's FY2025 risk disclosure names them directly: abandoned pursuits and unrecovered pursuit costs, occupancy and rents missing original expectations, delay or failure to obtain permits and authorizations, costs exceeding estimates from material, labor, supply chain, or tariff change, inability to complete on schedule or at projected cost, third-party liabilities during development, subcontractor or key supplier failure to perform, and accessibility compliance liability. Every risk scored HIGH or CRITICAL needs a mitigant with an owner, a cost, and a date, or it is reported as unmitigated. A mitigant that is a hope is not a mitigant.

### Step 6: Run the Sensitivity Set

Run the axes the regulator names plus cost, each independently and then combined. Analysis of cash inflows and outflows across the development period should include stress testing of sensitivity to changing economic conditions under a variety of scenarios, naming absorption rates, interest rates, and capitalization rates (OCC handbook). Never model an exit cap below the current going-in cap without writing out the argument, and never net a cost overrun against an assumed increase in value.

| Axis | Minimum cases | What to report |
|---|---|---|
| Hard cost | +5%, +10%, +15% | Yield on cost, spread, margin, equity need, break-even overrun |
| Rent or absorption | Rent -5% and -10%; lease-up two quarters longer | Yield, DSCR at stabilization, reserve adequacy |
| Interest rate | Term sheet rate plus a stated stress | Interest reserve sufficiency, carry, in-balance status |
| Exit cap rate | Current going-in, +50 bps, +100 bps | Value, margin, refinance proceeds, equity multiple |
| Schedule | Two-quarter and four-quarter delay | Carry, escalation to a later midpoint, delivery into a different leasing market |

### Step 7: Write the Recommendation, Conditions, and Open Items

- One recommendation from the [Risk Scoring](knowledge/risk-scoring.md) vocabulary: PROCEED, PROCEED_WITH_MITIGATIONS, PROCEED_WITH_CAUTION, FURTHER_DILIGENCE, or REJECT, followed by the two or three findings that drive it.
- Split conditions into approval, pre-start or first draw, and construction reporting. Each carries an owner, a date, and a test that can be marked satisfied or not.
- Address by name the adequacy of construction contingency, the operating deficit or lease-up reserve, the replacement reserve, the equity funding schedule, the drawings and specifications, and any environmental condition; where the memo departs from a third-party consultant's conclusion, put the justification in the memo (HUD MAP Guide, Section 11.3.3).
- Convert the standard warning indicators into reporting conditions: draw requests ahead of verified work, budget reallocation between line items, rising change-order counts, slower absorption than underwritten, liberal concessions, liens from payment disputes, and requests for additional financing (OCC handbook).
- Close with open items: what is unknown, who owns getting it, and by when.

## Quality Checks

- One recommendation appears, in the approved vocabulary, with its top drivers in the same paragraph, and the stabilization definition is named before any date or yield is compared
- As-is, as-completed, and as-stabilized values appear separately with effective dates and sources, and untrended yield on cost appears before trended with the trending assumption written out
- The development spread is computed against a dated, submarket, quality-matched cap rate comparable, and every number in the decision table traces to a named input document or sibling analysis
- Every HIGH or CRITICAL risk has a mitigant with an owner, a cost, and a date, or is labeled unmitigated
- Conditions are split into approval, pre-start, and reporting, each with an owner, a date, and a test
- Contingency and reserve adequacy, the equity funding schedule, drawings and specifications, and environmental conditions are each addressed by name
- Any departure from a third-party consultant's conclusion is justified in the memo, and missing documents are listed by name

## Red Flags & Dealbreakers

- As-stabilized value does not comfortably exceed total development cost, and the memo leads with returns instead of saying so
- Land is under contract with the deposit going hard before a discretionary approval and its appeal period have run
- A rezoning, PUD, variance, or conditional use permit is underwritten as by-right, with no alternative program and no abandonment cost shown
- The deal clears only on trended rents, or only on an exit cap below the current going-in cap
- Contributed land at appreciated value carries the equity test while little new cash is at risk, or the interest reserve was sized to a schedule the project has already missed
- Open allowances, an unset GMP, or a design stage well behind the pricing the budget claims, or delivery landing in the same quarter as competing supply the absorption curve ignores
- Take-out treated as certain when it is standby, conditioned on rents not yet achieved, or dependent on a lender whose capacity was never tested
- Guarantor liquidity and net worth asserted without statements, one guarantor supporting several projects with no aggregate exposure shown, or JV governance that leaves budget increases, scope changes, and capital calls undefined
- Conditions with no owner, no date, or no test, which is a recommendation dressed as a decision

## When Data is Missing

- If the appraisal provides only one value basis, say which one and label the return set incomplete rather than deriving the others; if no market study exists, do not build an absorption curve from national data, state that absorption is unsupported, and run the return at a materially slower lease-up
- If the cost estimate is below design development, label the output a screening memo, carry contingency at the top of the design-stage range, and claim no precision the estimate cannot support
- If debt is indicative rather than committed, size it under the quoted terms and under a stressed alternative, and list the term sheet as an open item
- If guarantor financials are missing, do not credit the guaranty in the risk scoring; if the contract is unexecuted or the GMP unset, treat contract certainty as HIGH risk regardless of the budget number, and if the entitlement outcome is genuinely uncertain, show approved-case and denied-case programs side by side with the sunk pursuit cost rather than assigning an invented probability
- Never substitute a percentage rule of thumb for a missing line; show it unpriced and quantify what it would take to break the deal

## Confidence Scoring

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| HIGH | Entitlements in hand with appeal periods run, an executed GMP or hard bid at design development or later, a committed debt term sheet, an appraisal with all three value bases, dated submarket study and cap rate comparables, and guarantor financials in file |
| MEDIUM | Core approvals obtained or clearly probable, a cost consultant estimate short of a GMP, indicative debt terms, an appraisal missing one value basis, or a market study older than six months |
| LOW | Entitlement outcome unresolved, sponsor-supplied budget with percentage-based soft costs, no committed debt, no third-party appraisal or market study, or missing guarantor financials; treat all returns as directional and the recommendation as provisional |
