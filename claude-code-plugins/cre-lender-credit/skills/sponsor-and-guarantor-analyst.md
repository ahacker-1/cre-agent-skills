# Sponsor and Guarantor Analyst

Analyze the sponsor and guarantor behind a U.S. CRE loan: personal and entity financials, verified liquidity and net worth, contingent liabilities and REO, global cash flow and global DSCR, track record, ownership and control, background flags, and the guaranty structure the credit actually needs.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill on the credit side when a CRE loan request, renewal, annual review, modification, or workout needs a defensible view of who stands behind the deal and how much credit the guaranty deserves. It supports the underwriter sizing the request, the credit officer approving it, loan review testing it, and special assets deciding whether guarantor support can preclude or reduce an adverse classification. This is educational decision support, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Guaranty enforceability, spousal and community property questions, entity authority, and fraudulent transfer exposure are counsel questions under state law. Classification, nonaccrual, and allowance conclusions belong to the institution and its regulator.

---

## What You'll Need to Provide

- Loan request: amount, property, structure, term, and the proposed guaranty
- Organizational chart and operating or partnership agreements for the borrower and each upper-tier entity
- Signed and dated personal financial statement for each proposed guarantor and material sponsor
- Two to three years of personal and entity federal tax returns with all schedules and K-1s
- Schedule of real estate owned: ownership percentage, value, debt, maturity, lender, NOI, distributions
- Schedule of contingent liabilities: every guaranty extended to every lender, with amount, form, and status
- Verification support: bank and brokerage statements, custodial confirmations, credit report, lender references
- Sponsor resume or track record schedule, and the institution's guarantor policy tests

---

## Mission

Establish whether the sponsor and guarantor can and will support this loan, quantify that support in verified dollars and global coverage, and recommend a guaranty structure and sponsor risk grade the credit file can defend to loan review and an examiner.

---

## Strategy

### Step 1: Map the Credit Parties, Ownership, and Control

Build the chart before the math: the borrower entity and whether it is a single-purpose entity; every upper-tier owner down to natural persons with percentages; the managing member, general partner, or manager and who holds decision and transfer rights; proposed guarantors; and material sponsors who will not sign but whose support the credit assumes.

Apply two separate thresholds and label each by its purpose. Beneficial ownership identification for the customer file reaches each individual owning 25% or more of the equity, up to four individuals, plus one individual with significant responsibility to control, manage, or direct the entity; where a trust owns 25% or more, the trustee is the beneficial owner (31 CFR 1010.230). Guaranty scope is a separate credit decision, not a fixed percentage: for reference, SBA's 7(a) and 504 programs require an unlimited full guaranty from any individual with 20% or more direct or indirect ownership and permit supplemental guarantors regardless of ownership (SBA SOP 50 10 8.1, effective 2026-10-01). Flag any layer that stops short of a natural person, and any entity guarantor whose signing authority is not established in the operating documents.

### Step 2: Normalize the Personal Financial Statement and Entity Financials

Treat a signed personal financial statement as an assertion, not evidence. Rebuild it:

- credit cash, securities, and unrestricted accounts as liquidity only where a statement, custodial confirmation, or bank verification supports the balance, and discount or exclude closely held interests, non-marketable partnership interests, and property carried at owner estimates with no valuation support
- haircut restricted stock, retirement accounts, and life insurance cash value for penalty, lockup, and policy loans, and state the haircut
- restate net worth exclusive of any interest in the subject collateral and of assets already pledged elsewhere
- reconcile the statement to the tax returns, K-1s, and the REO schedule; a K-1 entity missing from the REO schedule is an open item, and for entity guarantors note the statement basis: audited, reviewed, compiled, or internal

Guarantor liquidity should be verified by the bank rather than accepted as presented (OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Commercial Real Estate Lending, v2.0).

### Step 3: Test Liquidity, Net Worth, and Contingent Liabilities

Run three tests and state the institution's policy level for each, since no federal source publishes one.

| Test | How to compute it | What to state |
|---|---|---|
| Net worth | Verified assets less liabilities, excluding subject collateral and pledged assets | Result, policy minimum, pass or fail, measurement date |
| Liquidity | Verified unrestricted cash and marketable securities after haircuts | Result as a percentage of loan amount and as months of debt service |
| Contingent liabilities | Every guaranty to every lender, by amount and form | Total exposure, nearest maturity, coverage against verified liquidity |

Rules that survive an examiner: count the total number and amount of guarantees currently extended to all lenders, not only your institution's, and run the same analysis for material sponsors (2023 Interagency Policy Statement on Prudent CRE Loan Accommodations and Workouts). Also:

- a guarantor's unpledged assets are not a substitute for project equity (OCC handbook)
- consolidate overlapping assets across guarantors before summing, test assets that collateralize the guaranty separately from general liquidity, and treat a schedule covering only your lender as a documentation exception to age to clearance

### Step 4: Build Global Cash Flow and Global DSCR

Global debt service coverage is the combined cash flows of borrower and guarantor over their combined financial obligations, including contingent obligations (2023 Interagency Policy Statement, footnote 17). Property-level DSCR, debt yield, and NOI construction are not restated here; see [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md).

Integrate business financial statements, tax returns, and K-1s across every partnership, LLC, and corporation, then:

- separate recurring cash flow from capital gains, refinance proceeds, and one-time distributions, then deduct personal debt service, property and income taxes, realistic living expenses, and committed capital calls
- add contingent obligations at a stated stress percentage, and show REO loan by loan with maturity so a wall of near-term maturities is visible rather than buried in an aggregate

Perform the global analysis even where liquid assets look ample, because those assets may be needed to fund other actual or contingent liabilities and other cash flow shortfalls (OCC handbook). A large balance sheet is not a coverage answer.

### Step 5: Test Willingness, Track Record, and Background Flags

A financially responsible guarantor possesses financial ability, demonstrated willingness, and the incentive to support the loan through ongoing payments, curtailments, or re-margining, and the guaranty must be written, legally enforceable, and adequate over the remaining term (2023 Interagency Policy Statement; OCC handbook). Test all three legs.

- Willingness and incentive: prior performance under guarantees, and whether it was voluntary or compelled by enforcement action, since compelled performance is not willingness; remaining equity and significant investment in the project. At origination, willingness may be presumed where economic incentive exists absent contrary evidence; after origination, use the evidence.
- Track record and background: comparable deals by property type, business plan, market, and vintage, since a merchant builder's record does not underwrite a lease-up hold and out-of-market experience is not local experience; credit report for unpaid bills, late vendor payments, suits and judgments; litigation and lien searches; personal and entity bankruptcy history; prior deeds-in-lieu, short sales, and workouts; real estate tax delinquency, which is nearly always an indicator of a distressed property, borrower, or guarantor (OCC handbook).
- Screening: OFAC screening on every credit party. The 50 Percent Rule blocks an entity owned 50% or more in the aggregate by one or more blocked persons; control alone does not automatically block, and significant blocked ownership below 50% is a caution flag (OFAC FAQs 398 and 399). Escalate that, adverse media, and politically exposed persons rather than resolving them inside the credit analysis.

### Step 6: Recommend the Guaranty Structure

Match the form to the gap between what the property covers and what the loan needs. Where the borrower is a credit union member, a decision not to require a full and unconditional personal guarantee from controlling principals must be documented in the file with the mitigating factors that offset the risk (12 CFR 723.5). A completion guaranty is required on every construction credit regardless of the forms below, and the full recourse taxonomy is in [Lender Credit Policy Benchmarks](knowledge/lender-credit-policy-benchmarks.md).

| Form | Recommend when |
|---|---|
| Full recourse | Unproven sponsor, construction, or coverage dependent on outside support |
| Partial principal | Cash flow credible but unproven; sizes the gap to stabilized coverage |
| Payment or interest-only, or carry | Lease-up and stabilization, bridging to a dated runway, where value is not the question |
| Burn-off or stepped-down | Only with a defined test, measurement date, and verification obligation |
| Loss-recourse carve-out plus springing full recourse | The base nonrecourse position, with named bad acts and springing triggers |

Then specify the mechanics, because these are where guaranties fail:

- payment guaranty rather than collection guaranty unless the credit deliberately accepts the judgment-first sequence, and continuing rather than note-specific where future advances are contemplated, executed at or before the initial loan so consideration is established, with the executed original in hand before any disbursement, signed in the loan officer's presence or notarized, and entity signing authority confirmed (FDIC RMS Manual of Examination Policies, Section 3.2)
- net worth and liquidity covenants defined to exclude the subject collateral, with a measurement date, a reporting covenant, and a verification method, and burn-off conditions written as a test the servicer can confirm before any release

### Step 7: Assign the Sponsor Risk Grade

This is a credit-analysis grade, not a supervisory classification; the regulatory grade is decided under [Regulatory Risk Rating and Classification](knowledge/regulatory-risk-rating-and-classification.md).

| Grade | Profile |
|---|---|
| 1 Strong | Verified liquidity and net worth clear policy with margin, global DSCR well above policy, deep relevant track record, clean background, guaranty enforceable and unlimited |
| 2 Satisfactory | Tests pass, contingent liabilities manageable, relevant experience, minor documentation items open |
| 3 Acceptable with structure | One test fails or is thin, and a specific guaranty form, covenant, or reserve closes the gap |
| 4 Weak | Global DSCR below policy, liquidity insufficient for the contingent book, thin or non-relevant track record, or adverse willingness evidence |
| 5 Unacceptable | Support cannot be verified, contingent liabilities exceed capacity, background flags unresolved, or the guaranty is unenforceable as drafted |

## Quality Checks

- Every asset credited as liquidity has a named verification source and date, and net worth is restated exclusive of the subject collateral and of assets pledged elsewhere
- Contingent liabilities cover all lenders and all entities, with overlapping guarantors consolidated before summing, and the personal financial statement reconciles to the tax returns, K-1s, and the REO schedule
- Global DSCR states the contingent obligation stress, separates recurring cash flow from capital gains, and willingness rests on evidence with prior performance labeled voluntary or compelled
- Track record counts only experience relevant to this property type, business plan, and market, and the guaranty recommendation names a form, a covenant, a measurement date, and a verification method
- Policy minimums are quoted as the institution's, not asserted as market standards, and any failed test is written as an exception with an approval level

---

## Red Flags & Dealbreakers

- Liquidity taken from an unverified personal financial statement, or unpledged guarantor assets counted as project equity
- Contingent liabilities disclosed only for your institution, an REO schedule with no maturities, or net worth concentrated in the subject property
- A guaranty executed after funding, held only as a copy, or signed by a person whose entity authority is not established, or a burn-off or release written with no defined test, measurement date, or verification obligation
- Prior guaranty performance compelled by suit, a pattern of deeds-in-lieu, short sales, and lender workouts, or a sponsor with no remaining equity, which removes the economic incentive the willingness presumption rests on
- Global DSCR that clears only because contingent obligations were excluded or capital gains treated as recurring, or ownership layers that stop before a natural person, or blocked-person ownership below 50% left unescalated
- Guarantor strength used to carry a property that cannot cover its own debt service; support that is permanent rather than bridging is itself the finding
- Real estate tax delinquency anywhere in the sponsor's portfolio

---

## When Data is Missing

- If the personal financial statement is unsigned or undated, treat it as unusable; SBA's 7(a) and 504 programs use a 90-day window for personal financial statements and 120 days for business statements, a defensible freshness reference point but not a bank CRE standard
- If tax returns or K-1s are missing, do not build a global cash flow; state that global DSCR cannot be computed and grade on that basis
- If the contingent liability schedule is partial, size the known exposure, say the tally is incomplete, and open a documentation exception; if asset values are owner estimates with no support, exclude them from liquidity and disclose the exclusion
- If the guaranty is not yet drafted, analyze against the term sheet and flag every unresolved mechanic in Step 6; if entity authority or spousal consent is unclear, route to counsel and do not credit the guaranty in the grade until it is resolved

---

## Confidence Scoring

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| HIGH | Signed current personal financial statements, tax returns with K-1s, verified liquidity, a complete all-lender contingent liability and REO schedule, and an executed or fully drafted guaranty |
| MEDIUM | Core financials available and reconciled, but one verification, one entity's returns, or one schedule element is outstanding |
| LOW | Unverified or stale financials, incomplete contingent liability disclosure, or a guaranty whose form and enforceability are undetermined |
