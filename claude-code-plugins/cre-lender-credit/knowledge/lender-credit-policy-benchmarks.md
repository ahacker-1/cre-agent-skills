# Lender Credit Policy Benchmarks

Last updated: 2026-09-01

Scope: U.S. commercial real estate credit policy as it is written and examined on the lender side - banks, credit unions, debt funds, life companies, agency multifamily lenders, and CMBS originators. This is an educational reference for credit analysts, underwriters, credit officers, portfolio managers, and loan review, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. The supervisory limits below are ceilings and screening criteria, not sizing targets; your institution's board-approved policy and exception process control. Confirm current pricing, spreads, and program terms with live sources. Formula definitions are not restated here; see [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md).

## Current Context

- The July 2026 Federal Reserve Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey reported banks easing standards on nonfarm nonresidential and multifamily loans in Q2 2026, construction and land development standards basically unchanged, and the level of CRE standards still at the tighter end of its historical range.
- CBRE reported (2026-08-03) Q2 2026 averages of 59.6% LTV on commercial loans, 63.3% on multifamily, 1.43 DSCR, 10.2% debt yield, and a 5.7% coupon, describing lenders as competing on price rather than leverage.
- The OCC's Spring 2026 Semiannual Risk Perspective (data as of 2025-12-31) flagged CRE refinance risk as the enduring issue, noted office net absorption turned positive in the second half of 2025, and observed that banks over $250 billion in assets took additional office charge-offs.
- Portfolio marks for calibration: MetLife's FY2025 10-K reports a 68% average LTV and 2.1x average DSCR across its commercial mortgage book; Valley National Bancorp's FY2025 10-K reports a 59% weighted average CRE LTV, 1.69 DSCR, and a CRE concentration ratio of 333% of capital.

Implication: do not size from the supervisory ceiling. Size from the binding test among LTV, DSCR, debt yield, and refinance capacity, then check the structure against policy and the exception basket.

## What an Examiner Expects a CRE Credit Policy to Contain

The interagency real estate lending guidelines require underwriting standards addressing at minimum the maximum loan amount by property type, maximum maturities by property type, amortization schedules, pricing structure by loan type, and LTV limits by property type. The OCC Comptroller's Handbook expands that into a per-property-type checklist.

| Policy element | What the policy must state |
|---|---|
| Leverage | Internal LTV limits by property type, at or below supervisory limits, and lower for riskier subcategories |
| Coverage | Minimum DSCR and minimum debt yield by loan or property type |
| Sponsor support | Minimum net worth, guarantee support, and borrower and guarantor cash flow standards; global analysis of asset type, amount and liquidity, global cash flow, direct and contingent liabilities, and tertiary repayment sources |
| Tenor and structure | Maximum tenor; amortization criteria; limits on non-amortizing loans; IO limits on stabilized CRE; limits on partial-recourse and nonrecourse loans; required covenants and takeout commitments |
| Equity and reserves | Minimum initial hard equity with acceptable types, sources, and timing; standards for and limits on interest reserves; preleasing, presale, and release requirements |
| Portfolio and administration | Diversification and concentration limits; exception tracking and board reporting; appraisal ordering and review; documentation minimums; construction disbursement controls; environmental standards |

## Regulatory Leverage Ceilings and the Exception Basket

| Loan category | Supervisory LTV limit |
|---|---|
| Raw land | 65% |
| Land development or improved lots | 75% |
| Construction: commercial, multifamily, other nonresidential | 80% |
| Construction: one- to four-family residential | 85% |
| Improved property: commercial, multifamily, other nonresidential | 85% |
| Owner-occupied one- to four-family and home equity | No set limit; credit enhancement required at 90% or above |

- Loans originated above supervisory limits should not exceed 100% of total capital in aggregate, and within that basket, commercial, agricultural, multifamily, and other non-one-to-four-family loans should not exceed 30% of total capital. The entire outstanding balance counts, not only the excess.
- Loans that met the limit at origination and later exceed it because value fell are not exceptions and do not enter the basket.
- Meeting the limit is not a safe harbor; loans at those levels are not automatically sound.
- For construction credits, HVCRE capital treatment turns on a separate test: at least 15% of the property's appraised as-completed value contributed by the borrower and left in the project, with LTV at or below the applicable supervisory limit.

## Sizing and Structure Frames by Lender and Property Type

Directional. No regulator publishes minimum DSCR or debt yield by lender type; those are institution-set. Agencies are the exception because the programs publish their grids.

| Lender | Leverage frame | Coverage frame | Structure signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relationship bank / credit union | Internal limits at or below supervisory LTV; realized bank CRE portfolio LTVs in the high 50s to low 60s per FY2025 filings | Institution-set, tested as-if amortizing | Recourse common; covenants on DSCR, LTV, LTC, net worth, liquidity |
| Life company | 68% average LTV in MetLife's FY2025 book, with 56% of balances below 65% LTV | 2.1x average DSCR in that book | Long fixed terms, low leverage, minimal transitional appetite |
| Agency multifamily | Freddie Mac Optigo fixed-rate: 75% max LTV for 5-year and sub-7-year terms, 80% for 7-year and longer; full-term IO capped at 65% (terms of 7 years or less) and 70% (longer) | Minimum 1.25x amortizing DCR at every term | Non-recourse except standard carve-outs; escrows and replacement reserves generally required; 30-year maximum amortization |
| CMBS | Structure- and reserve-led | Loan-level debt yield and DSCR floors | Cash management, springing lockbox, rigid documents |
| Debt fund / bridge | Sized to as-is value plus funded reserves | Tested against in-place and stabilized cases | Milestones, holdbacks, extension tests, heavier fees |

Both agencies size the exit as well as the entry. Freddie Mac waives its refinance test only when the amortizing DCR is 1.40x or greater and LTV is 60% or less. Fannie Mae sizes the exit at the minimum Tier 2 DSCR and maximum Tier 2 LTV for the product, using a reversion cap rate at least 2.0% above the initial cap rate and a refinance rate at least 2.25% above the 10-year amortizing underwriting floor.

| Structure item | Frame |
|---|---|
| Maximum amortization, income-producing CRE | 30 years; 15 to 30 years appropriate in most cases |
| By property type | Multifamily up to 30 years; office, retail, industrial generally 25; hotel generally not more than 20 |
| Interest-only tenor | Usually 3 to 5 years maximum, shorter than amortizing tenors |
| DSCR test during IO | As-if amortizing; IO LTV and DSCR standards more conservative than amortizing, with the sizing mitigant being to lend the balance an amortizing loan would reach at maturity |
| Ground lease | Where the loan does not fully amortize, the lease should extend beyond the amortization period, customarily 20 years; where it does, 10 years beyond maturity is usually sufficient; ground rent is an expense in DSCR |

An IO renewal, refinance, or extension on stabilized property, and re-amortizing a maturing balance back over the original period, are treated in supervisory guidance as possible indicators of a troubled credit rather than neutral structuring choices.

## Sponsor, Guarantor, and Recourse

| Test | What to establish | Why it binds |
|---|---|---|
| Global cash flow | Consolidate business statements, tax returns, and K-1s across all entities, including personal debt service, taxes, and living expenses | Required even when liquid assets look ample, because those assets may be needed elsewhere |
| Verified liquidity and net worth | Bank-verified, not borrower-asserted; test liquidity of assets collateralizing the guarantee; policy-set net worth minimum, usually paired with a liquidity covenant | Unpledged guarantor assets are not a substitute for project equity; both are common CRE financial covenants |
| Contingent liabilities | Total number and dollar amount of guarantees extended to all lenders, not just yours | A guarantor stretched across other lenders cannot support every project at once |
| Willingness and enforceability | Prior performance, economic incentive, significant investment in the project, and a legally enforceable guarantee | Willingness may be presumed where economic incentive exists, absent contrary evidence |
| Equity timing | Equity contributed before construction disbursements begin | Deferred developer profit, unearned fees, overhead, and accrued land carry are generally not equity |

| Recourse form | What the guarantor owes | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Full recourse | Entire obligation | Bank construction and transitional credits |
| Partial principal | A stated amount or percentage of principal | Cash flow credible but unproven |
| Payment / interest-only guaranty | Interest and carry, not principal | Lease-up and stabilization |
| Completion guaranty | Lien-free completion per plans and budget | Every construction loan; separate from payment risk |
| Carry guaranty | Operating shortfalls, taxes, insurance for a defined period | Bridges the gap to stabilization |
| Burn-off / stepped-down | Reduces or releases on stated conditions | Conditions must be monitored and confirmed before release |
| Loss-recourse carve-out | Lender's actual losses from specified bad acts | The base non-recourse position |
| Springing full recourse | Entire loan becomes recourse | Voluntary bankruptcy, unpermitted transfer, additional debt, separateness breaches |

Standard carve-out acts include fraud, misrepresentation, voluntary bankruptcy, environmental issues, unapproved liens, waste, prohibited transfers, and diversion of funds. Two items stay case-by-case because no primary source publishes a number: sponsor liquidity and net worth covenant levels (multiples of loan amount or percentages of commitment), and burn-off trigger levels. Document the test, measurement date, and verification method rather than assuming a market standard.

## Reserves, Fees, and Pricing Frames

Pricing and spread figures are directional and time-sensitive. Verify against live quotes.

- Interest reserves require an explicit policy standard and limit. Project cash flow is applied to interest before the reserve is drawn, and repacking a depleted interest reserve with new debt is a recognized red flag for credit deterioration.
- Tax and insurance escrows and replacement reserve deposits are generally required on agency multifamily loans and are the market default for CMBS. Replacement reserves are underwritten per square foot per year for office, retail, and industrial and per unit per year for multifamily, varying with age and condition.
- Management fees are typically underwritten at 3% to 5% of effective gross income for office, retail (exclusive of reimbursements), and industrial, and 5% of revenue for multifamily. Leasing commissions are typically underwritten at 4% of total lease payments for new leases and 2% for renewals, with tenant improvement costs higher for new than renewing tenants.
- Q2 2026 average commercial mortgage spreads were 204 bps and multifamily spreads 162 bps (CBRE, published 2026-08-03). Freddie Mac's published conventional first-mortgage application fee is the greater of $2,000 or 0.1% of loan amount (Optigo fixed-rate product sheet, 4/2026). Pricing structure by loan type and pricing and profitability objectives are required policy elements, so a pricing exception is a policy exception and is reported as one.
- CREFC's Q1 2026 Board of Governors Sentiment Index fell 20.2% to 100.1 from 125.4, with respondents expecting banks (46%) and private credit or debt funds (34%) to be the most active new lenders and naming secondary and lower-quality office as the highest-risk borrower cohort (56%).

## Policy Exception Categories

Supervisory guidance says exceptions should be permitted only on a limited basis, each documented as to how it fails to conform, approved at the appropriate level, and reported to the board with number, nature, justification, and trend.

| Category | Examples | Reporting |
|---|---|---|
| Supervisory LTV exception | Origination LTV above the interagency limit for that category | Identified in the records and reported at least quarterly to the board; counted against the 100% and 30% baskets at full balance |
| Internal policy exception | LTV, DSCR, debt yield, tenor, or amortization outside internal limits; sponsor liquidity, net worth, or global coverage below minimum | Written justification in the permanent loan file; significant exceptions reported individually to the board |
| Structural exception | Recourse below policy, deferred equity, IO on stabilized property, interest reserve outside limits | Same |
| Documentation exception | Missing or stale financials, appraisals, rent rolls, or covenant certificates | Aged and tracked to clearance |
| Pricing exception | Coupon or fee below the policy return threshold | Tracked for trend |

Exceptions should be aged and stratified enough to show trends by volume, loan officer, and type. An excessive or rising exception rate reads as relaxed underwriting, a stale policy, or a policy out of step with the board's risk tolerance.

## Red Flags

- Sizing to the supervisory LTV limit as though it were a target, treating conformity as evidence of soundness, or clearing DSCR only because the loan is interest-only with no as-if-amortizing test run.
- Guarantor liquidity taken from an unverified personal financial statement, unpledged guarantor assets counted as project equity, or a guarantor whose obligations to other lenders were never tallied.
- Equity deferred past the first construction draw, equity composed of deferred developer profit, unearned fees, or accrued land carry, or an interest reserve repacked with new debt to keep a construction loan current.
- Recourse burn-off written with no defined test, measurement date, or verification obligation.
- Exceptions approved but never aged, stratified, or trended, or significant exceptions that never reach the board.
- Loan document debt service covenant definitions that differ from the underwriting DSCR with no reconciliation.
- An exit that relies on cap rate compression or rate relief rather than amortization and cash flow growth clearing a stated refinance test.
- CRE concentration approaching the 100% construction-and-development or 300% total-CRE screening criteria without heightened risk management practices in place. See [CRE Concentration and Stress Testing](knowledge/cre-concentration-and-stress-testing.md).
- Policy benchmarks applied to a credit whose real question is classification, accrual, or accommodation. See [Regulatory Risk Rating and Classification](knowledge/regulatory-risk-rating-and-classification.md).
