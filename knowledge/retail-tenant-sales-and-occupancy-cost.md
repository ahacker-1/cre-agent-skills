# Retail Tenant Sales and Occupancy Cost

Last updated: 2026-09-01

Scope: U.S. retail acquisitions, refinancings, and asset reviews across grocery-anchored, power, strip / unanchored, lifestyle, mall, mixed-use, pad, and single-tenant net lease (STNL) assets. This is an educational underwriting reference, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Tenant sales reports are unaudited landlord reporting, not GAAP revenue, and issuer-published occupancy cost figures are non-GAAP operating statistics. Confirm lease language with counsel, tenant credit with the actual lease signatory and any guarantor, and market conditions with active brokers and lenders.

---

## Current Context

Directional, dated market observations. Re-verify before use.

- Trepp data reported by MBA NewsLink put retail CMBS delinquency at 6.96% in July 2026, up 5 basis points, against 7.86% overall, 11.91% office, and 1.13% industrial - retail sits in the middle of the distress pack, not at the top.
- The Boulder Group reported Q2 2026 single-tenant net lease retail cap rates at 6.60%, up 5 bps, with McDonald's and Chick-fil-A ground leases asking 4.45%, investment-grade product under 10% of retail inventory, and retail supply up 16.2% quarter over quarter concentrated in non-credit assets.
- Tanger's 2025 Form 10-K reported portfolio occupancy cost at 9.7% of tenant sales, up from 9.5% in 2024, 9.3% in 2023, 8.6% in 2022, and 8.1% in 2021 - occupancy cost has been climbing off a pandemic-era trough.
- The U.S. Census Bureau reported Q2 2026 e-commerce at 17.1% of total adjusted retail sales, growing 12.2% year over year versus 6.7% for total retail sales, so store-reported sales increasingly understate a location's economic contribution.

Underwriting implication: do not read a single occupancy cost number as a verdict. Read it against the tenant's category, that tenant's other stores, the tenant's importance to the center, and the cost of replacing the tenant.

## Core Definitions and Formulas

| Term | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sales PSF | Trailing 12-month reported gross sales at the location divided by that location's leased SF | Use the same trailing period on both sides; exclude stores open under 12 months |
| Total occupancy cost | Base rent + percentage rent + CAM + real estate taxes + insurance + marketing / promotional fund, at that location | Tanger's 10-K uses base rent, CAM, taxes, insurance, and advertising and promotions; rating agency criteria describe fixed rent plus percentage rent plus all reimbursables |
| Occupancy cost ratio (health ratio) | Total occupancy cost divided by gross sales, same trailing period | The single most useful tenant-health screen where sales are reported |
| Breakpoint | Sales level above which percentage rent is owed | Natural breakpoint = base rent divided by the percentage rate |
| Health ratio delta | Change in occupancy cost ratio year over year | Rising ratio with flat rent means falling sales - the earliest quiet warning |

Do not restate DSCR, debt yield, or NOI mechanics here; use [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md). For weighting tenant risk into an asset-level score, use [Risk Scoring](knowledge/risk-scoring.md).

## Occupancy Cost Frames by Category

Screens, not pass/fail rules. Only the grocery and power-center-anchor rows and the two issuer reference points are traceable to sources; everything else stays case-by-case.

| Category | Frame | Traceability |
|---|---|---|
| Grocery anchor | Roughly 2%-7% of sales depending on volume; thin margins make grocers the most rent-sensitive anchor type | Morningstar DBRS CRE property analysis criteria |
| Power center / category-killer anchor | Roughly 10%; above 10% triggers a viability review of the tenant's ability to keep paying rent and operate profitably | Morningstar DBRS CRE property analysis criteria |
| QSR / fast casual | A large national fast-casual operator reported occupancy cost at 5.2% of revenue for FY2025 (5.0% in FY2024) at the company level | Chipotle FY2025 Form 10-K |
| Outlet / mall in-line (portfolio level) | A national outlet REIT reported 9.7% of tenant sales for 2025 | Tanger FY2025 Form 10-K |
| Drug, discount, apparel, full-service restaurant, fitness, medical / service, entertainment | Case-by-case. No primary or quasi-primary source in the companion research supports a category default; benchmark each tenant against its own other stores and against signed comparable leases | Stated as unsupported in the research note |

Two structural rules survive across categories: higher-productivity locations support higher occupancy cost ratios, so a ratio must always be read next to the sales PSF that produced it; and low-margin categories (grocery, club, hard discount) tolerate far less than high-margin categories (jewelry, specialty accessories) at the same sales PSF. One productivity data point: Sprouts reported FY2025 net sales of $8.806 billion across 12,992,097 SF at period end, roughly $678-$701 PSF depending on whether ending or average square footage is used - one specialty grocer derived from disclosed figures, not a supermarket category norm.

## Reading a Tenant Sales Report

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is the tenant contractually obligated to report, and how often? | Most open-air in-line and net lease tenants never report; the ratio is then not available, and should be marked so rather than estimated |
| What does the lease define as "gross sales"? | Gift cards, employee sales, sales tax, returns, and inter-store transfers are commonly excluded, which changes the denominator |
| Does the report include e-commerce fulfilled from or returned to this store? | Census data put e-commerce at 17.1% of Q2 2026 retail sales; omnichannel attribution can distort store sales in both directions |
| Is the store open a full 12 months, and was it remodeled or closed temporarily? | Partial-year and ramp-up stores produce meaningless ratios |

## Tenant Credit Tiers

| Tier | Typical evidence | Underwriting posture |
|---|---|---|
| Investment grade | Rated corporate credit at the lease signatory or a rated parent guaranty | Lowest credit spread; verify the rating attaches to the entity actually on the lease |
| Investment-grade equivalent / large national unrated | Public filer or large private with audited financials, national footprint | Underwrite financials directly; do not treat brand recognition as credit |
| National franchisor-branded, franchisee-operated | Franchise agreement, franchisee financials, unit-level economics | Credit is the franchisee's, not the brand's, absent a parent guaranty - this is the single most common credit misread in STNL and pad underwriting |
| Regional chain | Multi-unit operator, limited disclosure, often personal or corporate guaranty | Require financials plus guaranty; expect lender scrutiny |
| Local / independent | Owner guaranty, security deposit, limited or no financials | Underwrite to replacement cost and downtime, not to the tenant |

STNL context: Realty Income's FY2025 Form 10-K reported 32.2% of total portfolio annualized base rent from investment-grade clients, subsidiaries, or affiliates; top 20 clients at 35.8% of ABR with 11 of 20 investment grade; and a portfolio weighted average remaining lease term of approximately 8.8 years. Credit share is a portfolio-level output, not an automatic property-level attribute.

## Distress, Bankruptcy, and Store-Closure Watch Signals

| Signal | What to do |
|---|---|
| Occupancy cost ratio rising two consecutive reporting periods | Model a renewal at reduced rent or a vacancy; ask the tenant for a store-level outlook |
| Store ranks in the tenant's bottom quartile by sales | Treat renewal as unlikely and price the backfill |
| Tenant announces a closure program or a going-concern qualification | Move the unit to a watch list before any filing |
| Chapter 11 filing | The lease must be assumed or rejected by the earlier of 120 days after the order for relief or plan confirmation, extendable once by 90 days for cause; further extension requires the lessor's prior written consent (11 U.S.C. 365(d)(4)) |
| Post-petition rent | The debtor must timely perform post-petition lease obligations under 365(d)(3); track stub rent separately |
| Proposed assignment in a shopping center | 365(b)(3) requires adequate assurance on the assignee's financial condition, that percentage rent will not decline substantially, no breach of radius, location, use, or exclusivity provisions, and no disruption of tenant mix or balance |
| Rejection damages | Capped at the greater of one year's rent or 15% of remaining term rent (max three years), plus unpaid pre-petition rent, under 502(b)(6); a March 2026 decision reported by Jones Day applied the cap even to a solvent debtor |

Market context: Retail Dive's tracker lists 2026 U.S. filings including Saks Global (January 14, 2026), Pat McGrath Cosmetics, Francesca's, Eddie Bauer, QVC Group, and Sleep Number, after 2025 filings including Joann, Forever 21, Hudson's Bay, Rite Aid, At Home, and Claire's. Coresight's midyear 2026 outlook frames the year around roughly 7,900 U.S. store closures against 5,500 openings, with detail behind a subscription.

## Renewal Probability, Dark Store, and Backfill Economics

Renewal probability drivers, in rough order of predictive weight: the store's occupancy cost ratio and its rank inside the tenant's own fleet; sales trend rather than sales level; the spread between in-place rent and current market rent; remaining option economics and whether the option rent is below market; the tenant's capital position and any announced closure program; co-tenancy and anchor stability at the center; and the cost and downtime of the alternative for both sides.

Go dark is an economic event before it is a legal one: a tenant that stops operating while continuing to pay rent still removes traffic and can trip co-tenancy rights held by neighbors. Cox, Castle & Nicholson's negotiation checklist for go-dark provisions covers an initial continuous-operation period, 90 to 180 days' advance notice, a no-default condition, continued rent, continued maintenance and insurance, landlord recapture and termination rights, and landlord recovery of unamortized TI allowance and brokerage commissions - confirm which of these the subject lease actually contains before assuming a dark box can be recaptured.

Backfill cost benchmarks, directional and dated:

| Format | Reported figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Open-air / grocery-anchored | 2025 leasing costs of approximately $149.2 million on roughly 10.8 million SF, or $41.65 PSF ($115.6 million TI, $33.6 million external commissions); new-lease rent $22.61 PSF vs. renewals and options $21.50 PSF | Kimco FY2025 Form 10-K |
| Mall / premium outlet | 2025 average annual initial base minimum rent on new leases of $65.09 PSF with average tenant allowance of $63.92 PSF | Simon Property Group FY2025 Form 10-K |

The gap between new-lease and renewal cost is the whole renewal argument. When a renewal at flat or slightly lower rent avoids a full TI and commission cycle plus downtime, the renewal usually wins on present value even at a rent the rent roll makes look weak.

## STNL and Credit Tenant Frameworks

Single-tenant net lease and credit tenant analysis substitutes tenant credit and lease duration for the sales-based health ratio, because sales are rarely reported. Underwrite four things instead: the credit of the entity on the lease and any guarantor; remaining term and rent escalation structure; rent-to-replacement-rent, meaning how far in-place rent sits above what a replacement tenant would pay for the box; and residual value of the real estate if the tenant leaves. The Boulder Group's Q2 2026 report shows the pricing consequence of the first factor - retail STNL at 6.60% overall, best-in-class ground leases asking 4.45%, and investment-grade product under 10% of retail inventory - alongside its observation that buyers are scrutinizing tenant financials and store-level performance. Where a tenant does report sales, a low occupancy cost ratio on a long-dated STNL lease is the strongest single indicator that the rent survives a tenant restructuring.

## Red Flags

- Occupancy cost ratio computed from a partial-year, ramp-up, or recently remodeled store and presented as a stabilized health ratio.
- A category default applied where no source supports one - especially apparel, fitness, medical, or entertainment thresholds asserted as if they were standards.
- Franchisee-operated units underwritten at the franchisor's credit without a parent guaranty on the actual lease.
- A dark-but-paying tenant treated as occupied in the rent roll while its neighbors hold co-tenancy remedies.
- Underwriting an uncapped landlord claim on tenant rejection; 502(b)(6) caps it, including for solvent debtors.
- Assuming a lease can be assigned to a replacement operator without testing the 365(b)(3) shopping-center adequate assurance items, including percentage rent, exclusivity, radius, and tenant mix.
- Issuer portfolio-level occupancy cost figures used as the subject property's benchmark without the subject's own tenant sales file.
