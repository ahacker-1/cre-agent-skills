# Office Benchmarks

Last updated: 2026-06-16

Scope: U.S. office acquisitions, recapitalizations, refinancings, and asset-level hold/sell decisions. These benchmarks are directional guardrails, not investment advice. Always validate current submarket conditions, tenant demand, taxes, insurance, building systems, and local leasing economics.

---

## Market Context

Office underwriting must separate prime, well-located assets from commodity vacancy. The national office market is improving from a stressed base, but recovery is uneven by market, asset quality, tenant industry, and commute pattern.

Current guardrails:

- Overall U.S. vacancy remains elevated; CBRE reported 18.6% in Q1 2026, while Cushman & Wakefield reported 20.2%.
- Prime vacancy is materially tighter than overall vacancy. CBRE reported 12.7% prime vacancy in Q1 2026 and Midtown Manhattan prime vacancy at 2.9%.
- Net absorption has turned positive in multiple national surveys, but it is concentrated in high-quality buildings and select gateway or innovation markets.
- New construction is limited. CBRE reported Q1 2026 completions at the lowest quarterly level in its time series, and Cushman reported the construction pipeline down sharply from its 2020 peak.
- Sublease inventory is a leading recovery indicator. Cushman reported national sublease inventory down for the eighth consecutive quarter in Q1 2026.

Use these facts as a market-regime frame:

| Signal | Positive | Neutral | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net absorption | Positive for 2+ quarters and broadening across classes | Positive but concentrated in a few buildings | Negative or driven only by conversions/removals |
| Prime vs commodity gap | Prime vacancy falling with rising tour velocity | Prime stable, commodity weak | Prime vacancy also rising |
| Sublease inventory | Declining for 2+ quarters | Flat | Rising or discounting aggressively |
| Construction pipeline | Limited new supply | Select build-to-suit supply | Spec supply still meaningful |
| Asking vs taking rent | Spread narrowing | Stable spread | Spread widening due to concessions |

## Asset Quality Tiers

Use this tiering instead of relying only on Class A/B/C labels.

| Tier | Description | Underwriting Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Prime / trophy | Newer or deeply renovated, strong transit or talent node, modern systems, amenity-rich, high ESG/workplace quality | Can justify lower vacancy, better renewal probability, and stronger rent growth if local evidence supports it |
| Competitive Class A | Functional, well-capitalized, good location, can win tenants with normal TI and concessions | Underwrite market vacancy and normalized downtime |
| Transitional / value-add | Needs capital to compete, may have older systems, amenity or lobby gap, leasing story depends on investment | Model capital plan, downtime, and TI/LC explicitly |
| Commodity / stranded | Weak location or obsolete floorplates/systems, high vacancy, unclear tenant depth | Treat as repositioning, conversion, or land/value recovery case, not stabilized office |

## Leasing Benchmarks

Underwrite by lease economics, not headline asking rent.

| Item | Directional Office Guardrail |
|---|---|
| Market vacancy | Use current submarket and class-specific data; national vacancy is too blunt |
| Downtime after rollover | 6-18 months for ordinary office space; longer for large blocks, weak submarkets, or heavy build-out |
| Renewal probability | Higher for mission-critical, built-out, high-friction users; lower for downsizing, hybrid-heavy, or over-housed tenants |
| Free rent | Market-specific; rises with vacancy and build-out friction |
| Rent escalations | Fixed annual bumps or CPI caps are common; confirm in each lease |
| Sublease competition | Treat as direct competition when sublease space is same quality, location, and term length |
| Space measurement | Confirm BOMA standard, rentable area, usable area, and load factor before comparing rents |

## Flight-to-Quality Signals

Office demand is not only "more or less space." It is a sorting process.

Positive:

- Tenant expands or renews into higher-quality space.
- Building wins tenants from lower-quality competitors.
- Amenities, transit, building systems, security, and sustainability directly support tenant return-to-office goals.
- Large-block availability is scarce in the building's quality tier.
- Tour activity and proposal activity are rising before signed leases appear.

Negative:

- Leasing depends only on price concessions.
- Occupancy is held by tenants with near-term contraction rights or shadow space.
- Sublease competition in the same building or immediate comp set undercuts direct space.
- Building needs capital beyond the buyer's planned reserve.
- Major tenants have low space utilization, weak business momentum, or consolidation risk.

## Operating Benchmarks

Office operating expenses vary sharply by market, service level, security, energy, insurance, taxes, union labor, and owner-managed vs third-party-managed structure.

Treat these as check prompts:

- Real estate taxes: verify reassessment risk after sale; office values have moved unevenly, so do not assume taxes fall with purchase price unless local law supports it.
- Insurance: isolate property, liability, umbrella, and casualty coverage. High-rise, coastal, seismic, and older-building profiles need separate review.
- Utilities: normalize for occupancy and hours of operation.
- Cleaning / janitorial: compare to occupancy, service level, union labor, and amenity program.
- Repairs and maintenance: distinguish recurring R&M from deferred capital.
- Security: higher-touch buildings may need 24/7 lobby, access control, and elevator/security integration.
- Management fee: compare against asset complexity and leasing intensity.

## Red Flags

- Large rollover within first 24 months with no signed renewal or credible leasing pipeline.
- Major tenant pays above-market rent and has contraction, termination, or weak-credit indicators.
- Rent roll shows high leased occupancy but low physical occupancy or signed-not-commenced space.
- Sublease blocks compete directly with vacant direct space.
- Asking rent growth is positive but taking rents or net effective rents are flat after concessions.
- TI/LC capital is modeled below actual market fit-out cost.
- Building is called Class A but lacks systems, amenities, transit/location, or capital plan to compete with true prime assets.
- Debt sizing assumes stabilized NOI before downtime and lease-up capital.
- Conversion value is used as downside support without a real floorplate, zoning, cost, and financing test.
