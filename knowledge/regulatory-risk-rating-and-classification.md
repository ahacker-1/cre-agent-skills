# Regulatory Risk Rating and Classification

Last updated: 2026-09-01

Scope: U.S. bank, thrift, and credit union treatment of commercial real estate loans under the interagency supervisory classification framework, including watchlist and criticized designations, nonaccrual, modification and workout treatment, appraisal reconsideration triggers, and credit risk review expectations. This is an educational credit-side reference, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Classification, nonaccrual, charge-off, allowance, and regulatory reporting conclusions belong to the institution and its regulator. Non-bank lenders (debt funds, life companies, CMBS originators, mortgage REITs) are not subject to this framework and use internal or rating-agency grades instead. Verify current supervisory guidance, call report instructions, and your own board-approved credit policy before acting.

- FDIC Quarterly Banking Profile, second quarter 2026: the industry past-due and nonaccrual (PDNA) rate fell 9 basis points to 1.44%, and nonfarm nonresidential CRE PDNA fell 14 basis points to 1.52%, the largest quarterly dollar decline of any loan category. The nonfarm nonresidential noncurrent rate was 1.23% and reserve coverage was 172.7%.
- OCC Semiannual Risk Perspective, Spring 2026 (data as of December 31, 2025): CRE headwinds across several property types have not produced significant credit defaults, but refinance risk merits continued attention; office net absorption turned positive in the second half of 2025, while banks over $250 billion in assets took additional charge-offs on office loans.
- Federal Reserve July 2026 Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey (released August 3, 2026): banks reported easing standards for nonfarm nonresidential and multifamily CRE loans in Q2 2026, with construction and land development standards basically unchanged.
- The governing CRE workout guidance is the interagency Policy Statement on Prudent Commercial Real Estate Loan Accommodations and Workouts, adopted June 29, 2023 (FDIC FIL-23-034, OCC Bulletin 2023-23, Federal Reserve SR 23-5). It superseded the 2009 guidance, added a short-term accommodation section, and applies at all stages of the economic cycle to institutions of every size.

## Supervisory Classification Definitions

The agencies use one common scale. "Pass" has no formal regulatory definition; it is the residual for credits not covered below.

| Grade | Interagency definition (condensed) | Practical CRE markers | Accrual consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pass | No regulatory definition; not special mention or adversely classified | Repayment supported by property cash flow, sponsor, or collateral on reasonable terms | Accrual |
| Special mention (OAEM) | Potential weaknesses deserving management's close attention that, if left uncorrected, may deteriorate repayment prospects or the institution's credit position at a future date; not adversely classified | Declining revenue trend, reduced collateral margin, missing current financials, unordered valuation, structural or documentation weakness | Normally accrual |
| Substandard | Inadequately protected by the current sound worth and paying capacity of the obligor or the collateral; must have a well-defined weakness jeopardizing liquidation of the debt; distinct possibility of some loss | Inadequate debt service coverage, unprofitable operations, inadequate liquidity, marginal capitalization, repayment dependent on collateral | Nonaccrual where full collection of principal and interest is in doubt |
| Doubtful | All substandard weaknesses plus collection or liquidation in full is highly questionable and improbable, with loss deferred pending a specific event | Default with a pending sale, capital injection, refinancing, or valuation to be resolved | Nonaccrual required |
| Loss | Uncollectible and of such little value that continuance as a bankable asset is not warranted | Deficiency beyond fair value of collateral less costs to sell, with no other reliable repayment source | Charge off in the period the obligation becomes uncollectible |

Special mention is a distinct level of concern, not a compromise between pass and substandard, and should not be used to avoid making the harder call.

## Criticized, Classified, and Watchlist

| Term | What it covers | Who defines it |
|---|---|---|
| Watchlist | Institution-designated loans management is monitoring more closely; a prudently modified loan to a sound borrower may be watchlisted without adverse classification | Institution policy; no interagency definition |
| Criticized | Special mention plus all classified grades | Supervisory convention |
| Classified | Substandard, doubtful, and loss only; excludes pass and special mention | Interagency definitions |
| Split rating | More than one grade on one exposure where collateral or structure supports only part of it: substandard / doubtful / loss on a collateral-dependent loan across a value range; pass / adverse where part is secured by cash, liquid collateral, or an unconditional guarantee; and partial charge-off where the serviced residual balance is rated on its own merits | Interagency definitions |

For a problem CRE loan dependent on the sale of collateral: the portion adequately secured by the fair value of the real estate less costs to sell generally should be classified no worse than substandard, the excess should be classified loss, and doubtful applies only where full loss may be mitigated by pending events or the loss amount cannot reasonably be determined. Doubtful should be used infrequently and reassessed promptly.

## Mapping to an Internal Dual Risk Rating Scale

The agencies do not prescribe a rating system. They expect every system to address both the obligor's ability and willingness to repay and the loss protection provided by structure and collateral, using either a single rating or dual obligor and facility ratings. The grade count and mapping below are a common convention, not a regulatory default; the institution's board-approved policy controls.

| Dimension | What it measures | Regulatory tie |
|---|---|---|
| Obligor / PD rating | Probability of default: repayment capacity, cash flow to service debt, leverage, liquidity, business plan execution, willingness to repay | The point at which repayment capacity shows well-defined weaknesses is the substandard threshold |
| Facility / LGD rating | Loss given default: collateral quality and margin, lien position, covenants, guarantees, insurance, amortization and maturity structure | Drives split ratings, the loss portion of a collateral-dependent credit, and allowance measurement |
| Expected loss | Product of PD and LGD; varies with economic conditions | Not a regulatory grade; supports pricing, allowance, and portfolio decisions |

Stated system expectations: board approval and clear accountability; every exposure rated; several pass grades so pass risk is differentiated; ratings accurate, timely, and dynamic so they change when risk changes; criteria defined using both objective and subjective factors; independent validation and back-testing of rating definitions; and each rating supported and documented in the credit file. Do not restate CRE sizing formulas here; use [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md).

## Nonaccrual and Return to Accrual

| Question | Supervisory answer |
|---|---|
| When does accrual stop? | When the asset is maintained on a cash basis because of borrower deterioration, when payment in full of principal or interest is not expected, or when principal or interest has been in default 90 days or more, unless the asset is both well secured and in the process of collection |
| Is "in the process of collection" a 30-day rule? | The 30-day period is a benchmark, not an outer limit; a longer period is acceptable where timing and amount of repayment are reasonably certain |
| Are nonaccrual loans classified? | Nonaccrual loans will almost always be classified, and doubtful assets require nonaccrual treatment |
| Waiver vs deferral | A waiver of interest or principal generally puts a commercial loan on nonaccrual; a deferral without waiver is a judgment call on whether full payment is still expected |
| Return to accrual after restructuring | Requires sustained repayment performance for a reasonable period, generally a minimum of six months of cash or cash-equivalent payments; performance before the restructuring may count. No formal minimum is specified in the accounting or regulatory literature, so facts and circumstances govern |
| Note A / Note B split | Note A is sized off the fair value of the collateral, may return to accrual when the conditions are met, and generally would not be adversely classified; Note B is uncollectible, classified loss, and must be charged off |
| Partial charge-off without a note split | The loan should remain on or be placed in nonaccrual, because the charge-off indicates full repayment of contractual amounts is not expected |
| Can a charge-off be rebooked? | No. A charged-off amount is never reversed or re-booked to increase recorded investment or amortized cost, though expected recoveries are a component of the ASC 326 estimate |

## Modification, Workout, and Accounting Treatment

Under ASC Topic 326 with ASU 2022-02 adopted, troubled debt restructuring recognition and measurement no longer applies; the reportable event is a modification to a borrower experiencing financial difficulty in one or more of four forms: principal forgiveness, an interest rate reduction, an other-than-insignificant payment delay, and a term extension. Institutions that have not adopted ASC 326 or ASU 2022-02 continue to identify and report TDRs under the applicable reporting instructions. Expected credit losses on modified loans are estimated under the same CECL methodology as every other loan, collectively unless risk characteristics have diverged; for a collateral-dependent asset the allowance is measured using the fair value of the collateral less costs to sell where applicable. Loss classification and allowance measurement are related but not equivalent.

Examiners will not criticize an institution for engaging in a workout, even where the loan is adversely classified, when management has all six of the following.

| Workout program element | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Well-conceived, documented plan per loan | Updated borrower, project, and guarantor / sponsor financials; current collateral valuation supporting the plan; appropriate term, amortization, covenants, and curtailment or re-margining requirements; appropriate legal analysis and agreements |
| Global debt service coverage analysis | Realistic cash flow projections inclusive of borrower and guarantor cash flows and their combined obligations, including contingent obligations, plus availability and accessibility of repayment sources |
| Guarantor cash flow analysis | A legally enforceable guarantee from a guarantor with the financial ability, demonstrated willingness, and incentive to support the credit may preclude or reduce adverse classification |
| Demonstrated monitoring | Willingness and ability to monitor borrower and guarantor performance under the workout terms |
| Accurate internal ratings | A risk rating system that accurately and consistently reflects the risk actually present in the workout arrangement |
| GAAP allowance methodology | Timely loss recognition through provision expense and charge-offs, plus an allowance on related off-balance-sheet exposures |

What does not by itself justify a downgrade: a collateral value that has fallen below the loan balance on a performing loan; the borrower's association with a distressed industry; a prudent renewal or restructuring of a maturing loan to a borrower who can repay on reasonable terms; or elevated refinance risk absent well-defined weaknesses. What does justify one despite a current payment record: an interest reserve or serial extensions masking a stalled project or an inability to meet reasonable repayment terms. For structuring the borrower-side transaction, see [Workout and Extension Structures](knowledge/workout-and-extension-structures.md) and [Extension / Workout Strategy Builder](skills/capital-markets/extension-workout-strategy-builder.md); for securitized loans see [CMBS Servicing and Default Playbook](knowledge/cmbs-servicing-and-default-playbook.md).

## Appraisal and Evaluation Reconsideration Triggers

The CRE appraisal threshold is $500,000, raised from $250,000 by final rule announced April 2, 2018; an evaluation may be used for exempt transactions. A real-estate-secured business loan of $1 million or less may also be exempt where real estate is not the primary repayment source. A workout is treated as a new real estate-related financial transaction: with no new money beyond reasonable closing costs an evaluation may substitute for an appraisal, and with new money an evaluation is permitted only where there has been no obvious and material change in market conditions and no physical change threatening collateral protection.

| Trigger set | Factors |
|---|---|
| CRE loan in workout (2023 policy statement) | Material deterioration in project performance; conditions for the geographic market and property type; variances between actual conditions and original appraisal assumptions; changes in project specifications; loss of a significant lease or take-out commitment; increases in pre-sale fallout |
| Validity of an existing valuation (2010 interagency guidelines) | Passage of time; local market volatility; changes in terms and availability of financing; natural disasters; limited or over supply of competing properties; improvements to the subject or competing properties; lack of maintenance; changes in economic and market assumptions such as capitalization rates and lease terms; changes in zoning, building materials, or technology; environmental contamination |
| Regulator override | The agencies reserve the right to require an appraisal or evaluation on an existing real estate credit when safety and soundness concerns exist, allowing a reasonable time to obtain it |

Appraisal assumptions from a qualified appraiser appropriate to the property get reasonable deference; marginal differences from norms are not challenged, and credit-analysis adjustments are not based on unlikely worst-case scenarios. Where a valuation is stale or the facts materially deviate from its assumptions, adjust the estimated value for credit analysis and classification where the adjustment is supportable. No interagency source sets a re-order calendar, so ordering intervals stay case-by-case under board-approved policy. See [Credit Memo and Appraisal Review Standards](knowledge/credit-memo-and-appraisal-review-standards.md).

## Credit Risk Review Independence and Rating Migration Reporting

| Expectation | Standard |
|---|---|
| Independence | Reviewers must not control the loan and must not be part of, or influenced by, the loan approval process. Smaller institutions may use an independent committee of outside directors, or qualified staff not involved in originating or approving the specific credits and whose compensation is not influenced by assigned ratings |
| Reporting line | Whether or not a dedicated department exists, it is prudent for credit risk review to report directly to the board or a board committee; senior management may handle administration only if independence is preserved |
| Relationship to internal audit | Credit risk review is not intended to be performed by internal audit, and internal audit must retain the ability to independently audit the review function. Outsourcing is permitted but board responsibility remains, and using the external auditor raises added independence questions |
| Frequency | Significant loans, loan products, or groups of loans are typically reviewed annually, on renewal, or more frequently when internal or external factors signal deterioration |
| Scope (board-approved annually) | Loans over a set size; samples of smaller, new, and new-product loans; higher-risk indicators and policy exceptions; segments with common risk characteristics; rapidly growing segments; non-lending credit exposures; past due, nonaccrual, renewed, and restructured loans; previously classified and special-attention loans; insider, affiliate, and concentration exposures |
| Depth | Underwriting soundness, reasonableness of assumptions, guarantor and sponsor creditworthiness, credit and collateral documentation, lien perfection, approvals, covenant adherence, compliance with internal nonaccrual and classification policy, credit loss estimation and charge-off timeliness, and the accuracy and timeliness of officer-assigned ratings |
| Rating disputes | Resolved by a pre-arranged process, which may include formal appeals and independent arbitration or may default to the lower-quality rating. Where review sees lower quality than portfolio management, the lower rating typically prevails unless new information changes the reviewer's conclusion |
| Migration reporting | Results go to the board or board committee quarterly, with comparative trends covering changes in overall portfolio quality, risk rating migration, concentration trends, shifting underwriting standards, policy adherence, and management's response to criticisms; report more often when material adverse trends appear |

## Red Flags

- A loan is contractually current only because an interest reserve or a serial extension is funding the payments while the project has stalled.
- A performing loan is downgraded solely because a new appraisal came in below the balance, or a loan with well-defined weaknesses is left at pass solely because it is current.
- Doubtful ratings persist across quarters without the pending event resolving or a reassessment being made.
- Special mention is used as a parking space to avoid making the substandard call.
- A renewed or restructured loan never enters the internal grading system as a modification, so watchlist volume and migration reporting understate the portfolio.
- The loan is returned to accrual before a sustained payment record exists, or a charged-off amount is rebooked when the borrower recovers.
- The workout file lacks updated borrower and guarantor financials, a current valuation, or a global debt service coverage analysis, so the classification is unsupported.
- A guarantee is credited in the rating without evidence of the guarantor's financial ability, willingness, and incentive to perform.
- Credit risk review reports to the chief lending officer, or reviewers rate credits they helped approve.
- Bank classification vocabulary is applied to a debt fund, life company, CMBS, or credit union facility that is not subject to the interagency framework.
