# Office Lender Criteria

Last updated: 2026-06-16

Scope: U.S. office acquisition, refinance, recapitalization, and transitional financing fit. This is an educational underwriting reference, not financing advice. Confirm live lender appetite, loan quotes, covenants, and legal terms with active lenders and counsel.

---

## Current Capital Markets Context

Office lending is selective. High-quality stabilized assets are financeable, while commodity vacancy, near-term rollover, weak tenant credit, and uncertain capital needs face constrained proceeds or lender pass.

Current source-backed frame:

- CBRE reported Q1 2026 permanent office loan LTV ratios rising to 61.4% from 58.4% in Q4 2025, reflecting improving lender appetite for high-quality assets.
- Trepp reported office CMBS delinquency at 12.34% in January 2026, an all-time high in that data series.
- The Federal Reserve and OCC continue to flag CRE valuation, refinancing, and certain credit risks as areas to monitor.
- Fed SLOOS commentary in 2026 described CRE standards as generally unchanged in some quarters but demand mixed by property and bank type.

Underwriting implication: do not size office debt from historical leverage norms. Size debt from tenant durability, rollover, cash flow after leasing costs, and lender exit confidence.

## Lender Lane Matrix

| Lender Type | Best Fit | Likely Friction |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship bank | Local or regional sponsor, moderate leverage, relationship deposits, stabilized or lightly transitional asset | Office concentration limits, recourse, conservative debt yield |
| Life company | High-quality stabilized office, strong tenancy, low leverage, long-term hold | Low appetite for near-term rollover, vacancy, or heavy leasing capital |
| CMBS | Larger stabilized asset with predictable cash flow and clean structure | Office delinquency history, cash management, tenant concentration, reserve sizing |
| Debt fund / bridge | Transitional lease-up, recap, discounted basis, credible business plan | Higher coupon, extension risk, strict milestones, heavy reserves |
| SBA / owner-user | Owner-occupied office or mixed office operations | Occupancy requirements, business underwriting, not investor-office debt |
| Private credit / rescue capital | Distressed maturity, note purchase, preferred equity, gap capital | Cost of capital, control rights, intercreditor complexity |

## Core Sizing Tests

Use all of these. The tightest test controls.

| Test | Stabilized Office | Transitional Office |
|---|---|---|
| LTV | Often 50%-65% depending on quality and market | Lower initial leverage, sized to as-is value and reserves |
| DSCR | Stress at in-place NOI and stabilized NOI | Must survive downtime and leasing-cost drag |
| Debt yield | Use in-place NOI, not only stabilized NOI | Use current NOI and credible stabilized case |
| Rollover | Major expirations inside loan term reduce proceeds | Require reserves, holdbacks, or milestones |
| Tenant concentration | Top tenant and top 5 exposure matter | Single-tenant exposure may drive lender type |
| Leasing cost reserve | Required when vacancy or rollover is material | Often funded at close or controlled by lender |
| Exit cap / refinance test | Higher exit cap or lower value stress | Required before accepting bridge maturity risk |

## Office-Specific Credit Questions

Ask before sizing debt:

- What percentage of rent expires before maturity and each extension option?
- What tenants have contraction, termination, renewal, or purchase options?
- How much NOI is from signed-not-commenced leases?
- What is the cash cost to reach underwritten occupancy?
- How much vacancy is direct, sublease, shadow, or pending expiration?
- Is rent above or below market after concessions?
- Is the building prime, competitive, transitional, or stranded?
- What capex is needed for elevators, HVAC, facade, lobby, security, amenities, and code compliance?
- Does the borrower have liquidity to fund TI/LC overruns?
- Are taxes and insurance likely to reset?

---

## Red Flags

- Debt sized to stabilized NOI while in-place NOI fails coverage.
- Major tenant expires before loan maturity without reserve or signed renewal.
- Refinance plan assumes cap-rate compression and lower rates without a backup.
- Loan proceeds depend on conversion value not yet entitled, designed, or financed.
- Borrower cannot fund leasing-cost overruns.
- CMBS execution assumed for an asset with tenant or rollover story that requires active lender flexibility.
- Bank loan assumes local lender appetite despite office concentration limits.
