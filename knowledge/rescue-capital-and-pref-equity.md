# Rescue Capital and Preferred Equity

Reference guide for comparing preferred equity, mezzanine debt, JV equity, note purchase, and other rescue-capital solutions for CRE refinance gaps and recapitalizations.

---

## Rescue Capital Definition

Rescue capital is new capital injected into a stressed or constrained asset to preserve value, cure a maturity gap, fund stabilization, or avoid a forced sale or default. It is usually expensive because it enters when timing, control, and downside risk are already impaired.

Common triggers:

- Existing loan cannot be refinanced at par
- Asset has leasing, capex, or operating deficits
- Sponsor cannot fund required paydown or reserves alone
- Lender requires fresh equity for extension or modification
- Sale is possible but timing would destroy value

---

## Capital Types

| Capital Type | Position | Typical Use | Main Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor equity | Common equity | Paydown, reserves, show commitment | Concentration and liquidity drain |
| Third-party common equity | Common equity or JV | Large gap and reset ownership | Dilution and control sharing |
| Preferred equity | Equity senior to common | Gap capital behind senior debt | Preferred return, governance, redemption |
| Mezzanine debt | Debt secured by equity pledge | Gap capital where senior permits | Intercreditor limits and foreclosure rights |
| B-note / subordinate note | Debt behind A note | Split supportable and impaired debt | Complex control and valuation |
| Debt fund bridge | Senior or stretch senior | Transitional takeout | Rate, fees, reserves, recourse |
| Note purchase | Acquisition of existing debt | Control debt at discount | Execution risk and lender sale process |
| Discounted payoff capital | Capital to pay lender at discount | Lender wants exit | Tax, release, funding certainty |

---

## Preferred Equity Issue Map

Preferred equity is not just "cheaper common equity." It can behave like debt through required returns, mandatory redemption, control triggers, and cure rights.

Evaluate:

- Preferred return, current pay vs accrual
- Minimum multiple
- Redemption date
- Waterfall priority
- Catch-up and participation rights
- Conversion rights
- Dilution triggers
- Major-decision consent rights
- Budget and capex approvals
- Reporting cadence
- Cash-management rights
- Control shift triggers
- Recognition agreement with senior lender
- Tax treatment
- Sponsor promote reset

## Mezzanine vs Preferred Equity

| Topic | Mezzanine Debt | Preferred Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Legal position | Loan secured by equity pledge | Equity interest in ownership stack |
| Senior lender consent | Intercreditor agreement | Recognition or consent agreement |
| Remedies | UCC foreclosure on equity pledge | Contractual control, sale, redemption, or dilution rights |
| Current pay burden | Often required | May be current, accrued, or mixed |
| Tax/accounting | Debt-like | Debt-like or equity-like depending on structure |
| Control rights | Through defaults and covenants | Through operating agreement and consent rights |
| Best use | Strong cash flow and senior consent path | Gap capital where subordinate debt is constrained |

## Comparison Criteria

Score each capital source on:

- Certainty of close
- Time to close
- Amount of gap covered
- Current cash-pay burden
- Total cost of capital
- Required senior-lender consent
- Governance or control loss
- Sponsor dilution
- Guaranty or recourse exposure
- Exit feasibility
- Compatibility with business plan
- Downside remedies

---

## Rescue Capital Fit Rules

- Use sponsor equity when the gap is modest and sponsor liquidity is available.
- Use third-party common equity when value needs a true reset and the sponsor accepts dilution.
- Use preferred equity when the asset is viable, senior debt remains in place, and the gap can be solved with priority economics plus governance rights.
- Use mezzanine debt only when current-pay burden and intercreditor mechanics are realistic.
- Use bridge debt when the senior loan itself must be replaced and the business plan has a credible takeout.
- Use note purchase or discounted payoff when the lender is willing to sell or settle and the borrower or new capital can fund.
- Recommend sale when new capital preserves optionality for the sponsor but not economic value for the full stack.

---

## Red Flags

- Rescue capital proceeds only cover paydown, not operating deficit or capex
- Preferred return accrues beyond realistic exit value
- Control triggers are tied to metrics the asset is already likely to miss
- Senior lender consent is assumed but not confirmed
- Sponsor dilution removes incentive to execute the plan
- Capital source requires a valuation that the market will not support
- Exit depends on a refinance at stronger terms than current stabilized metrics support
