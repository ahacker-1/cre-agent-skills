# Office Market and Flight-to-Quality Study

Evaluate an office asset's submarket, competitive set, demand drivers, and flight-to-quality position.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when you need to understand whether an office building can win tenants in its market. It is useful for acquisitions, refinancings, asset reviews, lease-up plans, and hold/sell decisions where market vacancy alone is too blunt.

---

## What You'll Need to Provide

- Property address and submarket
- Building class, vintage, rentable area, floorplate size, amenities, transit/parking profile, and recent capital improvements
- Current rent roll, vacancy, and asking rents if available
- Competitive set or broker market survey if available
- Recent leasing comps, sublease comps, and concessions if available
- Tenant industries or target tenant profile
- Any known conversion, redevelopment, or obsolete-building context

---

## Mission

Determine whether the asset is prime, competitive, transitional, or stranded, and explain what that means for lease-up, rent growth, concessions, and underwriting risk.

---

## Strategy

### Step 1: Define the Relevant Market

Identify:

- CBD, suburban, urban-edge, medical, government, innovation, or mixed-use context
- Direct competitive submarket
- Tenant decision geography
- Transit, commute, parking, and amenity drivers
- Any market-specific demand engines, such as finance, technology, legal, government, education, health care, or AI-related growth

### Step 2: Establish Market Regime

Use current market data to summarize:

- overall vacancy
- direct vacancy
- sublease availability
- Class A / prime vacancy
- net absorption
- leasing volume
- construction pipeline
- conversions or demolitions removing supply
- asking rent vs taking rent or net effective rent

State whether the market is improving, stable, bifurcated, or deteriorating.

### Step 3: Score Flight-to-Quality Position

Rate the asset on:

- location and commute quality
- building systems and reliability
- lobby, amenities, food/beverage, conference, fitness, outdoor space, and tenant experience
- sustainability, energy, and wellness expectations
- floorplate functionality and divisibility
- security, access control, elevators, loading, and after-hours operations
- ability to support hybrid-work return-to-office goals

### Step 4: Build Competitive Set

Compare against 5-10 direct competitors:

- asking rent
- taking rent or net effective rent if known
- vacancy and contiguous block availability
- concessions
- tenant improvements
- amenities and renovation status
- tenant wins/losses

Do not compare against generic Class A inventory if tenants would not actually cross-shop the building.

### Step 5: Identify Demand and Obsolescence Risks

Flag:

- shadow vacancy
- large sublease blocks
- tenants downsizing on renewal
- obsolete floorplates
- under-amenitized buildings
- deferred systems capital
- weak parking/transit fit
- conversion feasibility claims not backed by design and zoning work

### Step 6: Conclude Underwriting Implications

Translate findings into:

- market rent view
- downtime assumption
- concession pressure
- renewal probability
- exit liquidity
- lender perception
- recommended diligence questions

## Quality Checks

- Uses submarket and quality-tier data, not only national office averages
- Separates prime vacancy from overall vacancy when data is available
- Identifies whether sublease space is direct competition
- Explains why the competitive set is relevant
- Translates market findings into underwriting assumptions
- Does not call a building "Class A" without testing actual tenant decision factors

---

## Red Flags & Dealbreakers

- No evidence of tenant demand for the building's quality tier
- Major vacancy competes directly with cheaper sublease space
- Asset requires capital to be competitive but underwriting treats it as stabilized
- Market rent growth is assumed despite widening concessions
- Conversion or redevelopment value is asserted without zoning, floorplate, cost, and financing support

---

## When Data is Missing

- If no broker survey is provided, use current public market reports and mark assumptions as directional
- If no competitive set is provided, build a proposed set from location, class, size, and tenant profile
- If concessions are unavailable, state that headline rents may overstate economics and reduce confidence
- If sublease data is unavailable, flag the analysis as incomplete for tenant decision-making

---

## Confidence Scoring

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| HIGH | Current submarket data, competitive set, concessions, and tenant demand evidence are available |
| MEDIUM | Current market data is available, but concessions or comp-level tenant evidence is incomplete |
| LOW | Only national data or broker narrative is available; competitive set is inferred |
