# Office TI / LC Economics Research

## Purpose

- Supports `knowledge/office-ti-lc-economics.md`
- Intended users: office underwriters modeling leasing costs, free rent, downtime, and net effective rent

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States and U.S.-Canada cost references where U.S. data is included
- Deal type: office lease-up, renewal, second-generation leasing, and repositioning
- Market assumptions: local contractor and broker quotes control over national ranges

## Key Findings

- Fit-out cost guides vary materially by specification and methodology, so underwriting should use ranges.
- TI allowance, free rent, and LC are distinct economic concessions and should not be collapsed into rent.
- Leasing costs affect cash flow and lender reserves even when NOI appears stable.
- Public office REIT filings reinforce that tenant improvements and leasing commissions are recurring capital needs.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

- Use JLL and Cushman fit-out figures as order-of-magnitude checks, not as universal defaults.
- Require net effective rent and annual cash flow after leasing costs.
- Model renewal TI/LC separately from new-tenant TI/LC.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- JLL and Cushman cost guides differ because of scope and methodology. Use local contractor quotes where available.
- Market rent reports may show positive rent growth while net effective rents remain pressured by concessions.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Shell, medical, law firm, lab-office, and high-security tenants can exceed generic office fit-out ranges.
- Spec suites can reduce downtime but create tenant-specific capital risk.
- Tenant termination rights can destroy the payback period on large TI allowances.

## Open Questions

- Market-specific current TI and free-rent comps usually require broker input.
