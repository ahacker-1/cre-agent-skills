## Key Findings

- Lender appetite is improving for high-quality office, but office credit remains highly selective.
- CMBS office delinquency stress makes lender scrutiny on rollover and tenant quality more intense.
- In-place NOI, debt yield, and leasing-cost reserves can constrain proceeds before LTV does.
- Bank, life company, CMBS, debt fund, and rescue capital lanes each fit different office profiles.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

- Do not set one universal LTV default. Use 50%-65% as a directional range for financeable office, with lower leverage for transitional assets.
- Require both in-place and stabilized DSCR/debt yield.
- Require reserve analysis for TI, LC, downtime, and base-building capital.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- CBRE shows improving permanent loan LTVs, while Trepp and regulators show ongoing office credit stress. The knowledge base resolves this by differentiating high-quality stabilized assets from commodity/transitional assets.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Bridge loans can solve timing but create refinance risk if stabilization slips.
- CMBS may be a poor fit when asset management flexibility is required.
- Relationship bank appetite depends on sponsor, deposits, market, and office concentration.

## Open Questions

- Live lender quotes, spreads, reserves, and recourse requirements change quickly and must be refreshed per deal.
