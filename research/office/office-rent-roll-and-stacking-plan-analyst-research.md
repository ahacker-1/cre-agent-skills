## Key Findings

- Office occupancy must separate leased, physically occupied, signed-not-commenced, sublease, and direct vacancy.
- Floor-level fragmentation can impair lease-up even when total vacant RSF appears manageable.
- WALT alone can hide tenant concentration and option risk.
- Public filings often distinguish occupancy from leased percentage, supporting that distinction in the skill.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

- Require RSF-weighted WALT.
- Require annual rollover by RSF and rent.
- Require tenant concentration by tenant and parent where known.
- Reconcile rent roll, stacking plan, and building RSF.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Broker summaries may use leased occupancy; lender and buyer diligence should also ask for physical occupancy and signed-not-commenced detail.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Sublease availability can hide tenant distress.
- Large blocks matter differently than small-suite vacancy.
- Suite measurement conflicts can change rent and expense recoveries.

## Open Questions

- Shadow vacancy often requires tenant interviews or broker confirmation.
