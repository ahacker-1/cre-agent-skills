## Purpose

- Supports `skills/office/office-market-and-flight-to-quality-study.md`
- Intended users: CRE teams evaluating whether an office asset can compete for tenants

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States
- Deal type: office acquisition, refinance, asset review, lease-up, or disposition
- Market assumptions: local submarket data and broker feedback control over national averages

## Key Findings

- Office is recovering selectively, with quality and location driving performance.
- Prime buildings can tighten while the broader market remains oversupplied.
- Construction scarcity supports high-quality assets, but commodity vacancy can remain stranded.
- Flight-to-quality is an operating thesis only if the asset has the systems, amenities, location, and capital plan to win tenants.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

- Require prime/quality-tier analysis in addition to overall market vacancy.
- Treat sublease inventory as a direct competitive factor.
- Use asset-tier verdicts: PRIME, COMPETITIVE, TRANSITIONAL, STRANDED.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Different publishers report different vacancy levels. The skill should use the most local and asset-relevant source and disclose the publisher.
- "Class A" is not enough; tenant decision criteria control the tier.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- A cheap basis can still be value destructive if tenant demand is absent.
- Conversions reduce supply but are not feasible for most buildings.
- High asking rents do not prove high net effective rents.

## Open Questions

- Broker-supplied tour velocity and proposal activity are often the best near-term demand evidence but are not public.
