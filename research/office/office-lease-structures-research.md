## Purpose

- Supports `knowledge/office-lease-structures.md`

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

## Key Findings

- Office leases commonly use full-service, modified gross, base-year, expense-stop, or NNN structures.
- The base year and expense exclusions determine whether rising taxes, insurance, utilities, and security costs are recoverable.
- Measurement standard matters because rents are usually charged on rentable square feet.
- Work letters and tenant improvement provisions are not ancillary; they can control rent commencement and cash obligations.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

- Require explicit classification of recovery structure in all lease abstracts.
- Include BOMA/area reconciliation as a standard office diligence item.
- Treat options, contractions, terminations, and assignment/sublease rights as valuation issues, not just legal issues.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Legal commentary explains concepts, but actual lease language controls.
- BOMA provides measurement standards but leases may lock different area numbers. The lease governs economics unless amended.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Mixed-use buildings require allocation by use.
- Base-year leases with abnormal first-year expenses can distort future recoveries.
- Tenant work letters can create hidden landlord capital obligations.

## Open Questions

- State-specific remedies, lien treatment, and enforceability questions require counsel.
