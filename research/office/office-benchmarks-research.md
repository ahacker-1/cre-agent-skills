# Office Benchmarks Research

## Purpose

- Supports `knowledge/office-benchmarks.md`
- Intended users: investors, lenders, brokers, and operators evaluating U.S. office assets

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States office markets
- Deal type: investor-owned office, including CBD, suburban, multi-tenant, and single-tenant assets
- Legal and market assumptions: local market data controls over national averages; lease interpretation requires counsel

## Key Findings

- National vacancy remains elevated, but prime vacancy is tighter and improving faster.
- New office supply is limited, so demand recovery can affect prime availability quickly.
- Sublease inventory is an important leading indicator because it competes with direct space.
- Commodity office should not receive the same assumptions as prime or heavily renovated assets.
- Conversions and demolitions can support market recovery, but conversion feasibility is narrow and property-specific.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

- Default to submarket, class, and quality-tier benchmarks when available.
- Treat national vacancy as context only.
- Use a four-tier asset quality framework: prime, competitive Class A, transitional, stranded.
- Require separate underwriting for downtime, concessions, and TI/LC whenever vacancy or rollover is material.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- CBRE, JLL, Cushman, and Colliers differ on vacancy levels because of data definitions and coverage. Use the user's broker survey or the most relevant local source for a specific deal.
- Prime and Class A definitions vary by publisher. The skill uses "prime" as a functional tenant-decision category, not just a label.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- CBD recovery can coexist with suburban weakness, and vice versa.
- A building can be Class A by vintage but still fail tenant decision criteria.
- Conversion value should not be treated as downside value without floorplate, zoning, cost, and financing support.

## Open Questions

- Market-specific concession data is often proprietary and must be supplied by brokers.
- Office utilization data by tenant is usually unavailable without tenant cooperation.
