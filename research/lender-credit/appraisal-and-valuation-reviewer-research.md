# Appraisal and Valuation Reviewer Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/lender-credit/appraisal-and-valuation-reviewer.md`
- Pairs with `knowledge/credit-memo-and-appraisal-review-standards.md` and `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`
- Intended users: bank and credit union appraisal review staff, credit analysts, underwriters, credit officers, loan review, and special assets officers who must accept, condition, or reject a valuation before a credit decision

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States
- Institution type: federally insured depositories and their subsidiaries subject to the federal banking agencies' appraisal regulations (12 CFR 34 subpart C for national banks, with parallel FRB, FDIC, and NCUA rules). Debt funds, life companies, and CMBS originators are not bound by these rules but commonly adopt the same review architecture, and the credit committee questions are the same either way
- Deal type: income-producing commercial real estate and acquisition, development, and construction (ADC) lending, including going concern assets and for-sale residential where the lender takes CRE collateral
- Legal assumptions: this research supports educational decision support. It is not legal, tax, investment, accounting, appraisal, or financing advice. The institution's own loan policy, its primary regulator, and its counsel control
- A lender-side reviewer who is not a state certified or licensed appraiser is performing a compliance and reasonableness review, not a USPAP appraisal review, and cannot produce a second opinion of value

## Key Findings

### The regulatory gate comes before the reasonableness review

- Transaction value is the amount of the loan or extension of credit (12 CFR 34.42(o)), not the property value. The reviewer sizes the requirement off that number.
- The OCC handbook lists the four situations where an evaluation may substitute for an appraisal: loan amount of $500,000 or less; a business loan of $1 million or less where income from the sale or rental of real estate is not the primary repayment source; an existing extension of credit with no new money beyond reasonable closing costs; or an existing extension of credit with new money advanced but no obvious and material change in market conditions or the physical aspects of the property that would threaten collateral adequacy.
- A state certified appraiser is required for all transactions of $1 million or more, nonresidential transactions of $500,000 or more including one- to four-family construction loans, and complex residential transactions of $500,000 or more.
- The $500,000 CRE figure comes from the 2018 joint final rule. The 2010 Guidelines still print $250,000 in section XI; that number is superseded.
- Abundance of caution: a lien may be taken without an appraisal or evaluation only if the credit is well supported by the borrower's cash flow or other collateral, the lender documents the adequacy and reliability of those repayment sources, and concludes that knowing the real estate's market value is unnecessary to the credit decision (12 CFR 34.43(a)(2) as described in the handbook). It does not apply if the transaction would not be adequately secured without the real estate.
- The 2018 FAQ (Q15) notes the appraisal regulations require an evaluation in certain loan workout situations, and that a lender may choose to obtain a new appraisal instead.
- The 2018 FAQ (Q14) draws the line the credit file has to respect: a lender may grant a conditional approval before obtaining a valuation, but the final credit decision or action should occur only after the lender receives, reviews, and accepts the appraisal or evaluation.

### Independence and engagement are testable file facts

- The lender or its agent must directly select and engage the appraiser. Written engagement letters should identify the client and the intended use and users as defined in USPAP, may state restrictions on sharing, and belong in the credit file. The appraiser should not begin work before being engaged.
- The Guidelines and the handbook list six prohibited communications: conveying a predetermined, expected, or qualifying value, loan amount, or target LTV; specifying a minimum value needed to approve the loan or as a condition of ordering; conditioning compensation on loan consummation; not compensating because the property did not value at a certain amount; implying retention depends on the value reached; and excluding a person from future engagement because a reported value missed a threshold. Asking the appraiser to consider additional information, provide additional support for a basis, or correct factual errors is permitted.
- Use of a borrower-ordered or borrower-provided appraisal violates the appraisal regulations. A borrower may tell the lender an appraisal exists; the lender then requests it directly from the financial services institution that commissioned it. FAQ Q16 confirms a developer may not order the appraisals and may not pick the appraiser off the lender's approved list.
- A transferred appraisal is usable only where the other financial services institution selected and engaged the appraiser, the appraiser had no direct or indirect interest in the property or parties, the lender determines the appraisal remains valid, conforms to the regulation, and is otherwise appropriate. The Guidelines add that a lender must not accept an appraisal readdressed or altered to conceal the original client, and should subject transferred work to at least the same review depth it applies to its own, documented in the credit file.
- A broker-ordered appraisal can be accepted where the broker qualifies as a financial services institution and the appraiser is independent, but FAQ Q19 tells lenders to review broker-ordered appraisals thoroughly.
- Staff appraisers can be independent: FAQ Q18 requires that they be independent of the loan production function, not involved in approving the transaction, and free of any direct or indirect interest. NCUA's 12 CFR 722.5 states the credit union version, requiring staff appraisers to be independent of the lending, investment, and collection functions and fee appraisers to be engaged directly by the credit union or its agent.
- USPAP's Ethics Rule requires the appraiser to disclose any services performed on the property, in any capacity, in the three years preceding acceptance of the assignment (FAQ Q17).
- Reviewers must be independent of the transaction, hold no direct or indirect interest, be insulated from loan production influence, and have education, expertise, and competence commensurate with the complexity of the transaction, the property type, and the market. In a small or rural institution the review may sit with the originating loan officer only if that officer abstains from directly or indirectly approving or voting to approve the loan.
- Depth of review is risk-focused. The Guidelines direct more comprehensive review of large-dollar credits, complex or specialized properties, and properties outside the institution's traditional lending market, and require the institution to set criteria for when to expand the review.

### Report content and scope tests

- 12 CFR 34.44 sets six minimum standards: conform to USPAP; be written with sufficient information and analysis to support the decision; be subject to appropriate review for USPAP compliance; analyze and report appropriate deductions and discounts; use the regulatory definition of market value; and be performed by a state licensed or certified appraiser.
- The appraisal must contain an opinion of market value as defined in 12 CFR 34.42(h): the most probable price in a competitive and open market under conditions requisite to a fair sale, with typically motivated parties, reasonable exposure time, cash or comparable terms, and a price unaffected by special or creative financing or sales concessions.
- Going concern value, value in use, and special value to a specific user may not be used as market value for a federally related transaction. They may appear as separate, clearly identified opinions. An appraisal that produces an enterprise value must allocate it among the market value of the real estate, personal property, and intangibles. FF&E may qualify as other acceptable collateral for the supervisory LTV calculation if it is secured by a perfected security interest, has a quantifiable value, and is appropriately discounted in the appraisal; business enterprise value does not qualify.
- The result of an automated valuation model, by itself or signed by an appraiser, is not an appraisal.
- Report option matters. The Guidelines state that a report option restricted to a single client and intended user will generally not be appropriate to support most federally related transactions because it lacks sufficient supporting information and analysis for underwriting, though it may suit portfolio monitoring. A report that merely states, rather than summarizes or describes, may lack sufficient support.
- The report must disclose the research and analysis performed, and disclose research and analysis typically warranted for the type of appraisal but omitted, with the rationale for the omission. USPAP requires disclosure of whether the property was inspected and whether anyone provided significant assistance.
- Appendix E asks whether the appraisal includes the legal description, whether the as-is value is reported, whether the property description is accurate and complete (address, ownership interest, square footage, year built or anticipated completion, condition analysis, areas and dimensions with source, photos, construction quality and layout), whether the appraisal considered any current agreement of sale, option, or listing, and whether it reported sales of the property in the prior three years.
- Appendix E item 13 is the one that catches assumption abuse in a lender file: are the assumptions and limiting conditions consistent with the bank's intended collateral position in the transaction? Item 18 asks whether the assumptions are logical and supportable with market data. Together these are the review test for extraordinary assumptions and hypothetical conditions, which USPAP requires the appraiser to disclose. The Appraisal Institute's Guide Note 6 illustrates the distinction in the contamination setting: an extraordinary assumption is used where the appraiser relies on information such as a third-party report that, if false, could alter the opinion; a hypothetical condition is used where the appraiser values the property contrary to a known fact, such as valuing a known-contaminated property as if uncontaminated.

### The three approaches and the comparables

- USPAP requires the appraisal to include any approach to value that is applicable and necessary, the appraiser to disclose the rationale for omitting an approach, and the appraiser to analyze and reconcile the approaches to arrive at market value. The appraisal should also discuss market conditions including value trends, demand and supply factors, and exposure time, and should analyze sales history and highest and best use.
- The FDIC manual is the most usable regulator text on approach selection. The cost approach is particularly helpful when reviewing construction loan draws, becomes harder to apply as a property ages, and is usually inappropriate in a troubled market outside special purpose facilities because construction cost for a new facility normally exceeds the market value of comparable existing properties. The sales comparison approach requires comparability in market location, financing terms, property condition and use, timing, and transaction costs, and available commercial sales data is often insufficient to carry a conclusion. The income approach converts expected future net operating income into present value; direct capitalization fits stable markets with no unusual rent or occupancy pattern, while a discounted cash flow with an explicit stabilization period and a discounted reversion is more typical for troubled properties.
- The FDIC manual also states that where current market activity is dominated by a limited number of transactions or liquidation sales, the high capitalization and discount rates implied by those transactions should not be used; rates should reflect market conditions that are neither highly speculative nor depressed.
- Appendix E item 17 asks, where sales comparison is the primary approach, whether the comparables are truly comparable in property characteristics and location and whether the appraiser clearly discusses and supports the adjustments. Item 24 asks whether the units of comparison, such as price per square foot or price per unit, are consistent with those cited in the comparables; whether the capitalization or discount rate is supportable and reasonable for the class, property type, and market conditions; and whether cost exceeds or closely approximates value, in which case the worksheet asks what the borrower's and the bank's motivation to engage in the transaction is.
- Appraisal Institute Guide Note 11 addresses comparable selection in a declining market and Guide Note 12 addresses analyzing market trends; both are interpretive material for the adjustment and trend questions rather than hard rules.

### Income, rent, vacancy, and absorption testing

Appendix E item 19 is the operating-assumption checklist for an income-producing property, and it maps directly onto what a credit memo has to reconcile:

- analyze and report data on current revenues, expenses, and vacancies
- include appropriate analysis of discount and capitalization rates
- analyze tenant strength, tenant rollover risk, anticipated rents, and probability of lease renewals
- analyze terms of outstanding leases and NOI compared with budgets
- analyze marketing and other re-leasing costs
- indicate current and projected vacancy and absorption rates
- indicate effective rental rates or sales prices including any concessions
- discuss expertise of property management
- consider the effect of any planned or new construction or renovation coming into the market area

Item 21 requires an adequate explanation where the appraisal cites significant differences from past performance in lease rates, expenses, or absorption. Item 22 asks whether the site and improvements are suitable for the market and whether the property can sustain its historical cash flow, whether the reviewer can determine the subject's relative position within its submarket and among competing properties, whether the report addresses future supply and demand, and whether it anticipates the need for and expense of replacements or improvements.

### Value premises, deductions, and discounts

- The as-is market value reflects the property's current physical condition, use, and zoning as of the effective date. The prospective market value as completed reflects value at the time development is expected to be completed; the prospective market value as stabilized reflects value at the date the property is projected to achieve stabilized occupancy, defined as the occupancy a property is expected to achieve after exposure to the market for lease over a reasonable period at terms comparable to similar properties.
- Prospective market values must rest on current and reasonably expected market conditions, and the appraisal must give a point of reference to the market conditions and time frame on which the analysis is based. The Guidelines cite USPAP Statement 4 on prospective value opinions. The lender should understand the as-is value and should consider the prospective value that corresponds to the credit decision and the phase of the project being funded.
- Deductions and discounts are mandatory for proposed construction or renovation, partially leased buildings, non-market lease terms, and tract developments with unsold units. The standard exists, in the agencies' words, to avoid appraisals prepared with unrealistic assumptions and inappropriate methods.
- For proposed and partially leased rental developments the appraiser must deduct and discount to reflect that stabilized occupancy has not been achieved, and must consider absorption of the unleased space. Appropriate items include leasing commissions, rent loss, tenant improvements, and entrepreneurial profit where that profit is not already inside the discount rate.
- For non-market lease terms, the appraisal must state the ownership interest appraised and discuss the leases in place. Where the leased fee interest is appraised and contract rent is below market on one or more long-term leases to a highly rated tenant, the leased fee value is lower than the unencumbered fee simple value, and the leased fee value is the one to use.
- The sum of retail sales for a proposed development is not the market value of the property for purposes of the appraisal regulations.
- Raw land must be valued in its current condition and existing zoning. A developmental approach must reflect deductions and discounts for feasibility studies, permitting, engineering, holding costs, marketing costs, entrepreneurial profit, and other property-specific costs, and where both sales comparison and developmental approaches are supportable the report should reconcile them.
- Tract developments of five or more units, developed lots, attached or detached homes, and condominiums require deductions and discounts for holding costs, marketing costs, and entrepreneurial profit over the sales absorption period, with projected prices and absorption supported by anticipated demand at the time units are expected to be exposed for sale. Entrepreneurial profit may be a line item or a component of the discount rate, but the treatment must be explained.
- Narrow exception: where the lender can demonstrate through a feasibility study or market analysis obtained independently of the borrower and the lender that all units collateralizing the loan can be built and sold within 12 months, appraisals of the individual units may be used, and for LTV purposes the value is the lower of the sum of the individual appraised values (retail sellout) or the borrower's actual development and construction costs.
- Presold units may be excluded in determining whether a tract development appraisal is required, where the buyer has a binding contract, a substantial nonrefundable earnest money deposit, and written prequalification or a commitment for permanent financing.

### Validity, deficiencies, and the limit on reviewer authority

- Validity is a facts test, not a calendar test. The listed factors are passage of time, volatility of the local market, changes in terms and availability of financing, natural disasters, limited or over supply of competing properties, improvements to the subject or competing properties, lack of maintenance of the subject or competing properties, changes in underlying economic and market assumptions such as capitalization rates and lease terms, changes in zoning, building materials, or technology, and environmental contamination. An arbitrary period such as 12 months should not be the decisive criterion, and the lender must keep documentation of the facts and analysis supporting the conclusion that an existing valuation remains valid.
- The 2023 workout policy statement adds workout-specific triggers for a new or updated valuation: material deterioration in the performance of the project, in conditions for the geographic market and property type, in variances between actual conditions and original appraisal assumptions, and in project specifications. It also allows an evaluation prepared by the institution to serve where it appropriately updates the original appraisal assumptions to reflect current market conditions and provides a reasonable estimate of fair value.
- The same statement pairs the value premise with the strategy: where the institution will work with the borrower so the project achieves stabilized occupancy, it can consider the as-stabilized market value for risk grading after confirming the appraisal's assumptions and conclusions are reasonable.
- The statement also sets examiner posture on rate disputes: examiners generally are not expected to challenge underlying assumptions, including discount rates and capitalization rates, when those assumptions differ only marginally from norms generally associated with the collateral under review.
- Where a deficiency cannot be resolved with the appraiser, the lender must obtain an appraisal or evaluation meeting the regulatory requirements before making the credit decision.
- A reviewer cannot change the value conclusion in the original appraisal. An appraisal review performed by an appropriately qualified and competent state certified or licensed appraiser in accordance with USPAP may result in a second opinion of market value, and the lender may rely on that second opinion.
- USPAP Standards 3 and 4 govern development and reporting of an appraisal review; the Appraisal Institute's Standards of Valuation Practice mirror this with Standard B for review development and Standard C for reporting. The 2024 edition of USPAP took effect 2024-01-01 and has no stated end date.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults because they are fixed in regulation or agency guidance:

- Evaluation permitted in place of an appraisal at a transaction value of $500,000 or less; for a business loan of $1 million or less where real estate income is not the primary repayment source; and for the two existing-extension-of-credit cases.
- State certified appraiser required at $1 million or more, and for nonresidential transactions of $500,000 or more including one- to four-family construction.
- Tract development means a project of five units or more constructed or to be constructed as a single development.
- The six minimum appraisal standards of 12 CFR 34.44 and the eight-item evaluation content list of Appendix F.
- The regulatory market value definition, and the bar on using going concern value, value in use, or special value to a specific user as market value.
- Mandatory deductions and discounts for proposed construction or renovation, partially leased buildings, non-market lease terms, and tract developments with unsold units, and the specific line items the Guidelines name for each case.
- The 12-month independent feasibility study exception for unit-level appraisals, and the lower-of retail sellout or actual development and construction cost rule for LTV in that case.
- The rule that the sum of retail sales is not market value.
- The rule that a reviewer may not change the value conclusion, and that a second opinion of value requires a USPAP-compliant appraisal or appraisal review by a state certified or licensed appraiser.
- The rule that a final credit decision follows receipt, review, and acceptance of the valuation, while conditional approval may precede it.
- The rule that unresolved deficiencies require a compliant appraisal or evaluation before the credit decision.
- Supervisory LTV limits are not restated in this skill; they live in `knowledge/credit-memo-and-appraisal-review-standards.md` and the skill cites that file.

Case-by-case only, and stated as such in the skill:

- Any percentage tolerance for a variance between the appraisal's NOI, cap rate, vacancy, absorption, or TI and LC assumptions and the lender's underwriting. No agency source publishes one. Institutions set their own reconciliation triggers, and the skill asks the user for the institution's threshold rather than inventing one.
- What counts as a supportable capitalization or discount rate. The regulator test is internal consistency with the comparables cited and reasonableness for the class, property type, and market, not a fixed spread over any index.
- Whether a specific appraisal is stale. The factor test governs; a fixed interval is an internal policy convenience, not the standard.
- How much weight any single approach deserves. The FDIC manual frames this as market and data dependent.
- Whether an extraordinary assumption or hypothetical condition is acceptable. The test is whether it is disclosed, whether it is logical and supportable with market data, and whether it is consistent with the lender's intended collateral position, not a list of permitted assumptions.

Formulas: none are restated. DSCR, debt yield, LTV, cap rate, and stress mechanics are in `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`, and the skill cites that file rather than re-deriving them.

Directional market context, dated in the skill text where used:

- The OCC's Spring 2026 Semiannual Risk Perspective release dated 2026-05-07 states that credit conditions and refinancing risk in certain segments of CRE lending and private credit markets warrant ongoing monitoring. That is a reason to test exit and refinance assumptions in a prospective value, not a benchmark.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Threshold conflict: section XI of the 2010 Guidelines prints a $250,000 appraisal threshold. The 2018 joint final rule raised the CRE threshold to $500,000, and the 2022 OCC handbook prints $500,000. The later rule and later handbook control, and the skill uses $500,000 only.
- Reviewer authority conflict: practitioner material sometimes describes a lender review that adjusts or corrects an appraised value. The Guidelines are explicit that a reviewer cannot change the value conclusion, and that only a USPAP-compliant appraisal or appraisal review by a state certified or licensed appraiser can produce a second opinion. Regulator text controls, and the skill's output template forces the reviewer to choose between accepting, conditioning, returning to the appraiser, or ordering a new valuation.
- Aging conflict: many institutions use a fixed 12-month rule. The handbook and the Guidelines both say an arbitrary period should not be the decisive criterion. The skill runs the factor test first and treats a fixed interval as internal policy.
- Rate-challenge conflict: the Appendix E worksheet invites the reviewer to test whether the capitalization or discount rate is supportable, while the 2023 policy statement tells examiners not to challenge rate assumptions that differ only marginally from norms for the collateral. These are reconcilable and the skill treats them that way: document the test, escalate only a material and supported difference.
- Approach-weighting conflict: the FDIC manual favors the income approach, and specifically a discounted cash flow, for troubled and unique properties, while noting sales comparison generally carries the most weight when adequate sales data exists. Both agree the choice is data dependent, so the skill asks whether the omission or the weighting was explained rather than prescribing a hierarchy.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Going concern assets such as hotels, senior housing, marinas, and self-storage: the appraisal must allocate value among real property, personal property, and intangibles. An unallocated going concern number cannot serve as market value, and business enterprise value cannot support the supervisory LTV calculation.
- Owner-occupied and business loans: the $1 million business loan exemption can permit an evaluation, but the credit still needs an operating-business underwrite. The exemption is not a reason to skip collateral analysis.
- Transferred, participated, and purchased loans: appraisals from another financial services institution need the more thorough review the agencies call for, plus documentary confirmation that the other institution, not the borrower, engaged the appraiser. An appraisal readdressed or altered to conceal the original client must be refused.
- Construction and lease-up: as-complete and as-stabilized values should be tested against a funded leasing capital budget, signed leases or a preleasing record, and an absorption schedule supported by the feasibility study or market analysis in the appraisal. Deductions and discounts must appear; their absence on a partially leased building is a regulatory finding, not a style preference.
- For-sale and tract product: watch for a retail sellout presented as market value, an absorption schedule that never touches competing supply, and entrepreneurial profit that appears in neither a line item nor the discount rate.
- Workouts and modifications: match the value premise to the actual strategy. As-stabilized belongs with a genuine stabilization plan; as-is fair value less costs to sell belongs with a foreclosure path. Borrower-side mechanics are in `knowledge/workout-and-extension-structures.md` and the Capital Markets pack; this skill stays on the lender's valuation decision.
- Small and rural institutions: where absolute independence is unachievable, the compensating safeguards must be documented, and any loan officer who performs the review must abstain from approving or voting on the loan. Missing documentation of the safeguard is itself a finding.
- Evaluations: use the eight-item Appendix F content list. An evaluation resting on unsupported assumptions, such as assuming average condition or a zoning change, does not meet the standard.
- Restricted-format reports: these generally do not support a federally related transaction and should be reserved for portfolio monitoring.
- Environmental and physical conditions: where the appraisal relies on a third-party report through an extraordinary assumption, the underlying report has to be in the file and current, or the assumption is unsupported.
- Do not import the consumer AVM quality control rule into a CRE file; its scope is mortgages secured by a consumer's principal dwelling.

## Open Questions

- The full text of USPAP, including Standards 3 and 4, Statement 4 on prospective value opinions, and the definitions of extraordinary assumption and hypothetical condition, sits behind The Appraisal Foundation's paywall. This research relies on the agencies' incorporation of USPAP, the Foundation's public FAQ, and the Appraisal Institute's public descriptions rather than quoting the standard.
- The 2010 Guidelines have not been reissued as a consolidated document reflecting the 2018 threshold change; users should confirm the current issuance set with their primary regulator before relying on the 2010 section numbering in an examination context.
- No public agency source publishes a numeric tolerance for variance between an appraisal's assumptions and a lender's underwriting, so the skill asks the institution for its own reconciliation trigger.
- CBRE's H1 2026 U.S. Cap Rate Survey was not retrievable in this session (the publisher returned a 403), so no third-party cap rate benchmark is carried into the skill; market context is limited to the OCC release cited above.
- NCUA's transaction-value thresholds for member business loans sit in other sections of 12 CFR part 722 that were not retrieved in this session; credit union users should confirm the applicable threshold in their own rule rather than assuming the OCC figures apply verbatim.
