## Shared Source Themes

- Screening, sizing, and covenant work all trace back to the same five interagency underwriting standards - maximum loan amount, maturity, amortization, pricing structure, and LTV by property type - so a request or covenant outside any one of them is a policy exception, not a math problem.
- CRE concentration is measured against supervisory indicators, not a hard cap: the 2006 guidance's two screening ratios (construction/land at 100% of capital; total CRE at 300% of capital plus 50% growth) explicitly do not set a lending limit, and the denominator has been tier 1 capital plus ACL/ALLL since 2020, not total risk-based capital.
- Risk rating and classification run on one common interagency scale - pass, special mention, substandard, doubtful, loss - driven by well-defined weaknesses in repayment capacity rather than collateral value or payment record alone, and annual review, covenant monitoring, and credit memos all feed that same rating.
- Workouts, modifications, and policy exceptions are protected when they are documented, not when they succeed: the 2023 interagency workout statement and the exception-reporting rules in the lending guidelines both condition safe treatment on updated financials, current valuations, global cash flow analysis, and a written, board-visible justification.
