# Problem Loan and Modification Analyst Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/lender-credit/problem-loan-and-modification-analyst.md`
- Supplements `knowledge/regulatory-risk-rating-and-classification.md`, `knowledge/cre-concentration-and-stress-testing.md`, and `knowledge/lender-credit-policy-benchmarks.md`
- Intended users: bank and credit union special assets officers, credit officers, portfolio managers, loan review staff, and workout analysts on the lender side of a distressed U.S. CRE loan. Debt fund, life company, and CMBS credit staff can use the resolution comparison, but the classification, nonaccrual, and allowance sections are written to the interagency supervisory framework that governs insured depositories.

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States only. Foreclosure, receivership, redemption, anti-deficiency, and one-action rules are state law and vary materially; nothing here is a substitute for local counsel.
- Deal type: income-producing commercial real estate and acquisition, development, and construction loans held on balance sheet by a regulated depository, plus the securitized and non-bank analogues where noted.
- Legal, accounting, and regulatory assumptions: the institution has adopted ASC Topic 326 and ASU 2022-02; regulatory reporting follows the call report instructions; classification follows the interagency definitions. Institutions that have not adopted ASC 326 or ASU 2022-02 continue reporting troubled debt restructurings under the applicable instructions.
- Scope boundary: the borrower-side view of the same event lives in `skills/capital-markets/` and is referenced, not duplicated. Cash flow formulas are not restated; they belong to `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`.

## Key Findings

**The governing document is one policy statement, and it is permissive.** The interagency Policy Statement on Prudent Commercial Real Estate Loan Accommodations and Workouts, adopted June 29, 2023 and published at 88 FR 43115 on July 6, 2023, superseded the October 2009 statement, added a short-term accommodation section, and applies at all stages of the economic cycle to institutions of every size. It states plainly that institutions implementing prudent workout arrangements will not be subject to examiner criticism, even where the restructured loan carries weaknesses that produce an adverse classification. The precondition is documentation, not outcome.

**Accommodation and workout are different events.** The statement defines a short-term accommodation as an agreement to defer one or more payments, make a partial payment, forbear delinquent amounts, modify a loan, or otherwise assist a borrower experiencing financial challenge, generally temporary and occurring before a loan reaches a workout scenario. A workout is the longer-term arrangement: renewal or extension of terms, extension of additional credit, or a restructuring with or without concessions. Choosing the label matters because it drives the depth of file work expected.

**Six things must be in the file.** The statement requires that management has obtained updated and comprehensive financial information on the borrower, the real estate project, and all guarantors and sponsors; obtained current valuations of the collateral supporting the loan and the workout plan; analyzed global debt service coverage with realistic cash flow projections and the availability, continuity, and accessibility of repayment sources; analyzed available guarantor cash flow; demonstrated willingness and ability to monitor ongoing performance; maintained a risk rating system that accurately reflects the risk in the workout arrangement; and maintained a GAAP allowance methodology recognizing losses timely. The FDIC's Commercial Real Estate Loan Review examination module (Examination Modules 05/24) adds items an examiner will look for by name: experienced workout officers, appropriate legal documentation for changes, procedures for identifying and reporting modifications to borrowers experiencing financial difficulty, the ability to modify the plan when targets are missed or values decline, specific triggers for further downgrades, and where warranted a strategy to foreclose or move the credit out of the institution.

**Classification is driven by well-defined weaknesses, not by the collateral value or the payment record.** A modified loan to a borrower able to repay on reasonable terms is not adversely classified solely because collateral value fell below the balance. Conversely, a current loan whose collateral covers the balance is still classified when well-defined weaknesses jeopardize repayment. The FDIC module lists examples of those weaknesses in CRE terms: excess similar product under construction, analysis that does not reflect current market conditions, rent concessions pushing cash flow below expectations, concessions on tenant space, moving expenses and lease buyouts, slow leasing, delinquent lease payments from major tenants, tax arrearages, minimal borrower equity, additional advances to service an existing loan without credible repayment support, and renewals or extensions lacking credible repayment support and a reasonable repayment schedule.

**The collateral-dependent split is arithmetic once the value is set.** For a problem CRE loan whose repayment depends on the sale of the collateral, the portion adequately secured by the fair value of the real estate less costs to sell generally is classified no worse than substandard; the excess is classified loss; and doubtful applies only where full loss may be mitigated by pending events or the loss amount cannot reasonably be determined. The Rating Credit Risk booklet names the three common split ratings: substandard / doubtful / loss on a collateral-dependent loan across a value range, pass / adverse where part of the exposure is covered by a payment guarantee from a responsible, liquid guarantor or by liquid collateral, and the partial charge-off case.

**The A/B note structure is the most fully worked example in the guidance.** The policy statement's shopping mall example splits a $10 million loan against $7 million of collateral fair value into a $7 million Note A on market terms with 1.20x coverage, graded pass and returned to accrual after six months, and a $3 million Note B classified loss and charged off. The August 2026 Bank Accounting Advisory Series states the conditions for returning Note A to accrual: the partial charge-off is supported by a good faith credit evaluation and recorded before or at the time of modification, ultimate collectibility of all amounts contractually due on Note A is not in doubt, and there is a period of satisfactory payment performance either immediately before or after the modification. If any condition fails, or the modification lacks economic substance, the loan stays on nonaccrual.

**The six-month performance period is a norm, not a rule.** The policy statement says a sustained period of repayment performance generally would be a minimum of six months involving payments of cash or cash equivalents, and that performance before the restructuring may be counted. The Bank Accounting Advisory Series is more explicit: neither ASC 942-310-35 nor regulatory policy specifies a minimum period; six months is the general expectation for a monthly amortizing loan. It also carves out a rare exception where the modification coincides with an event showing significant improvement in the borrower's ability to repay, naming substantial new leases in a troubled real estate project, significant new revenue sources, and significant new equity from a source not financed by the bank.

**Nonaccrual turns on waiver versus deferral and on the well secured / in process of collection test.** A bank does not accrue interest where the asset is maintained on a cash basis because of borrower deterioration, where payment in full of principal or interest is not expected, or where principal or interest has been in default 90 days or more, unless the asset is both well secured and in the process of collection. The CRE Lending handbook defines well secured as collateral with realizable value sufficient to discharge the debt including accrued interest, or the guarantee of a financially responsible party, and in process of collection as collection proceeding in due course through legal action or, in appropriate circumstances, non-legal efforts reasonably expected to restore the loan to current in the near future. The Bank Accounting Advisory Series adds that a waiver of interest or principal generally puts a commercial loan on nonaccrual, while a deferral without waiver is a judgment call, and that a bankruptcy filing that accelerates maturity under the default provisions can require nonaccrual even while adequate-protection interest payments are being received.

**Modification reporting has four triggers and a cumulative test.** Under ASC 326 with ASU 2022-02 adopted, the reportable event is a modification to a borrower experiencing financial difficulty in one or more of four forms: principal forgiveness, an interest rate reduction, an other-than-insignificant payment delay, and a term extension. Financial difficulty indicators include default or foreseeable default absent modification, bankruptcy, going-concern doubt, insufficient cash flow to service the debt, and inability to obtain funds elsewhere at a market rate. Two traps appear in the Bank Accounting Advisory Series examples: a modification of a contractually current loan is still reportable if it meets the disclosure criteria, and a below-market renewal at or after maturity does not escape reporting merely because the institution waited for the maturity date. A short-term delay is not automatically insignificant; the test weighs the delayed amount against unpaid principal or collateral value and the delay period against payment frequency, original contractual maturity, or original expected duration, considering the cumulative effect of modifications in the prior 12 months.

**The allowance question is separate from the classification question.** A loan is collateral-dependent under ASC 326-20-35-5 when repayment is expected substantially through operation or sale of the collateral and the borrower is experiencing financial difficulty at the reporting date. For regulatory reporting the ACL on such a loan uses the collateral's fair value less estimated costs to sell where applicable, even where foreclosure is not probable, measured as of the reporting date without adjusting the appraised value for expected future changes in value. When the borrower is no longer experiencing financial difficulty the loan stops being collateral-dependent and the collateral method no longer applies. For performing loans, the allowance does not necessarily increase solely because collateral value fell below the balance.

**Valuation is the input everything else depends on, and the trigger list is published.** The policy statement directs an institution to consider a new appraisal or evaluation on material deterioration in project performance, conditions in the geographic market and property type, variances between actual conditions and original appraisal assumptions, changes in project specifications, loss of a significant lease or take-out commitment, and increases in pre-sale fallout. A new appraisal may be unnecessary where an institution-prepared evaluation appropriately updates the original appraisal's assumptions to current market conditions and yields a reasonable fair value estimate, but where new money is advanced the appraisal regulations control. Under 12 CFR 34.43 the CRE appraisal threshold is $500,000, a business loan of $1 million or less not dependent on real estate as the primary repayment source is exempt, and a renewal or refinancing is exempt where either there has been no obvious and material change in market conditions or physical aspects of the property threatening collateral protection, or there is no advancement of new monies beyond funds necessary to cover reasonable closing costs.

**Which value to use depends on the chosen path, and this is the single most common analytical error.** The policy statement says institutions typically use the market value conclusion corresponding to the workout plan objective and the loan commitment: if the plan is to work with the borrower toward stabilized occupancy, the as-stabilized market value may be considered for credit risk grading after confirming the appraisal's assumptions and conclusions are reasonable; if the institution intends to foreclose, financial reporting requires the fair value less costs to sell of the property in its current as-is condition. Choosing the path therefore changes the number that drives classification, charge-off, and allowance.

**Foreclosure changes the accounting basis, not just the timeline.** On receipt of the real estate, OREO is recorded at fair value less estimated costs to sell, the remaining loan balance is derecognized, and the excess of loan cost basis over that amount is a loss charged to the ACL. That fair value less costs to sell becomes the new cost basis; subsequent holding-period declines run through a property-specific valuation allowance, and unallocated valuation allowances are not acceptable. For collateral other than residential real estate, the loan is reclassified at the earlier of physical possession or legal title, and physical possession means actual custody or control rather than the residential legal test. Workout expenses such as legal fees, appraisal and evaluation fees, consulting fees, and collateral liquidation costs must be captured and accounted for properly and not charged to the ACL.

**Structural traps the guidance flags in CRE specifically.** The CRE Lending handbook treats re-amortizing a maturing balance back over the original period as a decision that diverts cash flow from the bank to the borrower and requires a stated rationale in the credit approval document. It also says an amortization schedule appropriate for a slow-but-selling project with a re-margining guarantor is not appropriate for a fully stalled project with no guarantor able to supplement principal, where the workout terms should resemble raw land terms. The FDIC module names additional advances to service an existing loan without credible repayment support as a classification trigger in its own right.

**Market context as of the access date, directional and time-sensitive.** FDIC's Quarterly Banking Profile for Q2 2026, released August 25, 2026, reported industry past-due and nonaccrual at 1.44% (down 9 basis points), nonfarm nonresidential CRE at 1.52% (down 14 basis points, the largest quarterly dollar decline of any category), the nonfarm nonresidential noncurrent rate at 1.23%, reserve coverage of noncurrent loans at 172.7%, and a net charge-off rate of 0.57%. The OCC's Spring 2026 Semiannual Risk Perspective (data as of December 31, 2025) reported that CRE headwinds have not produced significant credit defaults, that refinance risk merits continued attention as loans originated at lower rates mature, that office net absorption turned positive in the second half of 2025, and that certain banks continue to work through legacy problem loans while banks over $250 billion in assets took additional office charge-offs. Trepp data reported July 27, 2026 put June 2026 CMBS delinquency at 7.35% overall and 11.57% for office. Bank and CMBS populations and definitions are not comparable; a securitized office book behaves nothing like a bank nonfarm nonresidential book, and the gap is definitional as much as it is credit.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults, because each traces to a primary source:

- The four modification forms (principal forgiveness, interest rate reduction, other-than-insignificant payment delay, term extension) as the reporting trigger test.
- The collateral-dependent classification split: substandard up to fair value less costs to sell, loss above it, doubtful only for the pending-event or indeterminate-amount case.
- The three conditions for returning an A note to accrual, and the general six-month sustained performance expectation with pre-modification performance countable.
- The nonaccrual test (cash basis due to deterioration, payment in full not expected, or 90 days in default unless well secured and in process of collection) and the waiver-versus-deferral distinction.
- The six workout-plan elements from the 2023 policy statement plus the FDIC examination module additions, as the file completeness checklist.
- The valuation re-order trigger list and the rule that the value conclusion used must correspond to the chosen path (as-stabilized for a work-with plan; as-is fair value less costs to sell for a foreclosure plan).
- The 12 CFR 34.43 exemption tests for whether a workout requires a new appraisal, including the no-new-money and no-obvious-and-material-change alternatives.
- OREO initial measurement at fair value less costs to sell with derecognition of the loan and the loss charged to the ACL, and the prohibition on charging workout expenses to the ACL.

Case-by-case only, no default published:

- Any recovery percentage, timeline, or cost estimate by resolution path. No regulator or standards body publishes expected recovery by workout type; foreclosure duration, receivership availability and cost, redemption rights, and deficiency exposure are state-specific and must be sourced from counsel and the institution's own history.
- Discount levels on a discounted payoff or note sale. These are negotiated and market-driven; the analytical anchor is net present value against the alternatives, not a benchmark discount.
- The size of a required principal curtailment, re-margin, or new equity contribution. The guidance requires that the plan address curtailment and re-margining, not that it hit a number.
- Any specific downgrade trigger levels. The FDIC module requires that specific triggers exist; the levels are institution-set.
- Costs to sell as a percentage of value. Sourced from the appraisal, broker input, or institutional experience, not from a default.

Analytical convention adopted for the skill, stated as a convention rather than a benchmark: compare paths on net present value of expected recovery, not gross recovery, because time to resolution differs by years across the menu and the carrying cost, workout expense, and nonaccrual interest foregone differ with it.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- **Appraisal threshold.** The 2010 Interagency Appraisal and Evaluation Guidelines reference a $250,000 threshold. That figure was superseded for commercial real estate transactions; 12 CFR 34.43 currently states $500,000. The current CFR text controls, and the 2010 guidelines remain authoritative on validity criteria, evaluation content, and independence, which the threshold change did not disturb.
- **TDR versus modification reporting.** The March 2022 CRE Lending handbook still describes troubled debt restructuring reporting and refers to a then-proposed FASB update. ASU 2022-02 has since been adopted and the 2023 policy statement removed TDR discussion from its examples. The 2023 statement and the August 2026 Bank Accounting Advisory Series control for institutions that have adopted ASC 326 and ASU 2022-02; institutions that have not continue under the TDR instructions. The skill states both.
- **Return-to-accrual period.** The policy statement says "generally would be a minimum of six months." The Bank Accounting Advisory Series says no minimum period is specified in the accounting or regulatory literature and that six months is the general case for a monthly amortizing loan, with a narrow exception for a coinciding event of significant improvement. The accounting interpretation is the more precise statement of the same rule; the skill presents six months as the expectation and the exception as narrow and evidence-heavy.
- **Bank versus CMBS delinquency levels.** FDIC's 1.52% nonfarm nonresidential PDNA and Trepp's 11.57% office CMBS delinquency describe different populations under different definitions. Neither is wrong; neither is a proxy for the other. Both are cited with their scope stated.
- **Vintage of the Rating Credit Risk booklet.** The April 2001 booklet predates CECL and cites FAS 114 for impairment. Its classification definitions, split-rating mechanics, and guarantee analysis remain the current supervisory articulation and are used; its impairment measurement references are superseded by ASC 326 and the Bank Accounting Advisory Series.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- **Non-bank lenders.** Debt funds, life companies, mortgage REITs, and CMBS trusts are not subject to the interagency classification, nonaccrual, or call report framework. Applying substandard / doubtful / loss vocabulary or nonaccrual mechanics to them is a category error. CMBS resolution runs through the pooling and servicing agreement, the special servicer's servicing standard, and the controlling class, which belongs to `knowledge/cmbs-servicing-and-default-playbook.md`.
- **Participations and syndications.** Intercreditor and participation agreements can restrict unilateral modification, and the lead's classification does not automatically bind the participant. Confirm voting thresholds and independent rating obligations before assuming a path is available.
- **Guarantor-dependent paths.** A guarantee only supports the rating where the guarantor has demonstrated financial ability, willingness, and incentive and the guarantee is legally enforceable. Willingness may be presumed until support becomes necessary; at that point it must be demonstrated. A guarantor litigating or protracting renegotiation reduces collectibility rather than improving it.
- **Bankruptcy.** Filing can accelerate maturity under the loan's default provisions, drive nonaccrual even while adequate-protection payments are received, and remove the lender's control of the timeline. Automatic stay, cash collateral, plan confirmation, and cramdown are legal questions for counsel, not analytical ones.
- **Environmental exposure.** The CRE Lending handbook warns that foreclosure can affect the secured creditor exemption analysis and that expert advice is appropriate before foreclosing on potentially contaminated property. Environmental exposure can make foreclosure the worst path even where it produces the highest gross recovery.
- **Interest reserves and serial extensions.** A loan that is contractually current only because an interest reserve or another extension is funding the payments while the project has stalled is a classification event, not a performing loan. Repacking a depleted interest reserve with new debt is a recognized deterioration signal.
- **Re-amortization at renewal.** Re-amortizing a maturing balance over the original period lowers the payment and diverts cash flow to the borrower. Supportable in some cases, but the rationale belongs in the approval document, and it is treated in supervisory guidance as a possible indicator of a troubled credit rather than a neutral structuring choice.
- **Value selection.** Using an as-stabilized value to support a classification while the actual plan is liquidation, or an as-is liquidation value while the plan is a funded lease-up, invalidates the analysis in both directions.
- **Deferred recognition.** Waiting for the maturity date to grant a below-market renewal does not exempt the modification from reporting, and doubtful ratings that persist across quarters without the pending event resolving are a supervisory finding.

## Open Questions

- Expected recovery, cost, and duration by resolution path are not published by any primary source and vary by state, property type, and lien position. The skill therefore requires the user to supply institutional experience or counsel estimates rather than offering a default table.
- Receivership availability, powers, and cost differ materially by state and by whether the loan documents contain a consent-to-receiver provision; no national standard exists and none is asserted.
- Note sale and discounted payoff pricing depends on the depth of the loan-sale bid market at the time, which is not a fixed benchmark and was not researched for a current level here.
- Credit union treatment under NCUA rules, and the interaction with 12 CFR part 723, was not researched in depth; the NCUA joined the 2023 policy statement, but member business loan specifics should be confirmed separately.
- Tax consequences of debt forgiveness, deed-in-lieu, and short sale to the borrower affect whether a path is acceptable to the counterparty. Out of scope here and a question for the borrower's tax advisor.
