# Loan Request Screening and Sizing Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/lender-credit/loan-request-screening-and-sizing.md`
- Pairs with `knowledge/lender-credit-policy-benchmarks.md` and `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`
- Intended users: bank and credit union credit analysts, underwriters, and credit officers; debt fund, life company, agency (DUS / Optigo) and CMBS originations and credit staff who take in a loan request and have to decide, quickly and defensibly, whether it fits the institution's product set and policy, what proceeds each sizing test supports, which test controls, and what structure would have to be added before the request can be advanced

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States only. Supervisory loan-to-value limits, HVCRE capital treatment, appraisal thresholds, the CRE concentration screening criteria, and the agency and FHA program grids are U.S. federal constructs with no direct offshore analogue.
- Deal type: income-producing commercial real estate (multifamily, office, retail, industrial, hospitality) plus transitional and lightly repositioned assets. Construction and land development appear only where sizing frames (LTC, HVCRE, interest reserves) bear on a screening decision; full ADC underwriting is out of scope.
- Legal and market assumptions: this is educational decision support, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Regulatory citations describe supervisory expectations and capital rules, not any single institution's board-approved credit policy. The institution's own limits, delegated authorities, and exception process control over anything in this note.
- Formula definitions (NOI, DSCR, debt yield, LTV, LTC, debt constant, break-even occupancy) are not restated here or in the skill; they live in `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`.
- Time-sensitive market figures (leverage, coverage, spreads, sentiment) are directional as of the access date and must be re-verified against live quotes before use.

## Key Findings

### Screening is a policy-fit question before it is a math question

- The interagency guidelines require underwriting standards that address at a minimum the maximum loan amount by property type, maximum loan maturities by property type, amortization schedules, pricing structure by loan type, and LTV limits by property type. A request that falls outside any of those five is a policy exception at intake, not at approval.
- FDIC Section 3.2 lists the written loan policy contents an examiner expects, including the lending authority of each loan officer and of the loan or executive committee, guidelines for rates and repayment terms, limitations on the amount advanced against collateral value with required documentation by collateral type, guidelines for obtaining and reviewing appraisals and for ordering reappraisals, credit file maintenance, limits on maximum loan volume relative to total assets, and a defined trade area with the circumstances under which the institution lends outside it. Trade area and approval authority are screen-stage facts, not closing-stage facts.
- The 2006 concentration guidance defines a CRE loan for concentration purposes as one where 50% or more of the source of repayment comes from third-party, non-affiliated rental income, or from sale, refinancing, or permanent financing proceeds, and excludes owner-occupied properties. That definitional line decides at intake whether the request lands in the concentration bucket at all.
- The two screening criteria are construction, land development and other land at 100% or more of total capital, and multifamily plus nonfarm nonresidential plus construction and land development at 300% or more of total capital. Whether the institution is at or approaching them changes what the same request is worth.

### The controlling test is the output, not the loan amount

- HUD states the sizing rule in the clearest published form: the maximum insured loan is the lesser of the requested mortgage amount, the amount allowed by statutory limits, the amount supportable by the applicable debt service coverage ratios, and the amount supportable by the applicable loan ratios (Mortgagee Letter 2025-03). Every lender type runs a version of that "lesser of" test even when it is not published.
- The OCC handbook treats DSCR, debt yield, and LTV as complementary rather than substitutable. Debt yield is described as a measure of risk independent of interest rate, amortization period, and capitalization rate, useful for quickly sizing up a loan, especially where low rates and cap rates make DSCR- and LTV-derived proceeds prudent only as long as that rate environment holds. The handbook is explicit that debt yield should be considered along with other criteria and supported by prudent DSCR and LTV ratios.
- DSCR selection is judgment: the handbook says a lower DSCR may be a prudent trade-off for a shorter amortization period or for stable cash flows such as long-term net leases to highly creditworthy tenants, while volatile cash flows such as hotels or owner-occupants with uneven earnings may warrant a higher ratio.
- The same logic applies to LTV, which the handbook ties to the same criteria as amortization: less volatile cash flow and value can merit higher LTV, higher-risk properties should mitigate with more equity, and supervisory LTV limits do not establish a safe harbor.

### Published grids give the screen a spine

- Freddie Mac Optigo fixed-rate (product sheet dated 4/26): minimum 1.25x amortizing DCR at every term; maximum LTV 75% for 5-year and sub-7-year terms and 80% for 7-year and longer terms on amortizing and partial-IO loans; full-term interest-only capped at 65% LTV for terms of 7 years or less and 70% for longer terms, still at 1.25x amortizing DCR. Maximum combined maturity LTV for partial-IO loans is 70%. Minimum loan amount $10 million, maximum amortization 30 years, non-recourse except standard carve-outs, tax and insurance escrow and replacement reserve deposit generally required. No refinance test is required where the amortizing DCR is 1.40x or greater and LTV is 60% or less.
- Fannie Mae computes underwritten DSCR on a level debt service payment including amortization at the greater of the actual note rate or the required underwriting interest rate floor, and uses that same level payment regardless of the length of any interest-only period. Its exit test estimates the balance at maturity using 30-year (or product) amortization and prices the refinance at the minimum Tier 2 DSCR and maximum Tier 2 LTV, with a target reversion cap rate at least 2.0% above the initial cap rate and a refinance interest rate at least 2.25% above the current 10-year amortizing nationwide underwriting floor.
- FHA, per Mortgagee Letter 2025-03 (effective 2025-01-08 for applications not yet at initial endorsement): market rate (or LIHTC without rent advantage) 221(d)(4) and 223(f) at 87% LTV/LTC and 1.15 DSCR with a 7% vacancy factor; affordable (LIHTC with rent advantage to market) at 90% and 1.11 with a 5% vacancy factor; properties with rental assistance on 90% or more of units at 90% and 1.11 with a 3% vacancy factor. Cash-out refinance remains governed by the separate Criteria 10 ratio.
- Bank and life company minimums are institution-set; no regulator publishes them. The market average is a calibration point, not a policy: CBRE reported Q2 2026 averages of 59.6% LTV on commercial loans, 63.3% on multifamily, 1.43 DSCR, 10.2% debt yield, and a 5.7% coupon, with spreads of 204 bps commercial and 162 bps multifamily (published 2026-08-03).

### Structure is part of the screen, not a later negotiation

- Tenor: the handbook warns that longer tenors can prevent the bank from requiring additional equity or restructuring when performance declines, and notes that interest-only tenors are typically shorter than amortizing CRE loans, usually three to five years maximum.
- Amortization: no regulatory maximum exists, but prudent lenders generally treat 30 years as a reasonable maximum for income-producing CRE, with a 15 to 30 year range appropriate in most cases: stabilized multifamily at the high end (up to 30), hotels at the low end (generally not more than 20), and office, retail, and industrial in the middle (generally 25).
- Interest-only: even where the loan permits interest-only payments, the property should meet the bank's debt service coverage requirement as though the loan were amortizing, and LTV and as-if-amortizing DSCR requirements for IO loans are generally more conservative than for amortizing loans. The handbook's own mitigant is arithmetic: on a $1 million, five-year loan at 6% with a 25-year amortization, the maturity balance would be $899,000, so originating at $899,000 offsets the loss-given-default risk of the IO structure. A renewal, refinancing, or extension on an interest-only basis can indicate a troubled loan, and IO periods should generally be limited to construction or stabilization periods when property cash flow is temporarily insufficient to support principal.
- Covenants: the handbook's list of appropriate financial covenants for income-producing CRE is debt yield, DSCR, LTV, LTC, and borrower or guarantor minimum net worth or liquidity. It also warns that debt-service coverage calculations for covenant compliance may differ from the DSCR used for underwriting and risk rating, so the definitions in the loan documents need to be set deliberately.
- Construction and heavy repositioning add a capital-treatment test: under 12 CFR 3.2, an ADC facility escapes HVCRE treatment only if, among other conditions, LTV is at or below the applicable maximum supervisory LTV and the borrower has contributed at least 15% of the property's appraised as-completed value before the bank advances funds (other than a nominal sum to secure the lien), with reclassification available on substantial completion and cash flow sufficient to support debt service and expenses.

### Value and NOI inputs have to be labeled at the screen

- The appraisal or evaluation is a screening constraint, not a closing item. Under the appraisal regulations as summarized in the handbook, evaluations are permitted in lieu of appraisals where the loan amount is $500,000 or less, or where the transaction is a business loan of $1 million or less and income from the sale or rental of real estate is not the primary source of repayment. A state-certified appraiser is required for all transactions of $1 million or more and for nonresidential transactions of $500,000 or more.
- The Interagency Appraisal and Evaluation Guidelines require the valuation function to be isolated from influence by loan production staff, with reporting lines independent of loan production, and require the institution to review appraisals and evaluations prior to a final credit decision. For construction and unstabilized property the appraiser should provide the as-is market value and, as applicable, prospective market value upon completion and upon stabilization, with appropriate deductions and discounts for proposed construction or renovation, partially leased buildings, non-market lease terms, and tract developments with unsold units.
- The practical consequence for a screen is that a value used before an ordered and reviewed appraisal exists is an indicative value, and any LTV computed from it is indicative. Sizing off an as-stabilized value while the property is not stabilized is a leverage decision, not a valuation decision, and should be shown as such.
- On the income side, the handbook distinguishes underwritten NOI from cash flow: NOI analysis may use market vacancy above or below actual and may include non-cash items such as management fees and replacement reserves, while re-leasing costs are not always an operating expense in NOI but matter to cash flow. Appendix D gives the underwriting conventions: management fees typically 3% to 5% of effective gross income for office, retail (exclusive of reimbursements), and industrial, and 5% of revenue for multifamily; leasing commissions typically 4% of total lease payments for new leases and 2% for renewals; replacement reserves per square foot per year for office, retail, and industrial and per unit per year for multifamily, varying with age and condition; multifamily operating expenses usually 35% to 45% of revenue, with older, more amenitized, or landlord-utility-paid properties at the upper end.

### Market context for the screen (directional, as of 2026-09-01)

- The July 2026 SLOOS reported that in Q2 2026 moderate net shares of banks eased standards on nonfarm nonresidential loans, a smaller net share eased on multifamily, and construction and land development standards were essentially unchanged, while the level of standards remained at the tighter end of the range since 2005 for CLD and relatively tight for the other two categories.
- CREFC's Q1 2026 Board of Governors Sentiment Index fell 20.2% to 100.1 from 125.4 (survey fielded 2026-04-07 to 2026-04-13). Respondents expected banks (46%) and private credit or debt funds (34%) to be the most active lenders over the following twelve months, and named secondary and lower-quality office (56%) and unstabilized transitional assets (32%) as the cohorts facing the greatest refinancing risk.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults because they are published rules or supervisory frames:

- The "lesser of" sizing frame: requested amount, program or statutory limits, DSCR-supportable proceeds, ratio-supportable proceeds (LTV / LTC), with debt yield and any exit test applied alongside. Adopted from HUD Mortgagee Letter 2025-03 and generalized using the OCC handbook's treatment of DSCR, debt yield, and LTV as complementary tests.
- Supervisory LTV limits (raw land 65%, land development 75%, commercial and multifamily construction 80%, improved property 85%) as screening ceilings, never as targets, with the 100% and 30% of total capital exception baskets tracked at full outstanding balance.
- As-if-amortizing DSCR for any interest-only request, and the origination-amount haircut to the projected maturity balance as the named mitigant.
- Amortization frames by property type: up to 30 years stabilized multifamily, generally 25 years office / retail / industrial, generally not more than 20 years hospitality; interest-only tenor usually three to five years maximum.
- Agency grids quoted as published: Freddie Mac Optigo fixed-rate 1.25x DCR with 75% / 80% LTV by term and 65% / 70% for full-term IO; FHA 223(f) and 221(d)(4) 87% / 1.15, 90% / 1.11, 90% / 1.11 by affordability tier.
- HVCRE screen for construction and heavy repositioning: 15% of as-completed appraised value contributed before advance, LTV at or below the supervisory limit.
- Appraisal engagement rules at screen: $500,000 evaluation threshold, state-certified appraiser at $1 million (or $500,000 nonresidential), ordering independent of loan production, review before final credit decision.

Case-by-case only, not defaults:

- Minimum DSCR, minimum debt yield, and maximum LTV for banks, credit unions, life companies, debt funds, and CMBS. These are institution-set or deal-specific; no regulator publishes them, and quoting a market average as a policy minimum is the error this note is written to prevent.
- Loan per square foot and loan per unit ceilings. No primary source publishes them; they are internal screens calibrated to local replacement cost and comparable sales, and should be presented as a basis check against market evidence rather than a threshold.
- Sponsor liquidity and net worth minimums, recourse burn-off triggers, and pricing floors. These come from the institution's policy and are handed to [Sponsor and Guarantor Analyst](skills/lender-credit/sponsor-and-guarantor-analyst.md).
- Exit or refinance test assumptions outside the agency programs. Fannie Mae's +2.0% reversion cap and +2.25% refinance rate spreads are cited as a published example of how to stress an exit, not as a universal standard.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Market averages versus policy minimums. CBRE's Q2 2026 averages (59.6% LTV, 1.43 DSCR, 10.2% debt yield) sit close to where many bank policies land, which makes it tempting to treat them as thresholds. The interagency guidelines and the OCC handbook control: limits are set by the institution's board-approved policy, and observed market averages are calibration only. The skill uses market data to frame reasonableness and always labels it directional with a date.
- Supervisory LTV limits versus prudent leverage. The 85% improved-property limit is far above where CRE is actually financed. The handbook resolves it directly by saying supervisory LTV limits do not establish a safe harbor, so the skill treats them as a ceiling and exception trigger rather than as a sizing input.
- Interest-only as a sizing benefit versus a risk. Program grids allow full-term IO at reduced leverage, while supervisory guidance warns that IO renewals can indicate a troubled loan. Both are correct within their frames: the agency grid prices IO with a lower LTV and an amortizing DCR test, which is the same mitigant the handbook recommends. The skill therefore permits IO only with an as-if-amortizing coverage test and a stated leverage reduction.
- Debt yield versus DSCR as the primary screen. Practitioner habit often leads with debt yield. The handbook is explicit that debt yield should be used along with other criteria and supported by prudent DSCR and LTV, so the skill runs all tests and reports the binding one rather than nominating a favorite.
- Definitional drift between underwriting DSCR and covenant DSCR. The handbook notes the two calculations may differ. The skill resolves it by requiring the screen to state which NOI (in-place, underwritten, or stabilized) and which debt service (actual, as-if-amortizing, or floor-rate) each ratio uses.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Owner-occupied and business-purpose requests. Where 50% or more of repayment does not come from third-party rental income, the credit is not a concentration-guidance CRE loan and the primary repayment analysis is the operating business, not the property. Screening it on property metrics alone misreads the credit.
- Requests below the appraisal threshold. An evaluation may be permitted at $500,000 or less, but the value support still has to be adequate for the credit decision, and a thin evaluation on a complex property is a documentation exception waiting to happen.
- Sizing off an as-stabilized or as-completed value before stabilization. Legitimate for construction and transitional lending when the value type is labeled and the deductions and discounts are present, and a misstatement of leverage when it is not.
- Signed-not-commenced leases, free rent, and lease-up NOI in the in-place case. In-place and underwritten NOI must be shown separately; a request that clears coverage only on the underwritten case is a structure conversation, not a pass.
- Single-tenant and net-lease credits. Lease term shorter than loan term, or a lease that expires before maturity, converts a coverage question into a residual value question. The handbook's own logic permits a lower DSCR only where the tenant credit and lease term are genuinely long-dated.
- Hospitality and other operating-intensive assets. Volatile cash flow argues for a higher DSCR and shorter amortization, and specialized property types are less marketable in liquidation.
- Ground leases, condominium regimes, and unusual collateral. These can defeat an otherwise clean sizing outcome; the handbook treats ground rent as an expense in DSCR and expects the lease term to extend beyond the amortization period where the loan does not fully amortize.
- Concentration-driven declines. A request can pass every sizing test and still be declined because the institution is at or approaching the 100% CLD or 300% CRE screening criteria, or is at an internal property-type or geographic sublimit. That is a portfolio answer, not a credit answer, and should be labeled as such. See [CRE Portfolio Concentration and Stress Tester](skills/lender-credit/cre-portfolio-concentration-and-stress-tester.md).
- Out-of-trade-area requests, requests above the officer's or committee's lending authority, and requests that require a pricing exception. All three are policy facts available at intake and should not surface after the analyst has spent a week underwriting.
- Repacked interest reserves, deferred equity, and equity composed of deferred developer profit or accrued land carry. Each defeats the equity and reserve tests that the sizing outcome assumes.

## Open Questions

- Institution-specific minimums (DSCR, debt yield, LTV, loan per unit and per square foot, sponsor liquidity, pricing floors) are deliberately not defaulted. The skill asks the user for them and, absent them, reports proceeds under each test rather than a verdict keyed to invented thresholds.
- CMBS and debt fund sizing conventions are documented in offering materials and rating agency presale reports rather than in published program grids; this note relies on the supervisory frame and market averages instead, and flags that gap.
- Life company appetite is disclosed only in aggregate portfolio statistics in public filings, which describe a book rather than a current screen.
- Whether the FHA ratios in Mortgagee Letter 2025-03 have since been superseded by a later mortgagee letter or a MAP Guide revision was not verified beyond the letter's own statement that it remains effective until amended, superseded, or rescinded; confirm against HUD's current issuances before relying on the grid.
- Small-balance and small-loan agency products carry their own grids that were not retrievable on the access date; sizing a small-balance request from the conventional grid will overstate proceeds in some markets.
