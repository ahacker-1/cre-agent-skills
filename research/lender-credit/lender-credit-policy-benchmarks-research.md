# Lender Credit Policy Benchmarks Research

## Purpose

- Supports `knowledge/lender-credit-policy-benchmarks.md`
- Intended users: bank and credit union credit analysts, underwriters, credit officers, portfolio managers, loan review staff, and debt fund / life company / agency credit teams who need a defensible frame for what a U.S. CRE credit policy contains and what sizing, sponsor, reserve, recourse, and pricing parameters are traceable to a real source

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States only. Supervisory loan-to-value limits, HVCRE capital treatment, concentration screening criteria, and agency multifamily program terms are U.S. federal constructs with no direct offshore analogue.
- Deal type: income-producing commercial real estate and acquisition, development, and construction (ADC) lending held on balance sheet by federally insured depositories, plus agency multifamily and life company mortgage lending for cross-reference.
- Legal and market assumptions: content is educational decision support, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Regulatory citations describe supervisory expectations, not a bank's own board-approved policy. Any institution's actual limits, delegations, and exception process control over anything in this note.
- Formula definitions (DSCR, debt yield, LTV, LTC, NOI) are not restated here; they live in `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`.

## Key Findings

### What a credit policy must contain

- The interagency real estate lending guidelines require underwriting standards that address, at minimum, maximum loan amount by property type, maximum loan maturities by property type, amortization schedules, pricing structure by loan type, and LTV limits by property type (OCC Comptroller's Handbook, quoting the guidelines).
- The OCC handbook expands that into a per-property-type policy checklist: minimum borrower/project net worth, guarantee support, borrower and guarantor cash flow, DSCR, LTV limits, maximum tenor, minimum debt yield, amortization criteria and limits on non-amortizing loans, pricing and profitability objectives, documentation minimums, construction disbursement controls, standards for borrower and guarantor global financial condition (assets by type/amount/liquidity, global cash flow, direct and contingent liabilities, tertiary repayment sources, minimum initial hard equity), feasibility and sensitivity expectations, interest reserve limits, IO limits for stabilized CRE, preleasing and presale requirements, limits on partial and nonrecourse loans, takeout commitment requirements, covenant requirements, borrower equity requirements including timing, and environmental standards.
- FDIC Section 3.2 lists the broader lending-policy content set every bank is expected to address regardless of size: fields of lending, officer and committee lending authority, board review/ratification responsibility, unsecured lending guidelines, rate and repayment guidelines, advance limits against collateral value with required documentation by collateral type, appraisal ordering and review guidelines, credit file maintenance, collection procedures, maximum loan volume relative to total assets, overdraft limits, trade area definition, portfolio mix and diversification goals with concentration monitoring, loan review and grading ("watch list") guidelines, ACL review guidelines, and environmental liability safeguards.
- The 2006 interagency concentration guidance states that lending policies should permit exceptions only on a limited basis, and that each exception should be documented as to how it fails to conform, obtain appropriate management approval, and be reported to the board with number, nature, justification, and trend.

### Leverage ceilings and the exception basket

- The interagency supervisory LTV (SLTV) limits are: raw land 65%; land development or improved lots 75%; construction of commercial, multifamily, and other nonresidential 80%; construction of one- to four-family residential 85%; improved commercial, multifamily, and other nonresidential property 85%; owner-occupied one- to four-family and home equity has no set limit but requires mortgage insurance or readily marketable collateral at 90% or above.
- Banks are expected to set internal limits at or below the SLTV limits, and to set lower internal limits for subcategories that carry greater credit risk than the overall category.
- Aggregate loans originated in excess of SLTV limits should not exceed 100% of total capital, and within that basket, loans on commercial, agricultural, multifamily, or other non-one-to-four-family property should not exceed 30% of total capital. The entire outstanding balance counts toward the basket, not just the excess over the limit. Collateral that declined in value after origination is not an SLTV exception.
- SLTV limits do not create a safe harbor; the OCC handbook says loans at those levels are not automatically considered sound.
- HVCRE capital treatment is a separate lever on ADC structure: a facility avoids the HVCRE classification only if, among other conditions, the borrower has contributed capital of at least 15% of the property's appraised as-completed value and the LTV is at or below the applicable maximum supervisory LTV, with the contributed capital remaining in the project until reclassification.

### Sizing, amortization, and interest-only

- The OCC handbook treats 30 years as a reasonable maximum amortization for income-producing CRE, with 15 to 30 years appropriate in most cases: stabilized multifamily at the high end (up to 30), hotels at the low end (generally not more than 20), and office, retail, and industrial in the middle (generally 25).
- Interest-only tenors are typically shorter than amortizing CRE loans, usually three to five years maximum. Even where a loan pays interest only, the property should still meet the bank's DSCR requirements as if amortizing. LTV and as-if-amortizing DSCR requirements for IO loans are generally more conservative than for amortizing loans. A renewal, refinance, or extension on an interest-only basis can indicate a troubled loan.
- The handbook offers a worked mitigant: reduce the origination amount to the balance the loan would have amortized to at maturity, so the IO structure does not increase loss given default.
- Debt yield is described as a rate-, amortization-, and cap-rate-independent risk measure, useful when rates and cap rates are low, with higher debt yields recommended for riskier property types. It is meant to be used alongside DSCR and LTV, not instead of them.
- Common financial covenants for income-producing CRE loans are debt yield, DSCR, LTV, LTC, and borrower/guarantor minimum net worth or liquidity.
- Ground lease structures: where the loan does not fully amortize, the handbook says the ground lease should extend beyond the amortization period, customarily 20 years; if the loan fully amortizes during the term, a lease extending 10 years beyond maturity is usually sufficient. Ground rent is deducted as an expense in DSCR. For a leased-fee land acquisition, the bank may lend up to the lower of 65% of market value or cost of the land.

### Agency multifamily as a published benchmark

- Freddie Mac's Optigo fixed-rate conventional product sheet dated 4/26 publishes a full grid: minimum 1.25x amortizing DCR at every term, with maximum LTV of 75% for 5-year and sub-7-year terms, 80% for 7-year, and 80% for terms over 7 years on amortizing and partial-IO loans; full-term IO drops maximum LTV to 65% for terms of 7 years or less and 70% for longer terms. Maximum combined maturity LTV for partial-IO loans is 70%. Partial-term IO requires at least 2 years of amortization. No refinance test is required if the amortizing DCR is 1.40x or greater and LTV is 60% or less. Maximum amortization is 30 years; tax and insurance escrows and replacement reserve deposits are generally required; loans are non-recourse except for standard carve-outs; the application fee is the greater of $2,000 or 0.1% of loan amount for conventional first mortgages.
- Fannie Mae's guide requires underwritten DSCR to be computed on a level debt service payment including amortization at the greater of the actual note rate or the required underwriting interest rate floor, and to use that same amortizing payment even during an interest-only period. Its refinance risk analysis sizes the exit at the minimum Tier 2 DSCR and maximum Tier 2 LTV for the product per Form 4660, using a reversion cap rate at least 2.0% above the initial cap rate and a refinance interest rate at least 2.25% above the current 10-year amortizing nationwide underwriting floor rate.

### Sponsor and guarantor analysis

- The OCC handbook requires cash flows to be assessed on a global basis, integrating business financial statements, tax returns, and Schedule K-1 forms across multiple partnerships, LLCs, and corporations, and to consider required and discretionary cash flows plus actual and contingent liabilities. Global cash flow analysis should be performed even when the sponsor holds significant liquid assets, because those assets may be needed for other contingent liabilities.
- Guarantor evaluation turns on both willingness and ability plus legal enforceability. Willingness may be presumed where the guarantor has economic incentive, absent evidence to the contrary. The analysis should consider demonstrated performance on prior obligations, economic incentive, significant investment in the project, and the liquidity of any assets collateralizing the guarantee. Guarantor liquidity should be verified by the bank, and unpledged guarantor assets should not be treated as a substitute for project equity.
- The guarantor assessment should consider the total number and amount of guarantees extended to all lenders, not just to the subject bank.
- Equity timing is a policy item: the handbook says policy should require equity to be contributed before construction disbursements begin, and that deferred developer profit, unearned developer fees, incurred overhead, and interest or holding fees accrued on contributed land are generally not equity.

### Recourse spectrum

- The handbook describes limited guarantees as interest-only, construction-completion-only, partial principal, stepped-down (reducing) in amount, or released during the term when conditions are met, and requires the bank to monitor and confirm achievement of those conditions before releasing the guarantor.
- On non-recourse loans, guarantees are usually executed with carve-out provisions limiting liability to losses from specified acts or omissions (fraud, misrepresentation, voluntary bankruptcy, environmental issues, unapproved liens, waste, prohibited transfers, diversion of funds), with certain carve-outs causing the loan to become full recourse.
- ArentFox Schiff's practitioner note draws the same two-tier distinction: loss-based recourse (indemnity limited to lender losses from covenant breaches) versus springing full recourse (typically insolvency actions, unpermitted transfers, control changes, additional debt, or separateness covenant breaches), which converts the entire loan to recourse.
- Freddie Mac's published product terms confirm the agency norm of non-recourse except for standard carve-outs.

### Reserves, escrows, and interest reserves

- Policy is expected to set standards for the acceptability of and limits on interest reserves. The handbook notes that project cash flow is ordinarily applied to interest before reserves are drawn, and that refunding or "repacking" a depleted interest reserve with additional debt is a red flag for credit deterioration.
- Agency multifamily practice, per the Freddie Mac product sheet, is that tax and insurance escrows and replacement reserve deposits are generally required.
- The OCC handbook underwrites replacement reserves on an annual per-square-foot basis for office, retail, and industrial and on an annual per-unit basis for multifamily, in each case varying with age and condition. It underwrites management fees at 3% to 5% of effective gross income for office, retail (exclusive of reimbursements), and industrial, and at 5% of revenues for multifamily. It cites typical leasing commission underwriting of 4% of total lease payments for new leases and 2% for renewals, with tenant improvement costs higher for new than renewing tenants.

### What real portfolios look like

- Q2 2026 CBRE data (published 2026-08-03): average commercial LTV 59.6% (from 60.8% a year earlier), multifamily LTV 63.3% (from 65.8%), DSCR 1.43 (from 1.34), debt yield 10.2% (from 9.7%), average coupon 5.7% (from 5.9%), commercial spreads 204 bps (down 21 bps), multifamily spreads 162 bps (down 15 bps). Non-agency origination share: alternative lenders 38%, banks 30%, life companies 21%, CMBS 11%. CBRE characterized lenders as competing on price rather than leverage.
- MetLife's FY2025 10-K reports an average commercial mortgage LTV of 68% and average DSCR of 2.1x at 2025-12-31, with 56.0% of the $42.4 billion portfolio at LTV below 65% and 84.0% at DSCR above 1.2x.
- Valley National Bancorp's FY2025 10-K reports a CRE concentration ratio of 333% at 2025-12-31 (down from 362% and 474% in the two prior years), a combined weighted average CRE LTV of 59% and DSCR of 1.69, and for its roughly $3.0 billion office book a weighted average LTV of 63% and DSCR of 1.91.
- MBA data reported 2026-08-06 shows Q2 2026 originations up 16% year over year, with CMBS up 68%, depositories up 61%, investor-driven lenders up 18%, GSEs down 17%, and life companies down 27%.
- The July 2026 SLOOS reports that banks eased standards for nonfarm nonresidential and multifamily loans in Q2 2026 while construction and land development standards were basically unchanged, and that the level of standards remains at the tighter end of the historical range.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults, because each is a published regulatory or program figure rather than a market observation:

- The SLTV limits table and both exception baskets (100% of total capital aggregate; 30% commercial subset).
- The HVCRE 15% contributed-capital-of-as-completed-value test and its supervisory LTV condition.
- The OCC amortization frame: up to 30 years maximum for income-producing CRE; 15 to 30 years typical; multifamily up to 30; hotel generally not more than 20; office, retail, industrial generally 25.
- The OCC interest-only frame: IO tenors usually 3 to 5 years maximum; DSCR tested as-if amortizing; IO LTV and DSCR requirements more conservative than amortizing.
- The OCC ground lease frames: ground lease extending customarily 20 years beyond a non-fully-amortizing loan's amortization period, or 10 years beyond maturity if fully amortizing; leased-fee land at the lower of 65% of market value or cost.
- The OCC property-type expense frames: management fee 3% to 5% of EGI for office, retail, industrial and 5% of revenue for multifamily; leasing commissions 4% new / 2% renewal.
- Freddie Mac's published Optigo fixed-rate LTV / DCR grid, refinance-test waiver (1.40x DCR and 60% LTV), and 2-year minimum amortization for partial-term IO, cited as an agency program benchmark rather than a universal CRE default.
- Fannie Mae's refinance risk parameters: reversion cap rate at least 2.0% above the initial cap rate and refinance rate at least 2.25% above the 10-year amortizing underwriting floor, cited as an agency stress convention.
- The policy content checklists from the OCC handbook, the interagency guidelines, FDIC Section 3.2, and the 2006 concentration guidance.

Case-by-case only, stated as such in the knowledge base:

- Any specific bank minimum DSCR, minimum debt yield, or maximum LTV by property type. No regulator publishes these. The OCC handbook explicitly leaves DSCR and debt yield calibration to the institution, varying with cash flow volatility, lease credit, and property type.
- Sponsor liquidity and net worth covenant levels (commonly expressed as a multiple of loan amount or a percentage of commitment). No retrieved primary source publishes a numeric standard; the handbook only requires that policy set minimums.
- Pricing, spreads, and fees. Directional only, dated to the source. CBRE Q2 2026 spread and coupon figures and Freddie Mac's published application fee are the only fee/pricing anchors in this note.
- Recourse burn-off thresholds (for example, recourse stepping down at a stated DSCR or occupancy). The handbook confirms stepped-down and conditional-release guarantees exist and must be monitored, but publishes no trigger levels.
- Concentration limits beyond the 100% / 300% screening criteria. Those live in `knowledge/cre-concentration-and-stress-testing.md`.

Formulas: none are derived here. DSCR, debt yield, LTV, LTC, and NOI definitions come from `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`. The only computational conventions adopted are Fannie Mae's (level amortizing debt service at the greater of note rate or underwriting floor, applied even during IO periods) and the OCC's IO-mitigant illustration (size to the projected maturity balance of an amortizing structure).

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Regulatory versus market leverage. The SLTV improved-property limit is 85%, while CBRE reports actual average commercial LTV at 59.6% in Q2 2026 and MetLife reports a 68% portfolio average. These are not in conflict: the SLTV is a supervisory ceiling with an exception basket, not a target. The knowledge base presents the SLTV as a ceiling and the market averages as dated observations, and does not present 85% as a sizing default.
- Bank versus agency versus life company sizing. Freddie Mac publishes 75% to 80% maximum LTV at 1.25x DCR for conventional multifamily; the bank and life company filings show materially lower realized leverage. Program documents win for describing that program; company filings win for describing that lender's book; neither is generalized across lender types.
- SLOOS easing versus CREFC caution. The July 2026 SLOOS reports easing standards, while CREFC's Q1 2026 sentiment index fell 20.2% to baseline. Both are dated and are presented as such; the regulator survey is given priority for the direction of bank credit standards, and the association survey for practitioner sentiment about issuance and refinance risk.
- Amortization guidance versus agency terms. The OCC's "generally 25 years" for office/retail/industrial and Freddie Mac's 30-year maximum multifamily amortization are consistent once property type is held constant; the knowledge base keeps them in separate columns rather than blending them into one range.
- MBA source access. MBA's own press release could not be retrieved on the access date (bot challenge), so the MBA figures are cited to the trade press article that reports them, with attribution to MBA stated in the source table.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Owner-occupied CRE is excluded from the 2006 guidance's CRE concentration definition, and its repayment analysis is business-cash-flow driven. Do not apply investor-CRE sizing tests to owner-occupied credits without saying so.
- ADC credits underwritten without required amortization or project-generated interest have no objective performance measure; the handbook directs evaluation of projected project cash flow, progress against plan and appraisal assumptions, and guarantor support instead.
- Interest reserve repacking, IO renewals or extensions on stabilized property, and re-amortizing a maturing balance over the original period all appear in the handbook as indicators of possible credit deterioration rather than neutral structuring choices.
- Cross-collateralized and multi-phase loans have specific SLTV computation rules (sum of each property's value less senior liens times its own limit; final-phase limit for multi-phase loans) that are easy to get wrong.
- Loans meeting SLTV at origination that later exceed it because collateral value fell are not SLTV exceptions. Treat them as risk-rating and monitoring items, not policy exceptions.
- Debt service covenant definitions in loan documents may differ from the DSCR used for underwriting and risk rating. The handbook says so explicitly; a covenant test and an underwriting test can disagree without either being wrong.
- Where a credit's classification, accrual status, or accommodation treatment is at issue, the controlling frames are the risk rating and workout guidance, not this note. Defer to `knowledge/regulatory-risk-rating-and-classification.md`.
- Any legal enforceability question about a guarantee, carve-out, or springing recourse trigger requires counsel. State law governs, and the practitioner commentary cited here is issue-spotting only.

## Open Questions

- Fannie Mae Form 4660 holds the actual tier grid (minimum DSCR and maximum LTV by product and feature) referenced throughout the selling guide chapter. It was not retrieved in this session, so the knowledge base cites Freddie Mac's published grid as the agency benchmark and describes Fannie Mae's tier mechanism without asserting its numeric values.
- No retrieved source publishes numeric sponsor liquidity or net worth covenant conventions (for example, liquidity equal to a percentage of loan amount). Left case-by-case.
- Credit union CRE (NCUA member business lending rules) and the specific policy expectations for debt funds and non-bank lenders were not researched here; the pack treats depository supervision as the reference frame.
- Life company and CMBS credit policy documents are not public in the way bank supervisory guidance is; the life company frame in this note rests on one insurer's 10-K disclosure plus market aggregates.
- CECL / ASC 326 measurement practice affects reserve levels but not origination policy; it is deferred to the risk rating and classification knowledge base.
