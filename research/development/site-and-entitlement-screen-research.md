# Site and Entitlement Screen Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/development/site-and-entitlement-screen.md`
- Also draws on and must stay consistent with `knowledge/entitlement-and-site-risk.md` and `knowledge/development-benchmarks.md`
- Intended users: developers, development managers, owner representatives, construction lenders, and equity partners deciding whether to pursue, option, or walk away from a U.S. development site before predevelopment capital is committed

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States. Land use is a state and local power exercised under state enabling statutes, so zoning districts, dimensional standards, hearing calendars, notice periods, appeal windows, and fee schedules are jurisdiction-specific. Federal overlays (CERCLA, Clean Water Act, Endangered Species Act, National Historic Preservation Act, NFIP) apply nationally but are triggered by project-specific facts.
- Deal type: ground-up development and heavy redevelopment across multifamily, industrial, retail, office, and mixed-use. Stabilized-asset underwriting is out of scope and belongs to the sector packs.
- Legal assumptions: this is educational decision support, not legal, environmental, engineering, tax, or investment advice. Local land use counsel, licensed environmental professionals, and licensed geotechnical and civil engineers control the actual conclusions.
- State examples used below (New York, California) are illustrations of how a regime is structured, not national defaults.

## Key Findings

### The screen has to separate what is fixed from what is negotiated

- Three different things get conflated as "entitlement risk": dimensional compliance (does the program fit the code as written), procedural risk (how long the approval takes and who decides), and political risk (whether a discretionary body will say yes). A screen that produces one blended adjective is not usable. Rate them separately and let the worst one drive the rating.
- The single most consequential fact in the screen is whether the intended program is ministerial or discretionary. A ministerial path fails on schedule and cost; a discretionary path can fail on the record with no remedy but litigation or a redesign.
- Statutory clocks exist and they are local. New York Town Law 274-a requires a decision within 62 days of the close of the hearing or of application receipt if no hearing, with 10-day mailed and 5-day published notice, extendable only by mutual consent. That is one state's site plan provision, not a national standard, and it is a floor on the local process rather than a forecast of it.

### Vesting is what turns an approval into an asset

- California Government Code 65864 states the legislative premise directly: lack of certainty in approval wastes resources, raises the cost of housing and other development, and discourages investment in comprehensive planning. That is the problem a development agreement is built to solve.
- Section 65866 supplies the mechanism: the rules, regulations, and official policies governing permitted uses, density, and design are those in effect when the agreement is executed, though the jurisdiction may still apply later rules that do not conflict with the agreement. The screen should therefore always ask what freezes, when it freezes, and for how long, rather than treating "approved" as a binary.
- Because vesting doctrine is state-specific (statutory development agreements, common law vested rights, permit-based vesting, and vesting tentative maps all differ), the skill must ask the question and route it to counsel rather than publish a rule.

### Exactions and fees are underwritable, and now challengeable

- Sheetz v. County of El Dorado (decided 2024-04-12, unanimous) held the Takings Clause does not distinguish legislative from administrative permit conditions, so a legislatively adopted monetary exaction is subject to the Nollan essential nexus and Dolan rough proportionality tests. The Court expressly left open how individualized a condition imposed on a class of properties must be, and the Kavanaugh concurrence notes the decision does not prohibit reasonable formulas or schedules assessing classes of development.
- Practical consequence for a screen: continue to budget the adopted fee schedule at face value, but flag a fee that has no visible nexus to the project's impact as a counsel question rather than assuming it is unassailable.
- Magnitude justifies the line item. NAHB reported on 2026-06-09 that regulation adds $131,734 to the price of an average new single-family home, 26.4% of a $499,500 average sales price as of January 2026, with $46,795 (9.4%) landing at the land development stage. That is single-family, dated, and directional, and it does not transfer to income property, but it is the right order of magnitude for why the fee line cannot be a placeholder.

### Environmental and physical constraints have hard, checkable triggers

- Phase I ESA: the AAI rule at 40 CFR Part 312 recognizes ASTM E1527-21 (and E2247-23 for forestland or rural property). Full AAI must occur within one year before acquisition, and the interviews, government records review, visual inspection, and lien search must be conducted or updated within 180 days before acquisition. Those two windows are the only defensible shelf-life test, and compliance is what supports the innocent landowner, contiguous property owner, and bona fide prospective purchaser defenses.
- Wetlands and waters: the Section 404 program requires the applicant to show avoidance first, then minimization, then compensation for remaining unavoidable impacts. General permits cover minimal-effect activities; individual permits carry public interest review and a public notice. The Corps administers the permit, EPA writes the 404(b)(1) Guidelines.
- Construction stormwater: a permit is required at 1 acre or more of disturbance, or below 1 acre where the parcel is part of a larger common plan of development or sale that will ultimately disturb an acre or more. EPA's own Construction General Permit applies only where EPA is the permitting authority, so on most sites the operative permit is a state general permit.
- Flood: 44 CFR 60.3 requires a permit for all proposed construction or other development in the SFHA, new residential lowest floor including basement at or above the base flood elevation, and either elevation or certified watertight construction for non-residential. The regulatory floodway is defined to carry the base flood without raising the surface elevation more than one foot, and new floodway development is prohibited unless analysis demonstrates no rise. FEMA's Map Service Center is the official lookup, searchable by address, place, or coordinates, and its maps are revised over time.
- Listed species: a non-federal project whose lawful activity could take a federally listed species needs a Habitat Conservation Plan supporting an ESA Section 10(a)(1)(B) incidental take permit. Roughly two thirds of federally listed species have at least some habitat on private land, so this is not an exotic trigger.
- Historic and federal nexus: Section 106 reaches projects a federal agency carries out or that receive federal financial assistance, permits, or approvals, under 36 CFR Part 800. Federal funding, a loan guarantee, or a Section 404 individual permit can pull a nominally private project into federal review.
- Geotechnical: IBC Section 1803 defines what a geotechnical investigation report must contain, and the building official may waive it only where satisfactory adjacent data exists, the structure is unoccupied and low-risk on failure, or it is agricultural. The expansive soil test is explicit: plasticity index of 15 or greater per ASTM D4318, more than 10% passing a No. 200 sieve, more than 10% finer than 5 micrometers, and an expansion index above 20 per ASTM D4829, or the expansion index criterion alone.

### Utilities, offsite work, and political risk are schedule items

- A will-serve letter is a utility's written commitment to deliver water, wastewater, electric, gas, or broadband service to the project. Practitioner guidance treats obtaining them early, together with right-of-way acquisition for the pipes, cables, and access roads that serve the site, as a named schedule risk to preempt rather than a formality.
- State environmental review adds an independent clock on top of local approvals. NYSDEC's published SEQR process contemplates 30 days to agree on a lead agency for a Type I action, 20 days for the determination of significance, 60 days to a final written scope, 45 days to review draft EIS adequacy (30 on resubmittal), a minimum 30-day comment period continuing at least 10 days past any hearing, and findings no sooner than 10 days after the Notice of Completion of the Final EIS with lead agency findings within 30 days on applicant actions. Those are structural minimums for one state, not a schedule.
- Litigation risk is small per project and large per big project. Holland and Knight's Hernandez research reported 47,999 housing units targeted by CEQA lawsuits filed in 2020, roughly half of that year's California housing production against a six-year average of 110,784 homes built annually. A large, visible, discretionary project belongs to the litigated population even where the base rate is low.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults, because each is a published federal or state rule rather than a market observation:

- Phase I shelf life: full AAI within 1 year of acquisition, with interviews, records review, visual inspection, and lien search within 180 days. A Phase I outside those windows is stale for the screen.
- Construction stormwater trigger: 1 acre disturbed, or any disturbance within a larger common plan of development or sale reaching 1 acre.
- Floodway rule: no new development in the regulatory floodway absent a demonstrated no-rise; SFHA development requires a local permit and BFE-referenced lowest floor or certified floodproofing.
- Geotechnical adequacy: measure the report against the IBC 1803 scope list, and apply the 1803 expansive soil criteria as stated.
- Federal nexus test: federal funding, permit, license, or approval pulls in Section 106 and potentially NEPA.
- Approval classification: ministerial versus quasi-judicial versus legislative, with the appeal and referendum window treated as part of the entitlement, not after it.

Deliberately left case-by-case, with no default published:

- Months to entitlement by approval type. There is no defensible national figure, and this library does not publish one. The schedule must be built from the local code, the published hearing calendar, and comparable recent approvals in the same jurisdiction before the same decision maker. This matches the position already taken in `knowledge/entitlement-and-site-risk.md`.
- Impact, linkage, and inclusionary fee amounts. These are set locally and must be pulled from the currently adopted schedule with the payment trigger confirmed. The NAHB and NAHB/NMHC percentages are magnitude indicators only.
- Phase II scope and cost, remediation allowances, and offsite improvement cost. Driven by recognized environmental conditions, the environmental professional's judgment, and the conditions of approval.
- Parking, FAR, height, and setback standards. Entirely local. The screen reads them from the code and tests the program against them; it does not carry defaults.

Decision rule adopted for the skill:

- Entitlement risk rating is set by the worst of three axes (dimensional fit, procedural path, political exposure), never by an average, and is expressed on the library's shared LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / CRITICAL scale from `knowledge/risk-scoring.md` so it carries into the development IC memo without translation. A by-right program with a hostile abutter group and no zoning relief needed is still LOW-to-MEDIUM on this framing; a fully compliant design that needs a rezoning is HIGH regardless of how clean the drawings are.
- Go / no-go is a pursuit decision, not an investment decision. The screen recommends how much predevelopment capital to risk and against what contingency structure, and hands the numbers to `skills/development/development-budget-and-yield-on-cost-analyst.md` rather than concluding on returns.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Federal minimum versus local standard. 44 CFR 60.3 sets the NFIP floor; many communities adopt freeboard above BFE and stricter floodway rules. Where they differ, the local ordinance controls and the screen must read it. The federal rule is the backstop, not the answer.
- EPA versus state permitting authority for construction stormwater. EPA's page describes its own Construction General Permit, but most states run authorized programs. Resolved in favor of naming the state general permit as the operative instrument and treating the EPA CGP as the exception.
- Sheetz versus settled fee practice. Practitioner habit treats a legislatively adopted fee schedule as unchallengeable. Sheetz says otherwise as to the legislative/administrative distinction, while expressly leaving the individualization question open. Resolved by treating the adopted schedule as the budget number and the nexus question as a counsel referral, not by inviting the user to discount fees.
- Litigation base rate versus targeted-unit counts. Low per-project CEQA litigation rates and large targeted-unit counts are both true and measure different things. Resolved by underwriting project-specific exposure (size, visibility, discretionary approval, organized opposition) rather than the base rate, consistent with the red flag already stated in `knowledge/entitlement-and-site-risk.md`.
- Single-family regulatory cost data applied to income property. NAHB's $131,734 figure is single-family. Resolved by using it only as a magnitude argument for why the fee line matters, never as a per-unit input to an income-property budget.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Sites where zoning permits the use but a recorded instrument does not: deed restrictions, restrictive covenants, prior development agreements, annexation agreements, easements, and access agreements bind independently of the code and are missed by a zoning-only screen.
- Legal nonconforming status on a redevelopment site. Demolition or a change of use can extinguish a nonconforming right that the pro forma silently assumes survives.
- Split-zoned parcels, overlay districts, form-based codes, and specific plans, where two sets of standards apply and the more restrictive usually governs.
- Assemblages where entitlement depends on a parcel not yet under control, or on a lot line adjustment, subdivision, or vacation that is itself discretionary.
- Sites where the buildable area shrinks after wetland delineation, floodway mapping, setback from a stream or bluff, or a required easement, so the program fits the parcel but not the developable envelope.
- Stale environmental work: a Phase I outside the 1-year or 180-day AAI windows, an unverified or expired wetland delineation, or a report addressed to a party other than the buyer with no reliance letter.
- A recognized environmental condition with no Phase II, no remediation allowance, and no environmental insurance in the pursuit budget.
- Utility capacity described in general adequacy language rather than committed to this project, or a will-serve letter that expires before permits are pulled.
- Redevelopment geotechnical exposures a Phase I will not find: undocumented fill, prior foundations and slabs, abandoned utilities, buried tanks.
- Federal nexus discovered late, after the schedule was built on local approvals only.
- A land contract with no entitlement contingency, or a deposit that goes hard before the discretionary approval and its appeal or referendum period runs.
- Importing a California density bonus, concession, waiver, or streamlining assumption into a state with no comparable statute.

## Open Questions

- No defensible national dataset of months-to-entitlement by approval type and jurisdiction was located. Comparable outcomes must still be built by hand from the local record.
- The California HCD State Density Bonus Law fact sheet PDF and the USACE nationwide permit pages could not be parsed or opened in this session (PDF encoding failure and HTTP 403 respectively). Density bonus and NWP 39 detail in the skill is carried through `knowledge/entitlement-and-site-risk.md`, which cites them from prior retrieval, rather than restated here.
- State vesting doctrine (common law vested rights, vesting tentative maps, permit-based vesting) varies enough that no cross-state summary is offered; the skill routes the question to counsel.
- Post-Sheetz lower court treatment of class-wide fee schedules is still developing; the individualization question the Court left open is unresolved.
- Emerging contaminant regulation, particularly PFAS, is state-driven and moving; the screen flags it as a state-specific question rather than a national trigger.
