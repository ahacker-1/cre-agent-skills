# Lease-Up and Stabilization Pro Forma Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/development/lease-up-and-stabilization-pro-forma.md`
- Cross-checks and reuses definitions from `knowledge/development-benchmarks.md`, `knowledge/construction-lending-criteria.md`, and `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`
- Intended users: developers, development managers, owner representatives, construction lenders, and equity partners modeling the interval between first delivery and stabilization, sizing the operating deficit and interest carry over that interval, and testing whether a permanent refinance or sale repays the construction loan

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States
- Deal type: ground-up development and heavy redevelopment of income-producing property (multifamily, industrial, retail, office, mixed-use) financed with a construction loan and exited through permanent refinance, sale, or contribution
- Legal and market assumptions: bank supervisory guidance cited applies to U.S. banks and savings institutions; FHA program criteria apply only to FHA-insured multifamily loans; non-bank and agency lenders are not bound by bank supervisory limits. Stabilized-asset operating assumptions belong to the sector packs and to `skills/asset-management/`. This is educational decision support, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice.
- Every rent, absorption, vacancy, and cap rate figure below is directional as of its stated date.

## Key Findings

### Stabilization is a definition, not an event

Three institutional sponsors publish three different tests, and the definition changes the reported date:

- AvalonBay: "Stabilized operations is defined as the earlier of (i) attainment of 90% or greater physical occupancy or (ii) the one-year anniversary of completion of development" (FY2025 10-K).
- Camden: "We generally consider a property stabilized once it reaches 90% occupancy" (FY2025 10-K). No time backstop, so a slow lease-up shows up as a later stabilization date rather than being capped.
- Prologis: reports a development portfolio of properties "under development and pre-stabilized" and calculates weighted average stabilized yield as "estimated NOI assuming stabilized occupancy divided by TEI" (FY2025 10-K).

The OCC supplies the appraisal-side definition, which is the one a lender's as-stabilized value rests on: stabilized occupancy is "the occupancy level that a property is expected to achieve after the property is exposed to the market for lease-up over a reasonable period of time and at comparable terms and conditions to other similar properties." The OCC also states that an as-stabilized value is the appropriate basis for "a property to be constructed that is not preleased to stabilized levels," while an as-complete value fits a preleased or owner-occupied building.

HUD adds a fourth, cash-flow-based milestone that is not an occupancy percentage at all. Sustaining occupancy is the occupancy at which monthly rents "are sufficient to pay all operating expenses for the month ... plus monthly debt service composed of principal, interest, and MIP," which the MAP Guide states "is equivalent to a debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) of 1.0, a ratio also referred to as 'breakeven.'" The standard test is sustaining that level for 6 consecutive months for new construction and substantial rehabilitation.

### Lease-up duration: derived intervals, dated

Computed from AvalonBay's table of 24 wholly-owned communities under construction at December 31, 2025 (quarters, using its earlier-of-90%-or-one-year definition, which truncates the tail):

| Interval | Median | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Initial projected or actual occupancy to estimated completion | 3 quarters | 0 to 8 |
| Estimated completion to estimated stabilized operations | 2 quarters | 1 to 4 |
| Initial occupancy to estimated stabilized operations | 6 quarters | 3 to 10 |

The first row is the finding most often missed in pro formas: institutional multifamily typically begins leasing roughly three quarters before construction completion, so the lease-up curve, the operating deficit, and the interest carry all straddle completion rather than beginning at it.

Camden, using a 90% test with no backstop, discloses both actual and forecast intervals from construction completion to stabilization:

- FY2025 actual stabilizations: Camden Woodmill Creek (189 homes, Spring TX) 2Q24 to 2Q25 = 4 quarters; Camden Durham (420 homes, Durham NC) 4Q24 to 3Q25 = 3 quarters; Camden Long Meadow Farms (188 homes, Richmond TX) 4Q24 to 4Q25 = 4 quarters.
- Completed and in lease-up at 12/31/2025: Camden Village District (369 homes, Raleigh NC), completed 3Q25, 60% leased at January 31, 2026, estimated stabilization 1Q27 = 6 quarters after completion.
- Under-construction pipeline forecasts: 2Q27 to 4Q28 = 6 quarters; 3Q27 to 3Q28 = 4 quarters; 3Q28 to 2Q30 = 7 quarters.

Camden's forecast intervals run roughly two to three quarters longer than its 2025 actuals. That gap is the single most useful sanity check in this research: a sponsor pro forma that assumes a lease-up faster than the same sponsor's own recent forecasts is optimistic on its face.

HUD imposes a hard program cap: "The absorption period used in estimating market demand for proposed newly constructed or substantially rehabilitated units should not exceed 18 months from delivery of the first units," a limit the guide notes was reduced from 24 to 18 months. Exceptions for large high-rise buildings require a larger initial operating deposit, and the large-loan chapter repeats that projects with an extended lease-up "may require larger operating deficit or debt service reserves." HUD also defines the pace metric plainly: "The Absorption Rate is the average number of units rented each month during the absorption period."

### The operating deficit is a budgeted line, and HUD shows how to build it

HUD requires an operating deficit escrow, called the Initial Operating Deficit (IOD) escrow for construction loans, "on all applications for new construction and many applications for substantial rehabilitation to provide funding for operating expenses and debt service when net income is not available during the initial lease up and stabilization period." It is not mortgageable and unused portions are returned to the borrower.

The MAP Guide's three-interval structure is a transferable model for any deal, agency or not, because it separates the costs that are already inside the construction loan from the ones that are not:

| Interval | Span | What is charged to it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (optional) | Certificate of occupancy to end of the construction period and cost certification (construction time plus two months) | Operating and leasing expenses only. No debt service, because construction-period mortgage interest already sits in the mortgage. No replacement reserves or ground rent |
| 2 (optional) | End of the construction period to the start of principal amortization; no more than 2 months | Operating expenses plus interest and MIP, no amortization. Ground rent if leasehold. No replacement reserves |
| 3 | From the start of amortization | Operating expenses plus fully amortizing debt service (principal, interest, MIP), plus ground rent and replacement reserves |

HUD requires amortization to begin "no later than 4 months after construction completion for insurance of advances," which is why unaccounted interest and MIP between the end of the construction interest line and the start of amortization must be added into the IOD.

### Interest carry after completion is where lease-up failures show up first

The OCC is explicit that an appropriate interest reserve "provides sufficient funds to pay interest through the project's anticipated completion and lease-up, sale, or occupancy," and that the sizing judgment turns on "the reasonableness of the development assumptions, including potential changes in interest rates, the timing of expected disbursements and pay downs, and the time required for the completion and sale or lease-up of the project." It also warns that "the presence of an interest reserve may not accurately reflect a borrower's ability to pay."

Two operating rules follow directly:

- Cash flow first. "During the lease-up period, any cash flow from the project is ordinarily applied to pay interest before interest reserves are applied. Once the cash flow is sufficient to cover the interest, no further draws on the reserve should be permitted."
- A depleted reserve is a credit event, not a budget amendment. "Extended lease-up periods can deplete the interest reserve prematurely and render the construction budget inadequate, requiring a contribution of additional equity or an unplanned increase in the loan amount," and repacking a reserve with new debt "is a red flag indicating possible credit deterioration." The FDIC module requires that any extension or increase in interest reserves be supported by a realistic reassessment of feasibility, timeframe, and guarantor capacity.

The OCC also prescribes lease-up-period monitoring that doubles as the pro forma's own variance test: "Monthly leasing reports with rent rolls should be obtained from the borrower during the lease-up period," and analyzed "to compare actual lease rates and other key terms with the underwriting pro forma projections and assumptions used in the appraisal."

### Takeout: the exit is conditioned on lease-up, not on completion

The OCC describes the construction-to-permanent path and its conditions:

- Bridge and mini-perm. "A bridge loan provides short-term financing to allow newly constructed or acquired commercial properties to reach stabilization. Bridge loans are usually written for a period of up to three years and allow for the lease-up and income stabilization necessary to enable either sale or qualification for permanent financing."
- Tenor and extensions. "The appropriate tenor is generally based on the time needed for construction and stabilization or sale and would not be shorter than that required for completion," with the facility optionally "converting to bridge financing for the expected stabilization period," and extension option length "consistent with the expected construction time plus the projected absorption period."
- Standby versus forward. A standby commitment is back-up financing priced to discourage use; a forward commitment obligates the permanent lender to fund "upon future completion and, almost always, lease-up." Take-outs are "usually conditioned on ... lease-up to break even or better with leases at minimum rental rates," and the handbook's examination questions contemplate a construction loan "limited to the floor of a take-out commitment predicated on achievement of rents or lease occupancy." The governing instruction: "Underwriting would ordinarily include analysis of the risk should the take-out commitment not be funded."

The FDIC module lists the mechanical validity tests for a third-party takeout: counsel review for acceptability, review of the permanent lender's financial statements to assess capacity, a tri-party buy and sell agreement completed before the construction loan closes, and an automatic completion-date extension clause for delays beyond the builder's control. It also directs examiners to consider "marketability of properties without take-out commitments."

Sized takeout criteria exist where the program publishes them. HUD Mortgagee Letter 2025-03 (January 8, 2025) sets maximum loan amounts as the lesser of the requested amount, statutory limits, the DSCR-supportable amount, and the ratio-supportable amount, with these criteria:

| Category | LTV / LTC | DSCR | Vacancy factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90% or greater units with rental assistance, 221(d)(4) NC/SR and 223(f) | 90% (no change) | 1.11 (no change) | 3% |
| Affordable, LIHTC with rent advantage to market, 221(d)(4) NC/SR and 223(f) | 87% to 90% | 1.15 to 1.11 | 5% |
| Market rate or LIHTC without rent advantage, 221(d)(4) NC/SR and 223(f) | 85% to 87% | 1.176 to 1.15 | 7% |

Conventional and agency takeout terms are quote-driven. Fannie Mae's Multifamily Guide carries a "Near Stabilized Property Submissions" update (Guide Update 26-18, dated July 20, 2026), confirming an agency path exists for properties short of full stabilization, but the underlying tests must be confirmed with the lender rather than assumed.

Availability context, dated: the Federal Reserve's July 2026 Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey reported that in Q2 2026 "standards for construction and land development (CLD) loans remained basically unchanged on net" with "a moderate net share of banks reported weaker demand for CLD loans," while moderate net shares eased standards for nonfarm nonresidential and modest net shares eased for multifamily, with demand for both basically unchanged.

### Concessions are a revenue-recognition problem, not just a rent problem

AvalonBay's FY2025 10-K states the mechanic precisely: "We amortize concessions on a straight-line basis over the life of the respective leases (generally one year), reducing the income recognized over the lease term." Same-store concessions granted rose $6,976,000 to $24,198,000 in 2025, with an unamortized balance of $13,025,000 at December 31, 2025 versus $9,436,000 a year earlier. Camden reports average monthly rental rate per home "incorporat[ing] vacant units and resident concessions calculated on a straight-line basis over the life of the lease."

The practical consequence for a lease-up model: a concession granted in month 4 keeps depressing recognized revenue into the following year, so stabilized NOI computed from a physical-occupancy milestone will overstate the cash and GAAP NOI a lender or buyer will actually see until the concession balance burns off.

Regulators require the same adjustment. HUD's market study standards direct the analyst to report "the extent of rent concessions or similar incentives, particularly in projects in initial occupancy," and to "address the impact of concessions on rent levels and whether the quoted rents are overstated due to concessions or other factors." The OCC's collateral-assessment list includes "effective rental rates or sale prices, considering sales and financing concessions." The FDIC module's market assessment includes "current and projected lease terms, rental rates, and sales prices, including concessions."

No quantified national concession benchmark (share of listings offering concessions, or average months free by product type and market) was retrieved this session. CBRE's Q2 2026 industrial report notes qualitatively that "ongoing supply chain uncertainty is driving tenants to take advantage of growing landlord concessions and hold more space," but does not quantify them.

### Pre-leasing shifts the whole curve, and is measurable in industrial

Prologis disclosed that at December 31, 2025 its consolidated development portfolio, including properties under development and pre-stabilized properties, "was expected to be completed before September 2027 with a TEI of $5.1 billion and was 53.5% leased," across 77 properties with $3.0 billion invested and $2.1 billion of estimated additional required investment. Build-to-suit accounted for 60.9% of 2025 development starts by TEI, up from 28.6% in 2024. On the 40 buildings stabilized in 2025 (11 million sq ft, $2,271 million TEI, 43.8% build-to-suit by TEI), the weighted average stabilized yield was 6.7% and the estimated weighted average margin 25.4%, versus 6.2% and 19.2% on 2024 stabilizations.

The OCC ties pre-leasing directly to the valuation basis: a preleased property to be constructed uses as-complete value, while one "not preleased to stabilized levels" uses as-stabilized value. Pre-leasing therefore changes the appraisal basis, the LTV test, and the takeout condition, not merely the absorption curve.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults:

- Require the stabilization definition to be stated before any date, yield, or NOI is reported, and require the reporting of both the occupancy milestone and the cash-flow milestone (breakeven / sustaining occupancy). Supported by three divergent public-filing definitions plus the HUD sustaining-occupancy definition.
- Breakeven occupancy formula, from HUD's sustaining occupancy definition: the occupancy at which monthly rental collections equal operating expenses plus full monthly debt service, that is DSCR = 1.0. Express it as (operating expenses + debt service) / (gross potential rent at achievable rents, net of concessions and other income), consistent with the DSCR convention in [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md).
- Absorption rate defined as average units or square feet leased per month over the absorption period (HUD).
- Absorption period measured from delivery of the first units, not from construction completion (HUD, and confirmed by AvalonBay's 3-quarter median gap between initial occupancy and completion).
- 18 months from first delivery as a screening ceiling on the underwritten absorption period, with anything longer treated as an exception requiring a larger operating deficit reserve. This is FHA program policy, not a universal market rule, and must be labeled as such.
- Operating shortfall built on HUD's three intervals, so the model does not double-count construction-period interest already inside the loan and does not omit the interest and any MIP or fee accruing between the end of the construction interest line and the start of amortization.
- Cash flow applied to interest before any interest-reserve draw, and reserve draws stopped once project cash flow covers interest (OCC).
- Takeout sized as the minimum of the LTV, LTC, DSCR, and debt yield results, consistent with [Construction Lending Criteria](knowledge/construction-lending-criteria.md), and always paired with a case in which the takeout does not fund (OCC).
- Concessions amortized straight-line over the lease term for reported NOI and shown separately from the physical-occupancy curve (AvalonBay accounting policy; HUD and OCC both require effective, concession-adjusted rents).
- Report the lease-up result on both a net-effective-rent basis and a face-rent basis, and state which one the takeout lender will underwrite.

Case-by-case only, no repo default:

- Absorption pace in units or square feet per month for any specific product type or market. The retrieved evidence is sponsor-specific and market-specific, and the office, retail, and mixed-use ground-up lease-up durations have no comparable public disclosure.
- Concession levels (months free, share of leases carrying a concession, TI allowance levels for first-generation space). No quantified national benchmark was retrieved.
- Conventional or agency takeout DSCR, debt yield, LTV, and lease-up occupancy conditions. Quote-driven. Only the FHA figures above are published.
- Operating deficit reserve sizing as a percentage of cost or of stabilized NOI. HUD prescribes a build-up method, not a ratio, and that is the defensible approach.
- Retention or burn-off levels for carry guaranties. Structures are documented in [Construction Lending Criteria](knowledge/construction-lending-criteria.md); the numbers are negotiated.

Derived and disclosed as directional, dated, sponsor-specific:

- Institutional multifamily initial occupancy to completion: median 3 quarters, range 0 to 8 (AvalonBay pipeline at 12/31/2025).
- Institutional multifamily completion to stabilization: median 2 quarters, range 1 to 4 under a one-year-backstop definition (AvalonBay); 3 to 4 quarters actual and 4 to 7 quarters forecast under a pure 90% test (Camden). Both consistent with the ranges already in [Development Benchmarks](knowledge/development-benchmarks.md).
- Institutional multifamily initial occupancy to stabilization: median 6 quarters, range 3 to 10 (AvalonBay pipeline, truncated by the one-year backstop).
- Industrial pre-leasing: 53.5% of a $5.1 billion development portfolio leased at 12/31/2025, with build-to-suit at 60.9% of 2025 starts by TEI (Prologis).

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Stabilization definitions conflict across AvalonBay, Camden, Prologis, HUD, and the OCC appraisal standard. Resolution: no single definition wins. The skill requires the analyst to name the definition in use and, separately, to report the breakeven / sustaining-occupancy date, because that is the date the debt actually cares about. Where a comparison is made across sponsors or comps, the skill requires the definitions to be reconciled first.
- Lease-up duration evidence conflicts between AvalonBay's short completion-to-stabilization interval (median 2 quarters) and Camden's longer one (3 to 4 quarters actual, 4 to 7 forecast). The conflict is definitional, not market-driven: AvalonBay's one-year backstop caps the measured interval regardless of how leasing is going. Resolution: use the initial-occupancy-to-stabilization interval for AvalonBay and the completion-to-stabilization interval for Camden, and never mix the two into one benchmark.
- Camden's own actual stabilizations (3 to 4 quarters) conflict with its current forecasts (4 to 7 quarters). Resolution: the forward disclosure controls for underwriting a new project, because it reflects the sponsor's current view of the delivery market. Recent actuals are the floor case, not the base case.
- Regulator guidance versus sponsor practice on interest reserves: the OCC treats a funded reserve as no evidence of repayment ability, while sponsor budgets routinely treat the reserve as coverage. Resolution: regulator guidance takes priority. The skill computes the shortfall independently of what is in the reserve, then compares.
- HUD's 18-month absorption cap conflicts with the observed institutional multifamily interval from first occupancy to stabilization (median 6 quarters). Resolution: these measure different endpoints. HUD's cap runs to sustaining occupancy at DSCR 1.0; the sponsor intervals run to a 90% physical occupancy or one-year-backstop test. Both are reported, neither is converted into the other.
- Bank supervisory guidance (OCC, FDIC) versus non-bank lender practice. Resolution: label the supervisory items as applying to U.S. banks and savings institutions, consistent with the scope note in [Construction Lending Criteria](knowledge/construction-lending-criteria.md).

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

Cases where the skill should avoid hard-coded defaults:

- Office, retail, and mixed-use ground-up lease-up. No public-filing interval evidence comparable to the multifamily and industrial disclosures was located. These must be modeled from signed pre-leases, letters of intent, submarket leasing velocity, and the competitive set.
- Build-to-suit and single-tenant industrial or retail, where there is effectively no absorption curve but there is delivery, tenant fit-out, and rent-commencement risk instead. The relevant date is rent commencement under the lease, not a percent-leased milestone.
- Phased projects. HUD notes that larger projects may phase additional units under a separate application; bank HVCRE treatment also runs phase by phase. A blended, whole-project absorption curve across phases can hide a first phase that never reaches breakeven.
- Condominium and for-sale components, where the analogue is sellout with release prices and principal curtailments, and the OCC's guidance on presold units, speculative unit limits, and re-margining applies instead of an occupancy curve.
- Affordable, LIHTC, and rent-assisted deals, where the absorption curve is driven by income certification and waiting-list dynamics and where the FHA criteria above may set the takeout, not market underwriting.
- Rent-regulated jurisdictions, where the achievable stabilized rent and the concession structure are constrained by law. Camden's FY2025 10-K flags rent control and rent stabilization laws as a risk factor affecting operations and property values.

Situations requiring lender feedback, counsel, or market-specific overrides:

- Any takeout that is a standby rather than a forward commitment, or a forward commitment with a floor conditioned on achieving stated rents or occupancy. Counsel review of the take-out agreement, review of the permanent lender's financial capacity, and a tri-party buy and sell agreement executed before the construction loan closes are the FDIC's own tests.
- Any extension request, reserve replenishment, or budget amendment tied to slower lease-up. These require reassessment of feasibility and guarantor capacity, not a spreadsheet revision.
- Concession structures that push free rent beyond the first lease term, or that are paid as up-front cash rather than abated rent, which changes both the accounting treatment and the effective-rent calculation.
- Deals where the construction loan matures before the modeled stabilization date, or before the takeout's own lease-up condition can be satisfied.

Red flags identified in the sources:

- A lease-up curve faster than the sponsor's own recent comparable projects, or faster than the submarket's demonstrated absorption at the subject's rent level. HUD notes that a market analyst's absorption opinion "is contingent on a specific rent level."
- Rents underwritten at face value where competing projects in initial occupancy are offering concessions. HUD requires the analyst to address "whether the quoted rents are overstated due to concessions."
- Interest reserve sized to construction completion rather than through lease-up, sale, or occupancy (OCC).
- Interest reserve draws continuing after project cash flow is sufficient to cover interest (OCC).
- A reserve replenished with new loan proceeds after depletion, which the OCC calls a red flag indicating possible credit deterioration.
- An extension option shorter than construction time plus the projected absorption period (OCC).
- A takeout treated as certain when it is a standby, or is conditioned on rents or occupancy the project has not reached, with no analysis of the case where it does not fund (OCC).
- A stabilized NOI that ignores unamortized concession balances, so the NOI presented to a takeout lender or buyer overstates what will actually be recognized.
- Demand erosion during a long build: the OCC observes that "properties with longer construction periods are also more vulnerable to market changes because of longer lead time from initial project start to actual delivery," and that "if actual rental rates achieved during lease-up are lower than those projected, a project's viability can be threatened."
