# Development IC Memo Writer Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/development/development-ic-memo-writer.md`
- Intended users: developers, development managers, owner representatives, construction lenders, and equity partners who must convert site, budget, debt, contract, schedule, and lease-up work into a single investment committee or credit committee decision document
- Secondary purpose: establish which decision-memo sections are supportable from published standards rather than from house convention

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States
- Deal type: ground-up development and heavy redevelopment of income-producing real estate across multifamily, industrial, retail, office, and mixed-use, plus for-sale and build-to-suit variants where noted
- Legal and market assumptions: land use is governed locally; bank supervisory guidance applies to U.S. banks and savings institutions and not to non-bank lenders; HUD program requirements apply only to FHA-insured multifamily transactions and are used here as a published, non-anonymous model of what a decision memorandum must address, not as a private-market rule
- Educational decision support only; not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice
- Stabilized-asset operating assumptions come from the multifamily, industrial, office, and retail packs and are not restated here

## Key Findings

### What a decision memorandum is required to contain

- HUD's MAP Guide Section 11.3.3 states that the memorandum recommending loan approval "will be in the standard loan committee report or alternative format" and must specifically address the adequacy of the initial operating deficit, the adequacy of the working capital escrow and construction contingency, the adequacy of the initial deposit and ongoing replacement reserve payments, any post-closing repairs, the schedule of equity contribution at closing and during construction in a tax credit transaction, the architectural drawings and specifications, and any environmental conditions or other concerns. Where the underwriter rejected or modified a technical reviewer's conclusion, the documentation and justification must be included in the memorandum.
- MAP Guide Appendix 4 gives a section order for a new construction narrative that maps closely to a development IC memo: sponsor and principals, general contractor, previous program experience, location and mapping, photographs, site improvements existing and proposed, commercial component and potential tenants, amenities, community and municipal support, development status including permits and approvals obtained, general market conditions with competitive and comparable properties, environmental issues, actual and potential risks with mitigating factors, and anticipated waiver requests.
- The FDIC examination module lists what belongs in the credit file for an ADC loan: appraisal or evaluation plus documentation of the subsequent review, evidence of insurance, feasibility study, business plan for the development, take-out commitments, signed commitment letter, plans for common area improvements and required developer contributions to public infrastructure, and governing documents for condominium and homeowners associations. Documentation standards separately require financial statements for the borrower, builder, proposed prime tenant, take-out lender, and guarantors, credit and trade checks, plans and specifications, building permit, survey, title search, ground leases, and assigned tenant leases or letters of intent.
- The OCC handbook's construction documentation list adds appraisals on an as-is and as-completed or as-stabilized basis stating when stabilized occupancy is expected, project plans, a feasibility study, and a construction budget showing development plans, project costs, marketing plans, and the borrower's equity contribution, with a detailed cost analysis separating land development and hard costs from indirect and soft costs. Where internal expertise is unavailable, an independent qualified professional must review the plans, budget, and third-party reports, and that review must be documented.

### Valuation basis discipline

- The Interagency Appraisal and Evaluation Guidelines require that for a transaction financing construction or renovation, the institution generally requests the current "as is" market value plus, as applicable, prospective market value upon completion and upon stabilization. Prospective value opinions must be based on current and reasonably expected market conditions with a stated point of reference to those conditions and time frame, per USPAP Statement 4 and Advisory Opinion 17.
- Prospective market value "as completed" is the value as of expected completion; "as stabilized" is the value as of the time the property is projected to achieve stabilized occupancy, defined as the occupancy level expected after exposure to the market for lease over a reasonable period at comparable terms.
- For proposed and partially leased rental developments the appraiser must apply deductions and discounts reflecting that stabilized occupancy has not been achieved, including leasing commissions, rent losses, tenant improvements, and entrepreneurial profit if not already in the discount rate, and must consider absorption of the unleased space. The sum of retail sales for a proposed development is not the market value.
- The OCC handbook adds the governing feasibility statement: construction costs that closely approach or exceed the expected value of the project's income generally indicate the project is not feasible.

### Return metrics and value creation conventions

- Prologis publishes the three definitions the market borrows: weighted average stabilized yield is estimated NOI assuming stabilized occupancy divided by total expected investment; estimated value creation is stabilized value above TEI; estimated weighted average margin is value creation less estimated closing costs and taxes on properties expected to be sold or contributed, divided by TEI.
- Prologis reported for 2025: 41 new development buildings started with $2,943 million of TEI, of which 60.9% was build-to-suit by TEI, versus 28.6% build-to-suit in 2024; 40 buildings stabilized with $2,271 million of TEI, a 6.7% weighted average stabilized yield against 6.2% in 2024, $577 million of estimated value creation, and a 25.4% estimated weighted average margin against 19.2% in 2024. The company notes margins fluctuate with cost of capital, capitalization rates used to estimate value at completion, and whether a project is build-to-suit or speculative.
- Directional and dated. The build-to-suit share is the more decision-relevant number for an IC memo than the margin: it says how much of the year's institutional development volume carried demand risk retired before start.

### Risk taxonomy for development

- AvalonBay's FY2025 development and construction risk factor enumerates the categories a memo should carry: abandoned pursuits and unrecovered pursuit costs including land impairment; occupancy and rents failing to meet original expectations because of market change or competing deliveries; inability to obtain or delay in obtaining zoning, occupancy, or other governmental or third-party permits; costs exceeding original estimates from material, labor, or supply chain change including tariffs and immigration enforcement; inability to complete on schedule or at projected cost, increasing construction and financing costs; third-party liabilities during the development process; failure to perform by third-party developers, subcontractors, or key suppliers producing delays, overruns, workmanship defects, or litigation; and accessibility compliance liability under the ADA and Fair Housing Act.
- AvalonBay also describes the land control practice an IC should look for: options and long-term conditional contracts that allow an interest in the site to be acquired after entitlements are complete and shortly before construction start, which reduces development risk and preserves capital, contrasted with acquiring and holding land through entitlement.
- The OCC's warning indicator list converts directly into IC conditions and post-approval reporting: declining sales prices or rental rates, liberal concessions, slower absorption than anticipated, increasing vacancy, changes to the initial concept or development plan, budget overruns and reallocation requests between line items, draw requests ahead of work completed, construction delays, liens from payment disputes, and borrower requests for additional financing.

### Stress testing and sensitivity

- The OCC handbook directs that the analysis of cash inflows and outflows across the development period "should include stress testing to analyze sensitivity to changing economic conditions under a variety of scenarios," naming absorption rates, interest rates, and capitalization rates. That is the sourced basis for requiring an IC memo to carry at least those three axes plus cost.
- The FDIC module expects policies to address feasibility studies, risk analyses, and the sensitivity of income projections to economic variables, and requires global cash flow analysis of the borrower and guarantors with identification of primary and secondary repayment sources.
- The FDIC module also sets the take-out test: assess the validity and reliability of third-party take-out commitments, including the financial capacity of the take-out lender, the marketability of the property without a take-out, and whether the agreement automatically extends the completion date for delays beyond the builder's control.

### Budget integrity items an IC memo must not lose

- GSA P-120 requires that escalation be estimated to the midpoint of construction using the project market study as a primary data source, and that any cost impact from scope change be funded by an approved revision to project funding rather than drawn from construction contingency. It also forbids concealing an allowance or contingency inside unit pricing or quantity takeoffs.
- The AIA GMP material sets the contract facts an IC memo should state in one line: which agreement governs (A102 cost plus a fee with a GMP, A103 cost plus a fee without a GMP, A133 construction manager as constructor with a GMP), when the GMP was set relative to design completeness, whether a savings provision exists and how savings are shared, and what records the contractor must maintain to support payment applications.

### Market context, dated

- FDIC Quarterly Banking Profile, Q2 2026: bank construction and development balances of $453.5 billion, down 3.4% year over year, with a C&D noncurrent rate of 0.99% and 30-89 day past due of 0.35%. Credit performance is holding while the book shrinks.
- Federal Reserve July 2026 SLOOS: over Q2 2026 standards for construction and land development loans were basically unchanged on net, while a moderate net share of banks reported weaker CLD demand.
- ULI and PwC Emerging Trends in Real Estate 2026: of 27 subsectors, investment prospect ratings increased for 16 while development prospect ratings declined for 18; traditional office development prospects remain rated exceptionally low in both central city and suburban settings, and data centers and senior housing lead both investment and development prospect ratings.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults:

- Require every development IC memo to state three valuation bases separately: as-is, prospective as-completed, and prospective as-stabilized, each with its effective date and its point of reference to market conditions (Interagency Appraisal and Evaluation Guidelines).
- Require untrended yield on cost to be reported before trended, and require yield, value creation, and margin to use the Prologis definitions so figures are comparable across sponsors.
- Require the minimum sensitivity set to be cost, rent or absorption, interest rate, and exit capitalization rate, because those are the four the OCC names plus the cost axis implied by its feasibility test.
- Require a risks and mitigants table as a named memo section, following the MAP Guide Appendix 4 narrative convention of "actual/potential risks and mitigating factors."
- Require a conditions list that separates conditions precedent to closing from conditions precedent to construction start and reporting conditions during construction, mirroring the way the FDIC module and the OCC handbook separate origination documentation from ongoing monitoring.
- Require the memo to state the stabilization definition in use before any date or yield is compared, since AvalonBay and Prologis use "earlier of 90% occupancy or one year after completion" while Camden uses a 90% occupancy test with no backstop.
- Use the four-level LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / CRITICAL scale and the PROCEED, PROCEED_WITH_MITIGATIONS, PROCEED_WITH_CAUTION, FURTHER_DILIGENCE, REJECT recommendation vocabulary from `knowledge/risk-scoring.md` so development memos categorize risk the same way the rest of the library does.

Case-by-case only:

- Any minimum development spread. The only spread figures located are practitioner conventions circulated by an advisory firm, ranging roughly 150 to 200 bps multifamily, 175 to 250 bps industrial, 250 to 350+ bps office, and 150 to 200 bps grocery-anchored to 300+ bps speculative retail, tighter in gateway markets and wider in secondary growth markets. Publish the computed spread and the cap rate evidence behind it, not a pass mark.
- Any minimum equity multiple, IRR, or promote structure. No published, non-anonymous source establishes one.
- Any months-to-entitlement or months-to-stabilization default. Build both from the local record and the sponsor's own comparable deliveries.
- Any contingency percentage carried forward into the memo without the design stage attached, since GSA's schedule is stage-dependent and federal owner policy rather than private-market practice.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- **Attribution of the development spread conventions.** The spread ranges widely quoted in this library come from a page published by Realty Capital Analytics, a real estate financial modeling and consulting firm, at realcapanalytics.com. That is a different organization from MSCI Real Capital Analytics. This research note attributes the ranges to Realty Capital Analytics and treats them as practitioner convention. The attribution has been corrected across this pack in [Development Benchmarks](knowledge/development-benchmarks.md), [Development Budget and Yield on Cost Analyst](skills/development/development-budget-and-yield-on-cost-analyst.md), and the companion research notes; the underlying caution, that these are conventions rather than measured market data, is unchanged either way.
- **Federal owner policy versus private lender practice on contingency.** GSA P-120 sets a design-stage contingency schedule and a 7% construction contingency for new capital construction; the OCC handbook describes private construction lender practice at 5% to 10% of the overall budget. These are different bases, not a disagreement. The memo states the design stage, the contingency by line, and which anchor it is being compared to.
- **Program minimums versus market sizing.** HUD program ratios are published and firm for FHA-insured transactions; conventional construction sizing is quote-driven. Where both are present, the memo shows the binding test, not the more favorable one. Sizing mechanics belong to the construction loan skill, not to the memo.
- **Sponsor pro forma versus third-party report.** Where a sponsor projection and an appraisal, market study, or cost consultant estimate disagree, the memo reports both figures and identifies which one the recommendation relies on. The MAP Guide requires exactly this discipline in the other direction: where an underwriter rejects or modifies a technical reviewer's conclusion, the justification goes in the memorandum.
- **Institutional sentiment versus project evidence.** ULI and PwC survey ratings describe sentiment about a subsector, not the merits of a site. They belong in the market context paragraph and never substitute for submarket delivery and pre-leasing evidence.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Build-to-suit or pre-leased projects retire lease-up risk but concentrate credit risk in one tenant; the memo must carry tenant credit, lease commencement conditions, and landlord work obligations instead of an absorption curve.
- For-sale and condominium projects change the entire back half of the memo: release prices, curtailment requirements, presale definitions, and the prohibition on using aggregate retail sales prices as market value.
- Phased projects need phase-level cost, value, and approval treatment; a phase presented as part of a whole can mask a phase that does not stand on its own.
- Joint ventures and public-private partnerships put governance, promote, and major-decision rights inside the recommendation. A memo that omits who controls a budget increase or a change in scope is incomplete.
- Land held through entitlement is a materially different risk posture than land controlled by option or conditional contract closing after entitlement; the memo must say which is proposed and what the deposit-hard date is.
- Adaptive reuse and heavy redevelopment carry unknown-condition risk that a new-construction contingency level does not cover.
- Any project with a federal nexus can pull NEPA, Section 106, or program-specific review into a schedule built for local approvals only.
- A memo that recommends approval subject to conditions no one owns or dates is not a decision document. Every condition needs an owner, a date, and a test.

## Open Questions

- No public, non-anonymous benchmark was located for institutional investment committee approval rates, average time from screening to approval, or the frequency with which conditions are later waived. Those would be useful calibration and are deferred.
- Private equity and LP-side documentation standards for development approvals were not retrieved in this session; the LP reporting and due diligence conventions published by limited partner associations remain an open source to add.
- Sector-specific lease-up duration evidence for retail, office, and mixed-use ground-up projects remains unavailable from public filings, so the memo cannot supply a default absorption curve for those product types.
- Whether and how sponsors should present probability-weighted entitlement outcomes in an IC memo has no published standard; the skill requires the alternative program and the abandonment cost to be shown instead.
