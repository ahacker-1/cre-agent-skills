# Development Budget and Yield on Cost Analyst Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/development/development-budget-and-yield-on-cost-analyst.md`
- Pairs with [Development Benchmarks](knowledge/development-benchmarks.md) and [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md)
- Intended users: developers, development managers, owner representatives, construction lenders, and equity partners building or auditing a ground-up or heavy-redevelopment budget and testing whether the project clears a return hurdle

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States only. Cost indices, tariff exposure, lender program criteria, and cap rate evidence cited here are U.S. measures and do not transfer abroad.
- Deal type: ground-up development and heavy redevelopment of income-producing property across multifamily, industrial, retail, office, and mixed-use. For-sale residential runs on a different cost and profit stack and is treated as an edge case, not the base case.
- Legal and market assumptions: educational decision support only, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Bank regulatory material cited here describes supervisory expectations for U.S. federally insured depositories and is used as evidence of how construction budgets are tested, not as a rule binding any private lender or sponsor.
- Stabilized-asset operating assumptions (market rent, expense loads, reserve levels at stabilization) are out of scope and belong to the multifamily, industrial, office, and retail packs.

## Key Findings

### Budget structure is settled; budget percentages are not

- The line-item groups are consistent across a federal regulator, a federal examiner module, federal owner policy, and public REIT capitalization disclosure. The FDIC module requires cost estimates covering land and site improvement, building construction, contractor and developer profits, professional fees, and loan interest, insurance and taxes, broken down by phase rather than total project. The OCC handbook splits the same content into hard costs (on- and off-site improvements, building construction, general conditions, general contractor fees, bonding and contractor insurance) and soft costs (interest, fees, predevelopment expenses, and related-party developer fees, leasing expenses, brokerage commissions and management fees where reasonable versus third-party pricing).
- AvalonBay's 10-K supplies the most complete public statement of what belongs in total development cost: land acquisition, construction, real estate taxes, capitalized interest and loan fees, permits, professional fees, allocated development overhead, other regulatory fees, and first-generation commercial tenant improvements and leasing commissions. This is the scope the denominator of yield on cost should match.
- What is settled is the taxonomy. Percentage-of-budget ratios for soft costs, FF&E, or developer fee are not published by any of these sources and should not be defaulted.

### Three items are explicitly excluded from a construction budget

The OCC handbook names them: interest or preferred returns payable to equity partners or subordinated debt holders, the developer's general corporate overhead, and selling costs funded out of sales proceeds such as brokerage commissions and closing costs. Finding any of these inside a hard or soft cost line is a budget-integrity flag, not a style preference.

### Contingency has two credible anchors that disagree by design

- OCC, describing private-market practice: contingency "usually range between 5 and 10 percent of the overall budget."
- GSA, as federal owner policy: design contingency scaled by design stage (10% at planning and development, 7.5-10% at concept, 5-7.5% at design development, 2-5% at 75% construction documents, 1-3% at 90% construction documents, 0% at final documents; renovation and alteration and complex projects run higher, up to 15% at planning for complex non-capital work), plus a separate construction contingency of 7% of the estimated cost of construction at award for new capital construction and 10% for modernization and repair and alteration.

These are not in conflict once the base is read carefully: OCC measures against the overall budget, GSA measures design contingency against direct cost and construction contingency against cost at award. GSA is also explicit that allowances, contingencies, and reserves are different things under ASTM E2168, that design contingency falls away as documents complete, and that scope change must be funded by an approved budget revision rather than by contingency.

### Yield on cost, margin, and value creation have a public, auditable definition

Prologis states the arithmetic directly: stabilized yield is estimated NOI assuming stabilized occupancy divided by total expected investment, and margin is estimated value creation, less estimated closing costs and taxes on properties expected to be sold or contributed, divided by TEI. The 2025 disclosure gives a full worked set: $2,271M TEI, 6.7% weighted average stabilized yield, $2,848M estimated value at completion, 25.4% estimated weighted average margin, $577M estimated value creation, against 6.2% and 19.2% in 2024. This is a specific sponsor in a specific sector and year; it is a definitional anchor and a magnitude check, not a hurdle.

### Developer profit is a feasibility test, not a residual courtesy

The OCC handbook frames it plainly: the difference between prospective market value and cost to construct is the developer's profit, a development that does not create that incentive is generally not feasible, and a project budget with modest or no developer profit leaves inadequate room for cost overruns. It also warns that cost increases do not necessarily result in an increase in value, which is the single most important sentence for change-order-driven budget growth.

### Valuation basis must be stated

Three appraisal bases matter and the OCC handbook defines each: as-is (actual physical condition, use, and zoning at the effective date), as-complete (prospective market value when development is expected to be completed), and as-stabilized (prospective market value when stabilized occupancy is projected to be achieved). Yield on cost and margin are stabilized-basis metrics and must be compared to the as-stabilized value, not the as-complete value.

### Sizing constraints bound the sources and uses

HUD Mortgagee Letter 2025-03 shows how a program lender sizes the same budget: the loan is the lesser of request, statutory limit, DSCR-supportable amount, and ratio-supportable amount. For Section 221(d)(4) new construction and substantial rehabilitation the market rate tier moved to 87% LTV/LTC at 1.15x DSCR with a 7% vacancy factor. Bank ADC sizing mechanics, HVCRE equity tests, and interest reserve structure belong to [Construction Lending Criteria](knowledge/construction-lending-criteria.md) and the sibling loan-sizing skill.

### Escalation is project-specific and the indices disagree

Turner reported its index at 1552 in Q2 2026, +1.44% quarterly and +5.15% year over year. RLB reported 288.58 in Q2 2026 with roughly 1% quarterly escalation and annual city growth from Chicago at 1.42% to Honolulu at 5.93%. Both are national aggregates of different baskets. GSA's method is the defensible one for a single project: escalate to the midpoint of construction using a market study, and where a cost-loaded schedule exists, escalate by activity.

### The macro backdrop argues for stress cases, not for optimism

Census reported total construction put in place at $2,157.6B SAAR in July 2026, down 0.5% month over month and 3.8% year over year. CBRE's H1 2026 Cap Rate Survey found the all-property average essentially flat with roughly 60% of respondents expecting no change over the next six months and more expecting increases than in the prior survey. Neither supports an exit cap assumption tighter than the current going-in cap.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults, each traceable:

|---|---|---|

| Trended yield on cost | Stabilized NOI at projected delivery-date rents and expenses / total development cost | Same formula, forecast inputs; must disclose the growth assumption |
| Development spread | (Yield on cost - market cap rate), stated in basis points | MSCI RCA; cap rate must be submarket-specific and quality-matched |
| Development margin | (Stabilized value - total development cost) / total development cost, net of closing costs and taxes where a sale or contribution is planned | Prologis margin definition |
| Value creation | Stabilized value - total development cost | Prologis |

| Profit multiple on equity | Total equity distributions / total equity contributed | Standard equity multiple per [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md) |

| Excluded from construction budget | Equity preferred returns, sponsor corporate overhead, sale-funded selling and closing costs | OCC |

Case-by-case only, not defaults:

- Any soft cost, FF&E, financing cost, or developer fee expressed as a percentage of hard cost or of total cost. No source in this set publishes one.
- Any cost per square foot or per unit target. The AvalonBay 2025 completions alone span $330 to $627 per rentable sq ft.
- Any minimum development spread. The Realty Capital Analytics ranges are practitioner conventions, and the same page frames retail two ways depending on subtype.
- Any minimum profit margin. Prologis's 25.4% is one sponsor, one sector, one year.
- Escalation rate. Turner and RLB disagree by construction; city-level dispersion is larger than the national number.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Contingency: OCC's 5-10% of overall budget versus GSA's stage-based design contingency plus 7% or 10% construction contingency. Resolved by base and by role rather than by preference. GSA's structure (two separate lines, design contingency retiring as documents complete, construction contingency measured against cost at award) is the better authoring discipline; OCC's range is the better sanity check on the total a private construction lender expects to see. The skill presents both with their bases stated.
- Retail development spread: the Realty Capital Analytics page frames grocery-anchored retail at 150-200 bps and speculative retail above 300 bps, while an earlier draft of [Development Benchmarks](knowledge/development-benchmarks.md) recorded a single 200-300 bps and up convention for retail. Neither is measured market data. Resolved in favor of the subtype-dependent framing, which is what the source actually publishes; the knowledge base now carries the grocery-anchored versus speculative split, and every output must still show the computed spread and the cap rate evidence behind it rather than a pass or fail against a convention.
- Land value method: the California BOE land residual technique capitalizes residual income to the land after deducting income attributable to improvements, which is an appraisal allocation method. The developer residual land value used in this skill solves a target-return equation for a maximum land price. These answer different questions and are kept separate; the skill uses the developer version and says so.
- Escalation magnitude: Turner at +5.15% year over year versus RLB at roughly 1% per quarter with wide city dispersion. Resolved by refusing a national default and adopting GSA's project-specific, escalate-to-midpoint method.
- Institutional cost frames versus a specific project: the public REIT per-unit and per-sq-ft figures are gateway-weighted or Sunbelt-weighted portfolio outputs. They are used only as order-of-magnitude checks; a local cost estimate or subcontractor pricing always controls.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- For-sale residential. The NAHB cost stack (finished lot 13.7%, construction 64.4%, financing 1.5%, overhead 5.7%, marketing 0.8%, commission 2.8%, profit 11.0% of sale price) does not translate to income-property yield on cost. For-sale and condo projects should be evaluated on gross margin, sellout absorption, and unit release provisions, not on a stabilized NOI divided by cost.
- Mixed-use. First-generation commercial tenant improvements and leasing commissions belong in project cost (AvalonBay capitalizes them), and the commercial component usually carries a different cap rate than the residential component. A blended cap rate applied to a blended NOI hides the spread on each component.
- Adaptive reuse and heavy redevelopment. GSA's renovation contingencies run materially above new construction at every design stage, and its construction contingency for modernization is 10% versus 7%. Applying new-construction contingency to a reuse project is a systematic under-budget.
- Ground lease structures. Residual land value has no meaning where land is not purchased; the equivalent test is the maximum supportable ground rent, and the ground rent must appear as an operating expense in stabilized NOI.
- Phased projects. The FDIC module requires cost breakdowns by phase rather than on a total project basis. A single blended yield across phases can conceal a phase that never clears the hurdle.
- Interest reserve. The OCC handbook cautions that the presence of an interest reserve may not accurately reflect a borrower's ability to pay, and that refunding depleted reserves may indicate an underperforming construction process. A budget whose interest reserve was sized to an optimistic schedule is a budget gap waiting to appear.
- Tariff-exposed scopes. Turner and RLB both flag tariff policy as a live cost variable in 2026. Structural steel, metal panel, aluminum glazing, copper wiring, switchgear, elevators, and rooftop mechanical equipment should be priced and stressed separately; the federal metals regime is documented in [Development Benchmarks](knowledge/development-benchmarks.md).
- Related-party fees. Developer fees, leasing commissions, and management fees payable to affiliates are allowable soft costs under the OCC handbook only if reasonable compared with third-party pricing. Disclose the payee relationship and whether the fee is paid current, deferred, or subordinated.
- Value is not cost. The OCC handbook states directly that cost increases do not necessarily result in an increase in value. Overrun analysis must flow through yield on cost and margin, never be netted against an assumed value increase.

## Open Questions

- No public, non-anonymous source in this set publishes market ranges for developer fee, soft cost as a share of hard cost, or FF&E per unit by product type. These remain deliberately undefaulted.
- No source in this set publishes measured development spreads actually transacted, only practitioner conventions and one sponsor's realized yields. A dataset of realized development spreads by sector and vintage would materially improve the spread guidance.
- Ground-up lease-up duration for retail, office, and mixed-use is not benchmarked in public filings the way multifamily and industrial are, so the trended side of yield on cost carries more forecast risk in those sectors.
- Sector-specific stabilized expense loads are deferred to the multifamily, industrial, office, and retail packs and are not re-derived here.
