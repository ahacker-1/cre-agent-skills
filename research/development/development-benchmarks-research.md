# Development Benchmarks Research

## Purpose

- Supports `knowledge/development-benchmarks.md`
- Also supports the Development and Construction pack skills that consume budget, yield, schedule, and absorption frames: `skills/development/development-budget-and-yield-on-cost-analyst.md`, `skills/development/lease-up-and-stabilization-pro-forma.md`, `skills/development/schedule-and-delivery-risk-tracker.md`, and `skills/development/development-ic-memo-writer.md`
- Intended users: developers, development managers, owner representatives, construction lenders, and equity partners evaluating U.S. ground-up and heavy redevelopment projects

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States only. Cost indices, tariff rules, entitlement cost surveys, and REIT pipeline data cited here are U.S.-specific. Canadian and international RLB or ULI series are excluded.
- Deal type: ground-up development and heavy redevelopment across multifamily, industrial, retail, office, and mixed-use. Stabilized-asset operating assumptions are out of scope and belong to the multifamily, industrial, office, and retail packs.
- Legal and market assumptions: entitlement processes, impact fees, and exaction practice are set at state and local level and vary enormously. Federal cost-estimating policy is cited as a structured reference anchor, not as a private-market rule. Tariff schedules change by proclamation and must be re-checked at the date of use.
- Everything time-sensitive in this note is dated. Construction cost levels, cap rates, and absorption are directional as of the access date and are not universal.

## Key Findings

### Budget taxonomy

- GSA P-120 gives an explicit, government-published total project cost stack. Estimated Total Project Cost (ETPC) equals Estimated Cost of Construction plus Art in Architecture plus Estimated Design and Review Costs plus Estimated Management and Inspection Costs plus Furnishings and Equipment plus Land Acquisition plus Financing. Within that, Estimated Cost of Construction equals direct cost plus design contingency plus general conditions and profit plus special use taxes, escalated to award, plus construction contingency.
- Public REIT definitions independently confirm the same broad stack. AvalonBay's projected total capitalized cost includes land acquisition, construction, real estate taxes, capitalized interest and loan fees, permits, professional fees, allocated development overhead, other regulatory fees, and first-generation commercial tenant improvements and leasing commissions. Prologis Total Expected Investment includes land, construction, and leasing costs.
- GSA P-120 requires that allowances and contingencies always be identified separately and never be concealed inside unit prices or quantities, following ASTM E2168 (allowance, contingency, and reserve sums) and ASTM E2083 (field requirements, office overhead, and profit).
- NAHB's 2024 for-sale survey gives a sale-price stack for single-family: finished lot including financing 13.7%, total construction cost 64.4%, financing 1.5%, overhead and general expenses 5.7%, marketing 0.8%, sales commission 2.8%, and profit 11.0%, on an average sales price of $665,298 with 2,647 sq ft of finished area.

### Contingency

- GSA design contingency, as a percent of direct cost for new capital construction, steps down by design stage: 10% at planning and development, 7.5% to 10% at concept design, 5% to 7.5% at design development, 2% to 5% at 75% construction documents, 1% to 3% at 90% construction documents, and 0% at final construction documents. Renovation and alteration work carries higher bands at every stage (10%, 10% to 15%, 7.5% to 10%, 3.5% to 7.5%, 1.5% to 3.5%, 0%).
- GSA construction contingency, as a percent of the estimated cost of construction at award, is 7% for new capital construction and 10% for modernization and repair-and-alteration work.
- GSA is explicit that construction contingency covers unforeseen conditions and design coordination, not scope additions. Scope growth must be funded by an approved budget revision.

### Escalation and cost level

- Turner Building Cost Index reached 1552 in Q2 2026, up 1.44% quarter over quarter and 5.15% year over year. Q1 2026 was 1530, up 1.32% and 4.87%.
- RLB's North America national index reached 288.58 in Q2 2026, with escalation described as settling near 1% per quarter. City-level annual growth ranged from Chicago at 1.42% to Honolulu at 5.93%.
- Mortenson's Q1 2026 national index rose 1.69% quarter over quarter and 6.77% year over year, with market dispersion from Portland at +2.55% to Denver at +10.37% year over year.
- The three indices disagree on level (roughly 4% to 7% year over year) but agree on direction and on drivers: metals, energy and transportation, and skilled mechanical and electrical labor.
- GSA's escalation method is to escalate to the midpoint of construction, with a market study as the primary data source for the escalation rate.

### Tariff and materials risk

- Proclamation 11032, signed June 1, 2026 and published in the Federal Register June 4, 2026, recites the regime set by Proclamation 11021 of April 2, 2026: a 50% ad valorem duty on products made of aluminum, steel, or copper; 25% on derivative products that tend to be predominantly composed of those metals; and a temporarily reduced 15% on a subset of derivative products, specifically fixed industrial machinery and power equipment.
- Turner, RLB, and Mortenson all named metals and tariffs as active cost drivers in their 2026 releases. The ULI and PwC 2026 outlook names tariffs and construction costs repeatedly as the binding constraint on new development, including in its industrial section.

### Entitlement and regulatory cost

- The NAHB and NMHC joint survey (fielded April 2022) found regulation averages 40.6% of total multifamily development cost, split across: costs when site work begins 8.5%, building code changes over the prior 10 years 11.1%, development requirements beyond the ordinary 5.4%, fees at construction authorization 4.4%, cost of applying for zoning approval 3.2%, affordability mandates 2.7%, other labor regulations 2.6%, land dedicated to government or left unbuilt 2.4%, and pure cost of delay 0.5%.
- The same survey found neighborhood opposition present for 74.5% of respondents, adding an average 5.6% to development cost and 7.4 months of delay when present.

### Cost per unit and per square foot

- AvalonBay's 24 communities under construction at December 31, 2025 carried $3,307M of projected total capitalized cost across 8,572 apartment homes, about $386,000 per home, weighted toward coastal and gateway markets.
- AvalonBay's four 2025 completions show total capitalized cost per rentable square foot of $330 (Hunt Valley, MD), $416 (Princeton, NJ), $446 (Annapolis, MD), and $627 (South Miami, FL, which includes 32,000 sq ft of commercial space).
- Camden's three communities under construction at December 31, 2025 carried $492.0M of estimated cost across 1,162 homes, about $423,000 per home, in Charlotte and Nashville.
- NAHB's 2024 single-family survey implies roughly $162 per finished square foot of construction cost ($428,215 over 2,647 sq ft), excluding lot.

### Yield on cost and development spread

- Prologis publishes the cleanest primary definition: weighted average stabilized yield equals estimated NOI assuming stabilized occupancy divided by Total Expected Investment. Its 2025 stabilizations came in at 6.7% (2024: 6.2%), with an estimated weighted average margin of 25.4% (2024: 19.2%). Margin is estimated value creation, less closing costs and taxes on properties expected to be sold or contributed, divided by TEI.
- Realty Capital Analytics states the arithmetic (development spread equals yield on cost less market cap rate) and quotes practitioner target ranges of roughly 150 to 200 bps for multifamily, 175 to 250 bps for industrial, 250 to 350+ bps for office, and 150 to 200 bps for grocery-anchored retail rising above 300 bps for speculative retail, tighter in gateway markets and wider in secondary growth markets. These are conventions, not measured market data. The publisher is realcapanalytics.com, a modeling and consulting firm, and not MSCI Real Capital Analytics; the two are distinct organizations.
- CBRE's H1 2026 Cap Rate Survey (published August 12, 2026) reported the all-property average cap rate essentially flat while the 10-year Treasury peaked at 4.67% in mid-May 2026. A trade summary of the same survey reported going-in cap rates of about 5.2% industrial, 5.3% multifamily, 6.4% office, and 6.4% retail.

### Schedule duration

- Computed from AvalonBay's 24-project under-construction table (10-K, FY2025): construction start to estimated completion runs a median of 10 quarters, range 4 to 18. Estimated completion to estimated stabilized operations runs a median of 2 quarters, range 1 to 4. Start to stabilization runs a median of 12 quarters, range 8 to 20. AvalonBay defines stabilization as the earlier of 90% physical occupancy or the one-year anniversary of completion, and initial occupancy typically precedes completion, so lease-up begins before the completion date.
- Camden defines stabilization as reaching 90% occupancy with no one-year backstop. Its three 2025 actual stabilizations reached 90% occupancy 3 to 4 quarters after construction completion (Woodmill Creek 2Q24 to 2Q25, Durham 4Q24 to 3Q25, Long Meadow Farms 4Q24 to 4Q25). Its three under-construction projects forecast 4 to 7 quarters from completion to stabilization.
- Prologis defines stabilization as the earlier of one year after completion or 90% occupancy, matching AvalonBay's structure.

### Absorption and market backdrop

- Multifamily, Q2 2026 (CBRE, published July 29, 2026): vacancy 4.3%, net absorption 167,000 units, completions 77,700 units down 14% year over year, average rent $2,257 with 0.5% year-over-year growth. Absorption outpaced completions.
- Industrial, Q2 2026 (CBRE, published July 29, 2026): vacancy 6.5% (first decline since Q2 2022), net absorption 85.1M sq ft, completions 47.9M sq ft (lowest quarterly total since 2016), 252.2M sq ft under construction, 58M sq ft of starts (highest in three years).
- Aggregate activity is contracting. Census reported total construction put in place of $2,157.6B SAAR in July 2026, down 0.5% month over month and 3.8% year over year, with year-to-date spending down 3.5%.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults, traceable to the sources above:

- Budget taxonomy with eight line groups: land and acquisition, hard costs (direct cost plus general conditions plus contractor fee plus escalation), soft costs, FF&E, financing costs, contingency (design plus construction, stated separately), developer fee, and reserves. Traceable to GSA P-120 ETPC plus AvalonBay and Prologis capitalized-cost definitions.
- Contingency must be shown as two separate lines (design and construction) and must never be embedded in unit prices. Traceable to GSA P-120 and ASTM E2168.
- Design contingency stepping down by design stage, and construction contingency of 7% for new build versus 10% for renovation, are usable as reference anchors, explicitly labeled as federal owner policy rather than private-market rule.
- Escalate hard costs to the construction midpoint, not to the start date. Traceable to GSA P-120.
- Yield on cost equals stabilized NOI divided by total development cost. Traceable to the Prologis 10-K and to Realty Capital Analytics.
- Development spread equals yield on cost minus a current, market-supported cap rate, stated in basis points. Traceable to Realty Capital Analytics.
- Multifamily lease-up from construction completion to 90% occupancy of roughly 3 to 7 quarters is a defensible directional band for institutional garden and mid-rise product, traceable to Camden actual and forecast intervals, with AvalonBay's shorter reported intervals explained by its one-year definitional backstop and earlier initial occupancy.
- Construction duration for institutional multifamily of roughly 8 to 12 quarters, median 10, traceable to the AvalonBay pipeline table.

Must remain case-by-case, stated explicitly in the knowledge base:

- Soft costs as a percent of hard costs. No source in this set publishes a general commercial soft-cost ratio. The NAHB shares apply to for-sale single-family only and cannot be transferred to commercial development.
- Developer fee percentage. No source in this set publishes a range.
- Operating and interest reserve sizing. Governed by lender structure, covered by `knowledge/construction-lending-criteria.md`.
- Untrended versus trended yield on cost. The distinction is a modeling convention, not a sourced benchmark. The repo convention is to compute both and let the untrended test control, and to say so.
- Absolute cost per square foot or per unit for any given project. The figures above are specific companies, specific markets, and specific dates.
- Spread targets by property type. The Realty Capital Analytics ranges are practitioner convention, and retail is subtype-dependent (grocery-anchored versus speculative). Treat them as directional and require the user to test against live cap rate evidence.

Formulas adopted:

- Yield on cost = stabilized NOI / total development cost.
- Development spread (bps) = (yield on cost - market cap rate) x 10,000.
- Development margin = (stabilized value - total development cost) / total development cost, net of closing costs and taxes where a sale or contribution is planned. Traceable to the Prologis margin definition.
- Residual land value = (stabilized NOI / target yield on cost) - all non-land development costs - required developer profit. This is the developer residual, distinct from the appraisal land residual technique of income capitalization.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Cost escalation level. Turner reported 5.15% year over year for Q2 2026, RLB described roughly 1% per quarter (about 4% annualized), and Mortenson reported 6.77% year over year for Q1 2026. These measure different baskets, geographies, and building types. Resolution: do not adopt a single national escalation rate. Present the range with each publisher, date, and index level attached, and require project-specific escalation from a market study, consistent with GSA P-120.
- Stabilization definitions. Prologis and AvalonBay use "earlier of one year after completion or 90% occupancy"; Camden uses 90% occupancy with no backstop. That definitional difference alone shortens reported lease-up periods for the first two. Resolution: benchmark against Camden's actual 90% occupancy intervals for a true lease-up duration, and always state which definition a reported stabilization date uses.
- Residual land value. The California BOE land residual technique of income capitalization allocates income between land and improvements using separate capitalization rates. That is not the same calculation as the developer residual land value used in feasibility. Resolution: adopt the developer residual formula for feasibility work, cite the appraisal technique only as the related valuation concept, and never present the two as interchangeable.
- Cap rate levels. The CBRE survey page itself confirmed direction and the Treasury reference but did not surface sector-level numbers; a trade summary reported them. Resolution: sector cap rate levels are cited as a directional level check with the trade summary named, and any spread analysis must use live, market-specific cap rate evidence rather than a national average.
- Regulatory cost share. The 40.6% figure comes from a 2022 survey and is the newest joint NAHB/NMHC multifamily figure located this session. Resolution: cite with its survey date, treat it as a magnitude indicator for entitlement and regulatory drag, and do not present it as a current-year number.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Renovation and adaptive reuse. GSA carries materially higher design and construction contingency for repair-and-alteration work than for new build. Any heavy redevelopment budget using new-construction contingency levels is under-reserved.
- Single-asset cost benchmarking. AvalonBay's South Miami completion at $627 per sq ft and Hunt Valley at $330 per sq ft are the same company, same year, same property type. Cost per square foot is not portable across markets, product form, parking type, or podium versus wrap versus garden construction.
- Tariff-exposed scopes. Structural steel, metal panel, aluminum glazing, copper wiring, switchgear, and rooftop mechanical equipment carry direct exposure to the Section 232 regime. Fixed-price contracts signed before a proclamation change may still shift cost through allowances, escalation clauses, or change orders.
- Trended yield on cost. A project that only pencils on trended rents is a leverage and timing bet, not a development spread. Flag it rather than accepting it.
- Cap rate compression as a source of return. A development spread that depends on an exit cap rate below the current going-in cap rate should be stress-tested at flat and wider cap rates.
- Regulatory and opposition delay. A 7-plus month delay from neighborhood opposition compounds through carry cost, escalation to a later midpoint, and lease-up into a different market. Delay risk belongs in the budget, not only in the schedule.
- Lease-up into a heavy delivery window. Multifamily completions fell 14% year over year in Q2 2026 and industrial completions hit their lowest quarter since 2016, but submarket delivery concentration can still overwhelm a single project. National absorption does not underwrite a specific lease-up.
- Contingency drawn for scope. GSA is explicit that contingency is not a scope-change fund. A project consuming construction contingency on owner-directed scope has an unfunded budget, not a funded one.
