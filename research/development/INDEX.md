## Shared Source Themes

- Development cost is governed by an explicit, auditable stack (land, hard cost, soft cost, contingency, financing) with contingency and allowances tracked separately, not concealed inside unit prices, and design contingency stepping down from roughly 10% at planning to 0% at final construction documents as design certainty increases.
- Regulation and entitlement friction is a large, measurable share of total development cost, not a soft risk factor: NAHB/NMHC data puts regulation at 40.6% of multifamily development cost and NAHB puts it at 26.4% of an average new home's price, with community opposition adding further cost and months of delay when present.
- Construction lending is bound by supervisory LTV and LTC ceilings, HVCRE equity-contribution rules, and interest-reserve and guaranty structures that shift with milestones (completion, carry, repayment guaranties burning off on DSCR or debt-yield tests), so financeability turns on contributed equity and reserve sizing as much as headline leverage.
- Money control on a live project runs through the same mechanics regardless of contract form: schedule of values and pay applications, retainage (increasingly capped or reduced by state statute), lien waivers sequenced with funded payment, title date-downs, and a monthly in-balance test comparing cost to complete against remaining loan and equity.
