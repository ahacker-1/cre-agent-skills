## Purpose

- Supports `knowledge/workout-and-extension-structures.md`
- Defines borrower-side structure selection for extensions, modifications, forbearance, discounted payoffs, note sales, and deeds-in-lieu

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States
- Deal types: CRE loan maturity extensions, defaults, workouts, and lender negotiations
- Legal posture: informational only; counsel controls documents and advice

## Key Findings

- Workout structures must solve the specific problem: time, value, cash flow, control, or exit.
- Extensions are more credible when paired with paydown, fees, reserves, reporting, and milestones.
- Forbearance is temporary and should not be treated as permanent cure.
- Deed-in-lieu and discounted payoff paths require tax, guaranty, title, and release review.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

- Require a give/get table for lender concessions and borrower protections.
- Require dates and milestones rather than open-ended relief.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- CMBS workouts are governed by PSA constraints; balance-sheet lenders may have more flexibility.
- Legal sources inform issue spotting only, not legal advice.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Borrower asks after default rather than before maturity.
- Relief request lacks sponsor contribution or recovery-maximizing rationale.

## Open Questions

- Actual loan document provisions and lender authority must be reviewed deal by deal.
