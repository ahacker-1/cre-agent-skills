# Retail Lease Abstract Reviewer Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/retail/retail-lease-abstract-reviewer.md`
- Pairs with `knowledge/retail-lease-structures.md` and `knowledge/retail-tenant-sales-and-occupancy-cost.md`; those two files carry the reusable definitions and thresholds, and this note documents only what the abstraction skill itself needs to defend
- Intended users: acquisition, asset management, leasing, and lender diligence teams converting U.S. retail leases, amendments, and REA obligations into a decision-ready abstract

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States
- Asset types: grocery-anchored, power, strip / unanchored, lifestyle, mall, mixed-use retail, pad, and single-tenant net lease (STNL)
- Deal type: acquisition diligence, refinancing, and asset review; not development entitlement or construction administration
- Legal assumptions: lease and REA interpretation, and the enforceability of co-tenancy, radius, continuous-operation, liquidated-damages, and guaranty provisions, are state-law specific. This research supports issue spotting only and is not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice.
- Bankruptcy assumptions: federal Bankruptcy Code as codified at 11 U.S.C. 365; shopping-center-specific provisions at 365(b)(3) apply only where the premises qualify as being in a "shopping center"
- Accounting assumptions: U.S. GAAP, where percentage rent and variable recoveries are variable lease consideration

## Key Findings

### The document stack, not the lease, is the unit of review

- Estoppel certificates routinely become the controlling factual record. The ICSC 2022 SNDA and estoppel materials state that facts recited in an estoppel can be conclusively presumed true against the signer even where the recitation misstates the lease, and describe a case in which a tenant's "none" answer on renewal options was litigated. An abstract that disagrees with a signed estoppel is a diligence exception, not a rounding difference.
- The same materials show why unfunded landlord obligations must be abstracted separately: lender SNDA forms commonly carve out unpaid tenant allowances so a foreclosing lender does not assume them, and where the SNDA is silent the issue has been litigated as an ambiguity.

### Economics: base rent, percentage rent, and the sales definition

- Holland & Knight sets the arithmetic: natural breakpoint equals annual minimum rent divided by the percentage rate, with a worked example of $140,000 at 7% producing a $2,000,000 breakpoint. An artificial breakpoint is any negotiated figure that decouples the two.
- Gross sales is a negotiated defined term. Holland & Knight lists common exclusions: customer refunds, employee discounts, accommodation sales, coin-operated devices, third-party credit card commissions, inter-store transfers, returns to manufacturers, and sales taxes collected.
- Percentage rent is a real but small revenue line in open-air retail and a real risk line in enclosed formats. Federal Realty's FY2025 10-K describes leases structured for minimum rents subject to periodic increases, percentage rents based on the level of tenant sales, and reimbursement of a majority of on-site operating expenses and real estate taxes.
- Term conventions differ by space size. Regency's FY2025 10-K states that leases for tenant space under 10,000 SF generally have initial terms of three to seven years, while leases greater than 10,000 SF (its "Anchor Leases") generally have initial terms in excess of five years. Federal Realty's FY2025 10-K states commercial leases generally range from three to ten years, with anchor leases potentially longer.
- Option rent is not automatically market rent. Federal Realty states that many leases contain extension options at pre-established rates that often include fixed increases, CPI adjustments, or other market rate adjustments.

### Recoveries, caps, and fees

- Regency's FY2025 10-K describes the standard open-air structure as fixed base rent plus the tenant's pro rata share of real estate taxes, insurance, and CAM, with reimbursement for utility costs if not directly metered.
- Cox Castle's CAM piece supplies the negotiating ranges an abstract should test against: administrative fees often 10-15% of other CAM expenses; third-party management fees often 2.5-4% of gross project revenues; capital expenditures commonly excluded for the initial 5-7 years of the term in new development; audit costs shifted to the landlord at a 3-5% overstatement; audits typically limited to once per year; caps drafted on a cumulative and compounding basis so unused cap room carries forward.
- Denominator convention follows format: regional malls commonly exclude anchors above a stated size, power and neighborhood centers commonly use a floor-area ratio, and ground-leased parcels commonly use a land-area ratio (Cox Castle).

### Control rights that move value

- Co-tenancy comes in two forms with different lives. The ICSC Shopping Center Legal Update article distinguishes an opening co-tenancy, which expires once satisfied, from an ongoing co-tenancy that runs the full term and typically triggers after the condition persists for a defined period in the range of 120 to 365 days. It describes landlord sunsets that force an election between terminating and resuming full rent, typically 12 to 24 months after the violation begins, and recapture rights as the alternative structure.
- Cox Castle's co-tenancy piece supplies the sizing conventions: key tenants are usually named anchors or mini-majors in roughly the 15,000 to 30,000 SF range, or occupancy thresholds such as 75% of shop spaces or at least 100,000 SF; landlord cure periods run at least six months and often a year; abatement remedies are commonly around 50% of rent; and after about a year of violation the tenant returns to full rent or the landlord recaptures.
- Regency's FY2025 10-K confirms the variants in an issuer's own words: co-tenancy clauses may let a tenant postpone opening, close before expiration if another tenant closes, or, more commonly, pay reduced rent until a certain number of tenants open. It also flags shadow anchors, retailers owning or occupying space adjacent to but not within the center, whose vacancy reduces traffic without appearing in the rent roll.
- Exclusives need scope, carve-outs, and a remedy. Hunton lists the required elements: a prohibition on landlord leasing in violation, a covenant to notify incoming tenants, an obligation to take actionable steps to remedy, and express remedies. It warns against overbreadth and advises distinguishing predominant from incidental use. The ICSC article adds remedy formulations seen in practice, including reduced rent of 2-4% of gross sales capped at the otherwise payable monthly rent, or 50% of base rent, plus termination if the violation is not cured, and describes "rogue tenant" provisions that cut off the remedy where the violating tenant's own lease already prohibits the use.
- Prohibited-use provisions are a separate item from exclusives. The ICSC article notes damages are harder to prove, remedies are less commonly express, and landlords have more success obtaining sunsets and recapture rights there.
- Continuous operation and go-dark are enforcement problems, not drafting problems. The ICSC 2025 operating covenants workshop states that courts disfavor specific performance because of the supervision burden and prefer damages, termination, or recapture; that liquidated damages are generally enforced where actual damages are hard to calculate and the formula is a reasonable estimate; and that where percentage rent is the sole or primary rent, courts may imply a duty to operate.
- Relocation rights are negotiable in structure and often limited in practice. Cox Castle's relocation piece lists notice, seasonal blackout periods such as holiday and back-to-school, frequency caps such as no more than twice per term, size and frontage and view-line parameters, landlord payment of actual out-of-pocket relocation costs with lost profits excluded, rent held flat or reduced if the new space is smaller, and tenant approval or termination outs. It also flags the practical test: how many other spaces in the center actually satisfy the criteria.
- Kick-out design controls whether the right is real. Woods Aitken advises against measuring ramp-up months or partial years and stresses defining the election window and the interval between election and effective termination.
- Guaranties are read for scope, not for the name on them. Cole Schotz reports the New York Court of Appeals decision in 1995 CAM LLC v. West Side Advisors LLC (2025) holding that a good guy guarantor's liability can end when the tenant surrenders as the lease requires, without landlord acceptance, unless the guaranty says otherwise, and advises auditing existing forms.

### REA / OEA, parking, and signage

- The ICSC REA materials describe the REA as the governing regime for a multi-parcel unified development: easements plus controls over construction, maintenance, architectural theme, prohibited, restricted and exclusive uses, site plan, and signage.
- Parking is set at the REA level, not only in the lease: owners specify the parking ratio to be maintained on each tract, often higher ratios for restaurant and theater uses and a separate ratio for office or residential in mixed-use, and usually require each tract to be self-parked rather than borrowing another owner's stalls.
- Sign rights typically travel through easements: access and maintenance easements for a panel on a shared pylon or monument, an electrical easement to power an owner's own sign, and a covenant against landscaping, signs, or structures that block visibility.
- Site plan control is where redevelopment plans die: anchors may fix permissible building areas, require the remainder to stay common area as shown on the site plan, and restrict changes to roadways, drives, parking, and access points without consent from multiple approving parties.

### Bankruptcy provisions that belong in the abstract

- 11 U.S.C. 365(b)(3) requires, for a shopping center lease, adequate assurance of the source of rent; that an assignee's financial condition and operating performance be similar to the debtor's as of lease signing, including guarantors; that percentage rent will not decline substantially; that assumption or assignment remain subject to radius, location, use, and exclusivity provisions and not breach any such provision in another lease, financing agreement, or master agreement for the center; and that it not disrupt tenant mix or balance.
- 11 U.S.C. 365(d)(4) deems a nonresidential lease rejected if not assumed or rejected by the earlier of 120 days after the order for relief or plan confirmation, extendable once by 90 days for cause, with any further extension requiring the lessor's prior written consent. 365(d)(3) requires timely performance of post-petition obligations.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults, because each traces to a cited source:

- Natural breakpoint = annual minimum rent / percentage rate; percentage rent due = (gross sales - breakpoint) x percentage rate (Holland & Knight). Already stated in `knowledge/retail-lease-structures.md`; the skill points there rather than restating derivations.
- Administrative fee 10-15% of other CAM, third-party management fee 2.5-4% of gross project revenues, audit cost shifting at 3-5% overstatement, annual audit frequency, capital exclusion in the first 5-7 years of a new development (Cox Castle). Treat as negotiating ranges to test against, never as market rules.
- Co-tenancy sizing and timing screens: key tenant 15,000-30,000 SF or occupancy thresholds such as 75% of shop space or 100,000 SF; ongoing co-tenancy trigger periods of 120-365 days; landlord cure of six months to a year; abatement commonly around 50%; sunset or forced election at roughly 12-24 months (Cox Castle; ICSC Shopping Center Legal Update).
- Exclusive-use violation remedy formulations of 2-4% of gross sales capped at monthly rent, or 50% of base rent, plus termination on failure to cure (ICSC Shopping Center Legal Update).
- Lease term screens: under 10,000 SF generally three to seven years; over 10,000 SF generally in excess of five years; commercial leases broadly three to ten years with anchors longer (Regency; Federal Realty).
- Bankruptcy deadlines and the shopping-center adequate assurance list are statutory and stated as such (11 U.S.C. 365).

Case-by-case only, with no repo default:

- Percentage rates, artificial breakpoint levels, radius mileage, kick-out sales thresholds, occupancy cost limits, termination fee amounts, and relocation cost caps. These are pure negotiation outputs. The skill instructs the reviewer to read and report them, never to assume them.
- Whether a given lease's recovery structure is pro rata, fixed, or capped. The label in the rent roll is not evidence.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Where practitioner commentary and an issuer filing describe the same structure differently, the filing wins for what the market actually does at scale and the commentary wins for what the clause language means. Regency and Federal Realty describe the standard structure; Cox Castle and ICSC explain the variations that break it.
- Cox Castle's CAM piece describes caps drafted on a cumulative and compounding basis, while `knowledge/retail-lease-structures.md` documents the cumulative versus non-cumulative distinction from Lowndes. These are not in conflict: cumulative is the landlord-preferred drafting position, and the reviewer must confirm which one the subject lease contains.
- Cox Castle's co-tenancy timing (cure of six months to a year, forced election at about a year) and the ICSC article's timing (trigger at 120-365 days, sunset at 12-24 months) overlap but do not match exactly. Both are reported as ranges rather than reconciled into a single number, because they describe different negotiated positions rather than a standard.
- On co-tenancy enforceability, state law controls and the sources are jurisdiction-specific. The California authorities discussed in `knowledge/retail-lease-structures.md` are not treated here as national rules.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Do not hard-code any occupancy threshold, radius distance, percentage rate, or kick-out sales floor. Every one of these is negotiated per lease.
- A single-tenant net lease or pad may have no percentage rent, no co-tenancy, and no CAM participation at all. Forcing the shopping-center abstract template onto it produces false negatives; abstract the ground lease reversion, leasehold financeability, and reset mechanics instead.
- Shadow anchors sit outside the rent roll and outside the REA in some centers, yet can trigger co-tenancy conditions (Regency FY2025 10-K). Confirm which named co-tenants the landlord does not control.
- An estoppel that contradicts the abstract is the higher-risk document, not the lower one, because the signer may be bound by it (ICSC 2022 workshop).
- Unfunded landlord work is not automatically a liability of a foreclosing lender; SNDA forms often carve it out (ICSC 2022 workshop). Flag it for both the seller and the lender rather than assuming who pays.
- 365(b)(3) applies to leases in a "shopping center." Whether a given pad, mixed-use ground floor, or freestanding store qualifies is a legal determination for counsel.
- Guaranty scope, release triggers, and the identity of the signing entity require counsel and state-law review; the reported New York good guy guaranty holding is New York law.
