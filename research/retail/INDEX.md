## Shared Source Themes

- Occupancy cost is category-dependent, not a single universal threshold: grocery runs roughly 2% to 7% of sales, power-center anchors around 10%, and mall shop bands scale with sales productivity per square foot, so a headline occupancy-cost figure must be read against format and tenant category.
- Retail lease and recovery structures diverge sharply by tenant role - anchor terms of 10 to 20 years against 5 to 10 for shops, fixed CAM increasingly standard, and thin percentage rent outside malls - while co-tenancy, go-dark, and radius/exclusive enforceability remain REA-specific and state-law dependent.
- Retail credit is the healthier side of the CRE ledger but uneven by lender lane and format: CMBS surveillance thresholds, bank concentration supervision, and STNL's separate credit-tenant financing universe each apply different tests, and percentage/overage rent generally does not create loan proceeds.
- National fundamentals (low vacancy, positive absorption, rising rents, retail CMBS delinquency near the middle of the property-type pack) mask real bifurcation between necessity and grocery-anchored strength versus legacy mall and outlet distress, with Bankruptcy Code mechanics (11 U.S.C. 365(d)(4) and 502(b)(6)) setting hard floors on anchor-failure recovery and rejection-damages exposure.
