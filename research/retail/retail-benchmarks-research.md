# Retail Benchmarks Research

## Purpose

- Supports `knowledge/retail-benchmarks.md`
- Provides the format definitions, occupancy, rent, leasing-capital, occupancy-cost, and cap-rate reference points used across the Retail v1 pack skills
- Intended users: acquisition, asset management, and credit teams underwriting U.S. retail centers and single-tenant net lease (STNL) assets

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States. Foreign portfolio data reported by the same issuers (Simon Japan / Europe, Realty Income U.K. and Europe, Tanger Canada) is excluded from every benchmark below.
- Deal type: acquisition, refinancing, and asset review of grocery-anchored, power, strip / unanchored, lifestyle, mall, outlet, mixed-use, pad, and STNL retail.
- Market assumptions: retail is lease-driven and sales-driven. Center-level benchmarks are directional starting points only; the controlling evidence is the trade-area comp set, the actual rent roll, and reported tenant sales.
- Legal and accounting assumptions: none of this is legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. REIT-reported metrics are defined by each issuer and are not standardized across companies.

## Key Findings

### Format definitions

- ICSC's U.S. classification separates general-purpose centers (super-regional mall, regional mall, community center, neighborhood center, strip / convenience) from specialized-purpose centers (power center, lifestyle, factory outlet, theme / festival) and limited-purpose property (airport retail).
- ICSC ranges, as printed: super-regional mall 800,000+ SF, 60-120 acres, 3+ anchors, 50-70% anchor GLA, 5-25 mile trade area; regional mall 400,000-800,000 SF, 2+ anchors, 50-70% anchor GLA, 40-80 stores, 5-15 miles; community center 125,000-400,000 SF, 2+ anchors, 40-60% anchor GLA, 15-40 stores, 3-6 miles; neighborhood center 30,000-125,000 SF, 1+ anchor, 30-50% anchor GLA, 5-20 stores, supermarket anchor, 3 miles; strip / convenience under 30,000 SF, anchor-less or small convenience anchor, under 1 mile; power center 250,000-600,000 SF, 25-80 acres, 3+ anchors, 70-90% anchor GLA, 5-10 miles; lifestyle 150,000-500,000 SF, 10-40 acres, 0-2 anchors, 0-50% anchor GLA, 8-12 miles; factory outlet 50,000-400,000 SF, 25-75 miles; theme / festival 80,000-250,000 SF, 25-75 miles.
- ICSC does not define a separate "grocery-anchored," "pad," "mixed-use retail," or "STNL" category. Grocery-anchored is a tenancy description that usually maps to the neighborhood or community center size band. Pad and STNL are ownership and lease-structure descriptions, not ICSC formats.
- Anchor GLA share is the cleanest structural separator: power centers run 70-90% anchor GLA, malls 50-70%, community centers 40-60%, neighborhood centers 30-50%, lifestyle 0-50%.

### Market context, mid-2026 (all directional and dated)

- CBRE reported the U.S. retail availability rate unchanged at 4.9% in Q2 2026, average asking rent of $24.79 PSF (up 2.4% year over year), four consecutive quarters of positive net absorption, and historically low construction completions.
- JLL reported 11.8 billion SF of existing inventory, 56.1 million SF under construction, 4.4% total vacancy, $26.02 PSF market rent, and Q2 2026 net absorption of 10.2 million SF after a negative Q1 2026 of -4.5 million SF. General retail led at roughly 7 million SF; malls and neighborhood centers turned positive after negative starts to the year. National rent growth eased to 1.7% year over year, ranging from Charlotte at +6.0% to Los Angeles at -1.0%. Construction averaged 16.4 SF under construction per 100 residents nationally, versus 95.9 in Dallas-Fort Worth.
- Cushman & Wakefield reported national shopping-center vacancy at 6.0% in Q2 2026, up 3 bps quarter over quarter and below the 7.4% historical average; asking rents up 2.2% year over year to $25.65 PSF; 2.3 million SF delivered in the quarter; and a development pipeline under 0.3% of existing inventory.
- JLL reported H1 2026 U.S. retail investment volume of $33.0 billion, up 14% year over year and the strongest first half since 2022, with cap rates compressing across retail sub-types.
- CBRE's H1 2026 Cap Rate Survey (about 3,600 estimates, 50-plus markets, completed in late June 2026) found average cap rates broadly stable, with neighborhood retail compressing more than other sectors, against a 10-year Treasury that peaked at 4.67% in mid-May 2026.
- ICSC's January 2026 roundup cited forecasts of 2026 store openings up 1.4% excluding restaurants (1.8% including restaurants), CoStar vacancy peaking below 4.4% in the second half, Colliers rent growth near 1.5% with construction down 37%, and CoStar quarterly net absorption averaging 3.8 million SF versus a 9.8 million SF prior five-year average.

### Occupancy by format (FY2025 REIT disclosure)

- Grocery-anchored / open-air: Regency 96.1% leased portfolio-wide and 96.5% same-property; within that, anchor space (10,000 SF and above) 98.0% leased and shop space (under 10,000 SF) 93.2% leased. Kimco 96.6% leased in its consolidated operating portfolio of 458 centers and 79.5 million SF, with proportionate portfolio occupancy of 96.4% and average center size of 177,308 SF.
- Mixed-use / high-quality community: Federal Realty 96.1% leased and 94.1% occupied at 12/31/2025, with the company attributing the gap to leased space in redevelopment, under improvement, or awaiting permits.
- Mall / outlet: Simon U.S. Malls and Premium Outlets 96.4% ending occupancy; The Mills 99.2%. Macerich leased occupancy 94.0%. Tanger 98% occupancy.
- STNL: Realty Income 98.9% leased across 15,511 properties, with the company stating that historically 1% to 4% of its portfolio is available for lease at any time. NNN 98.3% of properties leased across 3,692 properties.

### Rent levels and spreads (FY2025)

- Regency total ABR $25.91 PSF; 2026 expirations carry $28.45 PSF; new leases signed in 2025 averaged $36.02 PSF; shop-space signings averaged $41.67 PSF against $37.85 PSF on shop leases expiring in the next 12 months. Blended new-and-renewal rent spreads were +10.8% in 2025 versus +9.5% in 2024.
- Kimco ABR per leased SF rose from $20.36 to $21.05 during 2025.
- Simon average base minimum rent $60.97 PSF (up 4.7%); average annual initial base minimum rent on new leases $65.09 PSF in 2025 versus $66.61 PSF in 2024.
- Macerich mall and freestanding stores under 10,000 SF: $66.92 PSF consolidated ABR; releasing spread +$4.38 PSF, or +6.7%, with leases executed at $69.77 PSF against $65.39 PSF expiring.
- Tanger average annual base rent $27.77 PSF, up from $26.83 PSF.

- The same table for FY2024 shows materially higher new-deal capital: anchor new $61.64 PSF allowance and $6.77 PSF commissions; shop new $44.11 PSF allowance and $14.58 PSF commissions. - The renewal-versus-new gap is the most transferable finding: renewal capital in open-air centers ran roughly 1% to 4% of new-deal capital on both allowance and commissions in FY2025.

### Occupancy cost ratio and sales PSF

- Occupancy cost ratio = total tenant occupancy cost / tenant gross sales, where occupancy cost includes base rent, CAM reimbursement, real estate tax reimbursement, and percentage rent (Adventures in CRE). Tanger's disclosed definition also includes insurance and advertising and promotion.
- Macerich FY2025 consolidated centers: minimum rents 8.1% of sales, percentage rents 0.6%, expense recoveries 3.1%, total 11.8% (11.8% in 2024, 12.1% in 2023). Unconsolidated joint venture centers totaled 11.6% in 2025.
- Tanger FY2025 outlet occupancy cost ratio 9.7%, up from 9.5% (2024), 9.3% (2023), 8.6% (2022), and 8.1% (2021).
- Macerich portfolio tenant sales for spaces under 10,000 SF were $881 PSF on a trailing twelve months basis at 12/31/2025 versus $837 PSF a year earlier, with comparable tenant sales up 1.2% and traffic flat.
- No public issuer reviewed for this note publishes a category-level occupancy cost table (grocery, restaurant, apparel, service). The A.CRE treatment is explicit that a healthy ratio "depends" on retail type, tenant, and the store's position within the tenant's own fleet.

### Recoveries, percentage rent, and expense load

- Regency FY2025 same-property pro-rata: base rent $1,130,009k, recoveries from tenants $404,326k, percentage rent $15,468k, total real estate revenue $1,586,205k; operating and maintenance $265,592k, real estate taxes $205,725k, ground rent $15,045k, total real estate operating expenses $486,397k; NOI $1,099,808k. Derived from those figures: recoveries equal about 86% of operating and maintenance plus real estate taxes; total real estate operating expenses equal about 31% of total real estate revenue; percentage rent equals about 1.0% of total real estate revenue.
- Realty Income percentage rent was $18.2 million in 2025 versus $16.0 million in 2024 and represents less than 1% of rental revenue. Percentage rent is immaterial in STNL and near-immaterial in grocery-anchored, but is a visible 0.6% to 1.0% of sales in mall formats (Macerich).

### STNL and pad frames

- Realty Income: 355.0 million SF, 1,761 clients across 92 industries, 97.8% single-tenant, weighted average remaining lease term 8.8 years, annualized base rent $5.31 billion, FY2025 rent recapture 103.9% on re-leased units (104.9% in Q4 2025), $6.3 billion invested at a 7.3% initial weighted average cash yield.
- NNN REIT: 39,578,000 SF, weighted average remaining lease term 10.2 years; FY2025 acquisitions of 239 properties at a 7.4% weighted average cap rate (7.7% in 2024) and dispositions at a 6.4% weighted average cap rate (7.3% in 2024).

### E-commerce exposure

- Census: e-commerce was 17.1% of total U.S. retail sales in Q2 2026 (seasonally adjusted), up from 16.9% in Q1 2026, 16.7% in Q4 2025, 16.4% in Q3 2025, 16.3% in Q2 2025, and 16.0% in Q1 2025. Q2 2026 e-commerce sales grew 12.2% year over year against 6.7% for total retail sales.
- Census MARTS: advance July 2026 retail and food services sales were $763.6 billion, down 0.6% month over month but up 5.0% year over year; the May-July 2026 period was up 6.3% year over year.
- JLL's Q2 2026 category read (Census-sourced): sporting goods, hobby, and books +13.7% year over year; online and non-store retail +12.6%; electronics +8.0%; clothing +5.6%; furniture and home furnishings -1.1%.
- Reading those together: the aggregate e-commerce share is rising roughly 80 to 110 bps per year, but the growth is concentrated in the non-store channel itself and in categories that are already largely online. Necessity, service, food and beverage, and value categories continue to require physical space.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults (traceable to a named source and dated):

- ICSC GLA ranges, anchor counts, anchor GLA share, and trade-area radius as the format definition table.
- Occupancy cost ratio formula: (base rent + CAM reimbursement + real estate tax reimbursement + percentage rent) / tenant gross sales.
- Format occupancy reference band for FY2025: open-air grocery-anchored 96%-97% leased at the portfolio level, with anchor space near 98% and shop space near 93%; mall 94%-96%; outlet 98%; STNL 98%-99%.
- Anchor-versus-shop occupancy spread of roughly 400 to 500 bps as a normal structural feature of open-air centers, not a distress signal on its own.
- Renewal leasing capital in open-air centers running a small fraction of new-deal capital (roughly 1% to 4% in FY2025), which makes retention assumptions the single largest lever on capital-adjusted cash flow.
- STNL WALT reference band of roughly 9 to 10 years, and STNL portfolio occupancy of 98%-99%, with 1% to 4% of properties available at any time per Realty Income's own statement.
- The leased-versus-occupied distinction, and the requirement to underwrite both, sourced to Federal Realty's 96.1% / 94.1% disclosure and its stated explanation.

Case-by-case only, stated as such in the knowledge base:

- Category-level occupancy cost thresholds (grocery, restaurant, apparel, fitness, service). No primary or quasi-primary source reviewed publishes them, and the strongest practitioner source available says the answer is context-dependent. The knowledge base carries the formula and the two disclosed portfolio anchors (Macerich malls 11.6%-11.8%, Tanger outlets 9.7%) and directs users to the actual reported sales.
- Sales PSF by format outside malls and outlets. Only mall and outlet issuers publish comparable sales PSF; grocery-anchored and power-center owners generally do not. Grocer sales PSF must come from the tenant's own sales reports or estoppel.
- Cap rates and cap-rate spreads by format and class. The CBRE cap rate survey's by-type table was not retrievable in this session, so no numeric cap-rate matrix by format is published. Only the STNL transaction cap rates disclosed by NNN (7.4% acquisitions, 6.4% dispositions in FY2025) and Realty Income's 7.3% initial cash yield are stated, labeled as portfolio-level transaction evidence, not market cap rates for a specific asset.
- Opex PSF and capex reserve PSF by format. The Regency-derived ratios (recoveries about 86% of recoverable expense; opex about 31% of real estate revenue) are usable as a sanity check on a stabilized grocery-anchored center, but PSF opex and reserve levels vary too much by market, tax regime, insurance market, and structure to publish as defaults.
- TI/LC dollar defaults. The FY2024-to-FY2025 swing in Regency's own anchor-new allowance ($61.64 to $28.67 PSF) makes any fixed PSF default misleading. The knowledge base publishes the disclosed figures with their years attached and instructs users to size from actual deal terms.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Vacancy and availability disagree across the three institutional trackers: CBRE 4.9% availability, JLL 4.4% total vacancy, and Cushman & Wakefield 6.0% shopping-center vacancy, all for Q2 2026. These are not contradictions; they measure different universes and different metrics. CBRE publishes availability (which includes space marketed but still occupied) across all retail; JLL publishes vacancy across all retail including general retail and freestanding; Cushman & Wakefield publishes vacancy for shopping centers only, which excludes the single-tenant and general-retail stock that is running tightest. The knowledge base presents all three with their definitions attached rather than picking a single national number.
- Asking rent likewise differs ($24.79 CBRE, $26.02 JLL, $25.65 Cushman & Wakefield) because of the same universe differences. Treated as a $25-$26 PSF band, not as competing claims.
- Absorption differs sharply (JLL 10.2 million SF all retail versus Cushman & Wakefield 708,000 SF shopping centers, both Q2 2026). This is itself a finding: the general retail and freestanding segment is carrying national absorption, while shopping-center absorption is closer to flat.
- Forecast sources disagree on direction. ICSC's January 2026 roundup cited a CoStar view of vacancy rising into a peak below 4.4% and quarterly absorption well below trend, while the mid-year CBRE, JLL, and Cushman & Wakefield reads show vacancy holding and absorption recovering. Contemporaneous quarterly reporting is given priority over the earlier forecast, and the forecast is retained only as an indication of downside expectations.
- REIT-reported occupancy is not comparable company to company. Simon excludes mall anchors, majors, freestanding, and outlots from its occupancy calculation; Regency reports pro-rata percent leased split by anchor and shop; Realty Income reports occupancy by property count, not square footage; NNN reports the percentage of properties leased. Every figure in the knowledge base carries its issuer, because the definitions do not reconcile.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- A grocery-anchored center with a healthy headline leased rate can hide shop-space weakness. Regency's own split (98.0% anchor, 93.2% shop) shows the blended number is anchor-weighted by design. Always request the anchor-versus-shop split before accepting a portfolio-level leased rate.
- Leased and occupied are different numbers. Federal Realty's 200 bps gap is disclosed as benign redevelopment and permitting, but the same gap can also mean signed-not-open leases that will not pay for several quarters. Ask which it is.
- Percentage rent is immaterial in STNL and grocery-anchored formats and material only in malls and outlets. Underwriting percentage rent growth into a strip or STNL model is not supported by the disclosure reviewed here.
- Tenant sales are self-reported and, per Simon's own risk disclosure, may be understated where curbside pickup, ship-from-store, and in-store online returns are handled inconsistently. Occupancy cost ratios built on unaudited reported sales inherit that error.
- Anchor new-deal leasing capital can exceed several years of that anchor's base rent (Regency FY2024: $61.64 PSF allowance against $20.06 PSF base rent). Anchor replacement is a capital event, not a leasing assumption.
- National rent growth of 1.7% masks a range from +6.0% to -1.0% across the 22 markets JLL published. National figures are not a substitute for a submarket read.
- Construction is not uniformly scarce. JLL's 16.4 SF per 100 residents national average sits against 95.9 in Dallas-Fort Worth. A Sun Belt lease-up assumption based on the national pipeline figure will be wrong.
- REIT portfolio metrics are large-portfolio, institutional-quality averages. A single Class B center in a secondary trade area should not be underwritten to a Regency or Kimco portfolio occupancy.
