# Retail Underwriting Model Builder Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/retail/retail-underwriting-model-builder.md`
- Pairs with [Retail Benchmarks](knowledge/retail-benchmarks.md), [Retail Lease Structures](knowledge/retail-lease-structures.md), and [Retail Lender Criteria](knowledge/retail-lender-criteria.md)
- Intended users: acquisition, asset management, and debt teams building a lease-by-lease cash flow model for a U.S. retail asset before pricing, financing, or an investment committee decision

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States only. Rent, recovery, cap rate, and lender evidence below is U.S. and does not transfer to other jurisdictions.
- Deal type: acquisition, refinance, and asset review of grocery-anchored, power, strip / unanchored, lifestyle, mall, mixed-use retail, pad, and single-tenant net lease (STNL) assets. Ground-up development and entitlement modeling are out of scope.
- Legal and market assumptions: lease and REA interpretation, co-tenancy and radius enforceability, and percentage rent audit remedies are state-law specific and require counsel. This is educational decision support, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice.
- Accounting: model contractual cash rent. GAAP straight-line rent, above- and below-market lease amortization, and ASC 842 fixed-CAM straight-lining are reporting conventions, not cash, and are excluded from the underwriting cash flow.

## Key Findings

**Cash flow architecture.** The CREFC IRP v8.4 operating statement template gives the only widely used standardized line structure for a U.S. commercial mortgage income statement: Base Rent (or Gross Potential Rent less Vacancy Loss), Expense Reimbursement, Percentage Rent, Parking Income, and Other Income to Effective Gross Income; then operating expenses including ground rent to Net Operating Income; then Leasing Commissions, Tenant Improvements, Capital Expenditures, and Extraordinary Capital Expenditures as Total Capital Items to Net Cash Flow; then debt service, with DSCR reported on both NOI and NCF. IRP also instructs that capital expenditures and TI/LC "should be normalized to the values used for Underwriting." A retail model that stops at NOI is not comparable to how a lender will read it.

**Base rent and percentage rent.** Holland & Knight states the natural breakpoint as minimum rent divided by the percentage rate, worked at $140,000 / 7% = $2,000,000, and describes artificial breakpoints as a negotiated trade against minimum rent rather than a derived number. Because the natural breakpoint moves with each rent step, a model that hard-codes one breakpoint across the hold period misstates overage rent in every step year. Percentage rent is format-dependent and small: Macerich reports it at 0.6% of tenant sales in 2025 and 2024, Realty Income reports it at under 1% of rental revenue, and Regency's FY2025 consolidated percentage rent of $13.9 million sits against $986.9 million of base rent.

**Recoveries.** Recovery structure, not recovery rate, is the modeling input. Kimco discloses that certain leases provide a fixed-rate reimbursement of taxes, insurance, utilities, and CAM, which means those tenants do not participate in expense inflation at all. Cox Castle sets out the mechanics that create leakage: caps on controllable expenses that are cumulative or non-cumulative, denominators that vary by format (malls commonly exclude anchors and use occupied floor area, power centers use floor area ratios), management fees of 2.5%-4% of gross revenues layered with administrative fees of 10%-15% of other CAM, capital items excluded in the first 5-7 years of a new development and then amortized over useful life with interest, and audit cost-shifting at a 3%-5% overstatement.

**Rollover and renewal probability.** Brixmor states anchor original terms of 10 to 20 years against 5 to 10 years for smaller tenants, so a shop-heavy center rolls two to three times as often as its anchor GLA share suggests. The renewal decision is a sales-and-occupancy-cost decision: Adventures in CRE defines occupancy cost as base rent plus CAM and tax reimbursements plus percentage rent over gross sales, states that a tenant whose ratio is too high may vacate or demand a rent reduction, and expressly declines to publish a numeric healthy threshold because it depends on retail type, tenant, and how the store compares to that tenant's other locations. Macerich provides the only clean published portfolio ratio series, at 11.8% consolidated in 2025 against $881 PSF of TTM tenant sales.

**Option rent is the ceiling on mark-to-market.** Brixmor achieved 38.7% new-lease spreads in 2025 but 21.7% blended excluding options and 16.4% including options. Option exercises absorb roughly a third of the blended spread. A model that renews every tenant at market rent, rather than at the contractual option rent where an option exists, overstates revenue systematically.

**Downtime.** No reviewed source publishes a national downtime default. The available empirical anchors are indirect: Brixmor reports a 350 basis point spread between total leased and total billed occupancy plus a signed-but-not-yet-commenced population of 2.7 million SF and $62.3 million of ABR at 2025 year end, which is the lag between signature and rent commencement in a large open-air portfolio. Realty Income reports that roughly 1% to 4% of its STNL portfolio is available for lease at any given time, with 173 of 15,511 properties available at 2025 year end.

**Leasing capital by tenant type.** Regency's FY2025 pro-rata table is the clearest published split: anchor new at $17.46 base rent PSF with $28.67 allowance plus landlord work PSF and $4.65 commissions PSF; anchor renewal at $15.14 / $0.65 / $0.41; shop new at $43.16 / $51.12 / $17.37; shop renewal at $40.89 / $1.45 / $1.30; all leases blended at $27.46 / $12.58 / $3.89. The renewal-versus-new gap is the dominant driver: renewal capital ran roughly 1% to 4% of new-deal capital. The same table for FY2024 shows anchor new allowance at $61.64 PSF against $28.67 in FY2025, a swing that disqualifies any fixed PSF default.

**Capital and the anchor reserve.** Kimco discloses that many of its leases require the landlord to make roof and structural repairs while a number of leases place that responsibility on the tenant, so roof, structure, and parking field obligations must be read off the lease stack rather than assumed by format. CREFC separates recurring Capital Expenditures from Extraordinary Capital Expenditures, which is the right structure for separating a recurring reserve from a one-time roof or parking lot replacement. Kimco's 36 consolidated ground-leased centers, with $12.0 million due in 2026, confirm ground rent as a real operating line rather than a footnote.

**Co-tenancy downside case.** Cox Castle describes the standard remedy ladder: reduced rent, commonly 50% of fixed rent or percentage rent only; delayed opening for opening co-tenancy; and termination generally available only after six months, often a year, and longer for large spaces, with a snap-back to full rent or landlord recapture once the violation runs past about a year. Landlords may require proof of a sales decline. This maps directly to a modelable downside: an abatement period, then a decision point where the tenant either returns to full rent, terminates, or is recaptured.

**Exit cap and market context.** JLL reported $33.0 billion of H1 2026 U.S. retail volume, up 14% year over year and the strongest first half since 2022, with cap rates compressing across retail sub-types, alongside Q2 2026 net absorption of 10.2 million SF after a negative Q1 (-4.5 million SF) and rent growth easing to 1.7% year over year with a market range of +6.0% to -1.0%. CBRE's H1 2026 survey, completed in late June 2026, found neighborhood retail compressed the most on average. The Boulder Group reported STNL asking cap rates for Q2 2026 at 6.82% overall, 6.60% retail, with tenant-segment detail from 4.45% for premium ground leases to 7.85% for drug store. The OCC's Spring 2026 Semiannual Risk Perspective calls retail "a bright spot" with low vacancy while flagging CRE refinance risk at prevailing rates.

**Lender sizing view.** CREFC's Servicer Watchlist codes are the clearest published statement of the conditions institutional retail lenders treat as trouble: NCF DSCR below 1.10x (1E); current NCF DSCR below 1.40x and below 75% of underwritten DSCR (1F); floating-rate DSCR below 1.00x and below 90% of in-place NOI as of underwriting (1G); occupancy below 80% of underwritten for fixed-rate loans, or below 90% of in-place tenants for floating-rate loans (4A); a single tenant or any tenant above 30% of NRA expiring within 12 months for loans at or above $30 million, within 6 months for smaller loans, or on notice of non-renewal (4C); top-three tenants each at least 5% and cumulatively above 30% of NRA expiring within 6 months (4D); bankruptcy on the same combination (4E); and a major tenant above 30% of NRA in default, terminated, or dark (4F). KBRA's BBCMS 2024-C24 rating action, on a pool 43.0% retail, reported KNCF 10.4% below issuer cash flow and KBRA values 40.0% below third-party appraisals, which is the size of haircut a credit committee applies to a broker or issuer cash flow.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults:

- Adopt the CREFC IRP v8.4 line structure as the required output shape: EGI components, NOI, then LC / TI / CapEx / Extraordinary CapEx as Total Capital Items, then NCF, with DSCR reported on both NOI and NCF. It is a published standard and it is what a lender will reconcile against.
- Adopt Natural Breakpoint = Annual Minimum Rent / Percentage Rate, recomputed at each base rent step, and Percentage Rent Due = (Gross Sales - Breakpoint) x Percentage Rate. Deal-level rent, NOI, DSCR, debt yield, and return formulas are not restated in the skill; use [Underwriting Calculations](knowledge/underwriting-calc.md).
- Adopt Occupancy Cost Ratio = (base rent + CAM reimbursement + tax reimbursement + percentage rent) / tenant gross sales, matching both Adventures in CRE and Macerich's disclosed components, and require the model to state which components a reported ratio includes.
- Require renewal at the contractual option rent, not market rent, wherever an unexercised option exists. Supported by Brixmor's 38.7% new versus 16.4% blended-including-options spread.
- Require anchor and shop to be modeled as separate rollover populations with separate term, capital, and downtime assumptions. Supported by Brixmor's 10-20 versus 5-10 year term disclosure and Regency's anchor-versus-shop capital split.
- Require cash rent modeling and explicit exclusion of straight-line rent and above- and below-market lease amortization from the cash flow.
- Require Total Capital Items to be shown separately from NOI so a NOI-DSCR and an NCF-DSCR can both be reported.

Case-by-case only, no repo default:

- TI/TA and LC per square foot by tenant type. Regency's FY2025 table is a dated reference point, not a default; the FY2024-to-FY2025 anchor allowance swing from $61.64 to $28.67 PSF proves the point. Size from actual deal terms and current concession posture, and cite the year of any benchmark used.
- Downtime months by tenant type. No reviewed source publishes one. Build from signed LOIs, local comps, and the property's own leasing history; sanity-check against the signature-to-rent-commencement lag evidenced by Brixmor's 350 bps leased-to-billed spread.
- Renewal probability percentages. No reviewed source publishes a table. The skill supplies a decision rule driven by occupancy cost versus the tenant's own fleet, sales trend, option rent versus market, co-tenancy exposure, and capital required, and requires the analyst to state the probability and its basis.
- Capex reserve PSF, and roof, parking lot, facade, and HVAC replacement cost and timing. Build from the property condition assessment and the capital plan, and read landlord versus tenant responsibility off the lease stack per Kimco's disclosure.
- Exit cap rates by retail format. No reviewed primary or institutional source publishes a defensible format matrix suitable as a default. Use a comp-supported going-in cap plus an explicit expansion, state the spread and its basis, and treat the Boulder Group figures as STNL asking-cap transaction evidence only.
- Operating expense ratios and PSF by format. Not published here; build from the property's operating statements.

Derived guidance:

- Underwrite percentage rent as equity upside with no debt-service credit, consistent with its measured immateriality outside mall and outlet formats.
- Expect a haircut between broker or issuer NOI and lender-sustainable NCF. KBRA's 10.4% KNCF variance on a 43.0%-retail pool is a directional, dated reference for the size of that gap, not a formula.
- Pre-screen the model's base and downside cases against CREFC Watchlist triggers 1E, 1F, 4A, 4C, 4D, and 4F. A base case that trips several of these on a plausible anchor loss is not a clean permanent-loan candidate.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- **Cap rate direction.** CBRE's H1 2026 survey found neighborhood retail cap rates compressed the most among sectors, and JLL reported cap rates compressing across retail sub-types in H1 2026. The Boulder Group reported STNL retail asking cap rates rising five basis points to 6.60% in Q2 2026. These are not in conflict: they measure different universes, an institutional multi-tenant broker survey versus the 1031 single-tenant asking-cap market, on different bases. Resolution: do not blend them. Use the survey for multi-tenant format direction and the Boulder Group series only for STNL and pad, and cite which one is being used.
- **Leasing capital magnitude.** Regency's own FY2024 and FY2025 disclosures conflict with each other on anchor new-deal allowance ($61.64 versus $28.67 PSF). Resolution: the primary filing wins on both counts, which is precisely why neither becomes a default. The skill requires actual deal terms and a dated citation.
- **Occupancy cost thresholds.** Practitioner articles circulate category-level "healthy" ratios; the strongest practitioner source reviewed (Adventures in CRE) expressly declines to publish one, and the only clean primary series (Macerich) is portfolio-level mall data. Resolution: primary and self-limiting practitioner sources take priority. Compare a tenant to its own fleet, not to an invented table.
- **Sector optimism versus asset risk.** The OCC calls retail a bright spot with low vacancy; KBRA applies a 10.4% cash flow and 40.0% value haircut to a heavily retail conduit pool. Resolution: both hold. Regulator commentary describes the sector; rating agency practice describes the asset. Size debt from the asset.
- **National absorption versus shopping-center absorption.** JLL reported 10.2 million SF of national net absorption in Q2 2026 while shopping-center-specific absorption reported elsewhere was far smaller. Resolution: freestanding and general retail carries the national figure. Lease-up assumptions for shop space are built from local comps and signed LOIs, never from the national headline.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Fixed-CAM leases modeled as pro rata recoveries. The recovery line grows in the model and does not grow in reality.
- A single natural breakpoint carried across the hold period while base rent steps up, overstating percentage rent in every step year.
- Renewals modeled at market rent where an unexercised below-market option exists.
- Anchor replacement modeled as an ordinary leasing assumption. Anchor new-deal allowance can exceed a full year of that anchor's base rent, and the backfill pool may be narrowed by recorded REA exclusives the model cannot see.
- Percentage rent, specialty and temporary income, or signed-not-open rent carried into NOI as if it were durable, then capitalized at exit.
- Straight-line rent, below-market lease accretion, or ASC 842 fixed-CAM straight-lining left in the cash flow.
- Ground rent omitted from operating expenses on a ground-leased parcel, or a ground-leased pad modeled as if it participated in CAM.
- Co-tenancy relief already running and shown as in-place rent, or a downside case that models abatement without modeling the termination and recapture decision point at roughly one year.
- Roof, structure, or parking field capital assumed to sit with the landlord (or the tenant) without reading the lease stack.
- Exit cap set equal to going-in cap with no spread, no comp, and no basis, in an asset whose WALT shortens materially over the hold.
- Debt sized to a stabilized pro forma that fails a CREFC-style in-place DSCR or occupancy test in year one.
- STNL or pad modeled with center-level metrics. These are lease-term, credit, rent-to-market, and residual-use assets.

## Open Questions

- No reviewed source publishes defensible national defaults for downtime months, renewal probability, capex reserve PSF, or exit cap rates by retail format. These stay analyst-supplied and analyst-defended.
- Category-level occupancy cost thresholds (grocery, restaurant, apparel, fitness, service) remain unpublished in primary and quasi-primary sources and are deliberately not asserted.
- Tenant-reported sales integrity under omnichannel fulfillment remains an open measurement problem that affects percentage rent, occupancy cost, and kick-out tests simultaneously.
- Whether the H1 2026 cap rate compression reported by CBRE and JLL persists is unknown; every figure in this note is dated and must be refreshed before use in a live pricing or credit conversation.
- State-law enforceability of co-tenancy, radius, and continuous-operation remedies varies and requires counsel; the model quantifies exposure, it does not resolve enforceability.
