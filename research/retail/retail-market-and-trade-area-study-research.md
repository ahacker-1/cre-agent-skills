# Retail Market and Trade Area Study Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/retail/retail-market-and-trade-area-study.md`
- Establishes the trade-area definition method, the void / leakage and surplus formulas, the demographic and daytime-population data sources, the traffic-count standard, and the mid-2026 submarket context used in the Retail v1 pack
- Intended users: acquisition, asset management, credit, and appraisal-review teams testing whether a U.S. retail asset is supported by its trade area

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States. Trade-area radii, census geographies, traffic-count standards, and grocery-access thresholds cited below are U.S. constructs and do not transfer to non-U.S. markets.
- Deal type: acquisition, refinancing, and asset review of grocery-anchored, power, strip / unanchored, lifestyle, mall, mixed-use, pad, and single-tenant net lease (STNL) retail.
- Data assumptions: sales, spending, and demographic estimates below the county level are modeled, not measured. Every leakage or capture number is an estimate built on other estimates and must be treated as a hypothesis to test, not a finding.
- Legal and accounting assumptions: none of this is legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice. Appraisal and lender references describe supervisory expectations, not a compliance standard this skill can satisfy.

## Key Findings

### Defining the trade area

- ICSC publishes a trade-area size for each U.S. shopping-center format: strip / convenience under 1 mile, neighborhood center 3 miles, community center 3 to 6 miles, power center 5 to 10 miles, regional mall 5 to 15 miles, super-regional mall 5 to 25 miles, lifestyle 8 to 12 miles, factory outlet and theme / festival 25 to 75 miles. ICSC's own disclaimer states the criteria are typical general features rather than a rule covering all situations.
- ICSC pairs each format with GLA range, acre range, anchor count, anchor GLA share, and typical tenant count, so format can be verified from the rent roll and site plan rather than asserted from the offering memorandum label.
- The UW-Extension toolbox distinguishes the convenience trade area (where most customers of a district live, and where residents buy gas and groceries), competing trade areas around competing trade centers, and the trade region, which is the outer boundary of the subject's own and all competing trade areas. That three-level structure is what makes a competition-adjusted trade area different from a plain ring.
- Placer.ai's visit data shows real trade-area distance decay varies by tenant rather than by center size: 41.9% of visits to one mall's Scheels came from 50 or more miles away by 2024, while dining and small-format tenants drew locally. A single ring cannot describe a center with both a destination anchor and convenience shops.

### Demographics, daytime population, and growth

- Census LEHD OnTheMap publishes jobs by work location (Workplace Area Characteristics) and home-to-work commute flows at census block detail, which is the primary-source way to build daytime population and employee draw for a trade area rather than buying a modeled estimate.
- LODES is described by the Census Bureau as a partially synthetic dataset, so block-level counts carry noise; aggregate to the whole trade area before drawing conclusions.
- The UW-Extension and Ohio University methods both weight demand by income: potential sales are scaled by the ratio of local per capita income to the benchmark geography's per capita income, which is why median household income alone is a weak trade-area screen.

### Void, leakage, and surplus

- Ohio University publishes the four formulas explicitly. Potential sales for community c in sector i equal population times benchmark per capita sales for that sector times the ratio of local per capita income to benchmark per capita income. Trade Area Capture equals actual sales divided by (benchmark per capita sales times the income ratio). Pull Factor equals Trade Area Capture divided by population. Surplus or leakage equals actual sales minus potential sales.
- Deller's reading of the Pull Factor: greater than one means the market draws customers from outside, less than one means it loses them. For practical mapping he uses three bands rather than a strict 1.0 line: under 0.9 underperforming, 0.9 to 1.1 performing as expected, over 1.1 overperforming.
- Esri's Retail MarketPlace builds supply from retail sales by NAICS and demand from household spending, and reports a Leakage / Surplus Factor where positive values mean leakage of retail opportunity out of the trade area and negative values mean a surplus that draws customers in.
- UW-Extension converts a sector gap into space two ways: number of businesses (potential sales divided by average sales per store) and square feet (potential sales divided by average sales per square foot). Both require a benchmark table that must be current; the toolbox's own example uses a 2008 ULI sales-per-square-foot source and 2007 Economic Census sales, which is exactly the staleness problem to avoid.
- Ohio University notes that sector data is suppressed where fewer than ten entities exist in a geography, and those sales are pushed into an "Unclassified" bucket. In small or rural trade areas this alone can manufacture an apparent leakage.

### Competitive supply and new deliveries

- The Interagency Appraisal and Evaluation Guidelines direct that an appraisal discuss market conditions including property value trends, demand and supply factors, and exposure time; that a reader be able to understand the risk characteristics of the subject and its market, including the anticipated supply of competing properties; and that absorption be based on market demand in light of current and expected competition in the market area. Limited or oversupply of competing properties is listed as a change in market conditions that can trigger a new appraisal or evaluation.
- National supply is thin but extremely uneven. JLL reported 56.1 million SF under construction against 11.8 billion SF of inventory in Q2 2026, a national average of 16.4 SF under construction per 100 residents, and a range from New York City at 0.0 to Dallas-Fort Worth at 95.9, with Tampa / St. Petersburg at 69.0 and Las Vegas at 68.7. Cushman & Wakefield put the national pipeline under 0.3% of existing inventory with 2.3 million SF delivered in Q2 2026.
- Demand is carried by format. JLL reported Q2 2026 U.S. net absorption of 10.2 million SF after -4.5 million SF in Q1 2026, with general retail (freestanding and single-tenant boxes) at nearly 7 million SF, and malls and neighborhood centers turning positive after a negative start to the year. Cushman & Wakefield reported only 708,000 SF of shopping-center absorption in the same quarter and -2.7 million SF year to date.

### Anchor draw and cross-shopping

- Placer.ai's visit-based approach identifies anchors by share of center visits rather than GLA, and reports cases where a small-format tenant out-draws a department store: Barnes & Noble at Coronado Center captured 7.9% of mall visits, ahead of Macy's and JCPenney; Porto's Bakery at Northridge Fashion Center drew 15.6% of visits; In-N-Out at Glendale Galleria drew 8.6%.
- The same source reports cross-shopping evidence: after Aldi opened at Green Acres Commons alongside BJ's Wholesale Club, visits to BJ's increased, and overlapping audiences behaved as a strength rather than cannibalization.
- USDA ERS defines supermarket access by distance to a supermarket, supercenter, or large grocery store, at 0.5 and 1 mile in urban areas and 10 and 20 miles in rural areas, and explicitly excludes military commissaries, warehouse clubs, drug stores, dollar stores, and convenience stores from that definition. That gives a defensible screen for whether a grocery anchor sits in a genuine grocery void.

### Traffic counts, access, and visibility

- FHWA distinguishes AADT from ADT. AADT estimates the mean traffic volume across all days of a year at a location. ADT (mean daily traffic) is the average over a short duration, often seven days or less. Marketing materials frequently quote a short count as if it were AADT.
- FHWA describes three AADT estimation methods (simple average, AASHTO average-of-averages, and the FHWA AADT method) and states that FHWA recommends the FHWA method for reduced bias. Counts from different states or different years are therefore not automatically comparable.

### E-commerce resilience by category

- Census reported e-commerce at 17.1% of total U.S. retail sales in Q2 2026 (seasonally adjusted), with e-commerce sales up 12.2% year over year against 6.7% for total retail sales. Share is rising because the non-store channel is growing roughly twice as fast as total retail, not because physical sales are shrinking.
- JLL's Q2 2026 category read (Census-sourced) showed sporting goods, hobby and books +13.7%, non-store retail +12.6%, electronics +8.0%, clothing +5.6%, general merchandisers +3.6%, food and beverage places +3.8%, grocery +1.6%, and furniture and home furnishings -1.1%, the only category in year-over-year decline.

### Submarket vacancy and rents by format

- Three institutional sources published three different national numbers for Q2 2026 because they measure different universes: CBRE availability 4.9% with average asking rent $24.79 PSF (+2.4% YoY); JLL total vacancy 4.4% with market rent $26.02 PSF across 11.8 billion SF; Cushman & Wakefield shopping-center vacancy 6.0% against a 7.4% historical average with asking rent $25.65 PSF (+2.2% YoY).
- Rent growth is a local variable. JLL put national rent growth at 1.7% year over year in Q2 2026, ranging from Charlotte at +6.0%, Minneapolis +5.2%, Nashville +4.6%, and Phoenix +4.3% down to coastal gateway markets in negative territory.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults:

- Start the primary trade area from the ICSC trade-area size for the subject's format (strip under 1 mile; neighborhood 3 miles; community 3 to 6; power 5 to 10; regional mall 5 to 15; super-regional 5 to 25; lifestyle 8 to 12; outlet 25 to 75), then adjust for drive time and competing centers. Always state the ring, the drive time, and the competitive adjustment separately.
- Use the Ohio University / UW-Extension formula set as the void analysis engine: potential sales, Trade Area Capture, Pull Factor, and surplus / leakage as defined above.
- Use Deller's practical Pull Factor bands for reading results: under 0.9 underperforming, 0.9 to 1.1 performing as expected, over 1.1 overperforming. Treat a Pull Factor between 0.9 and 1.1 as no signal.
- Adopt the Esri sign convention when reading a purchased Retail MarketPlace report: positive Leakage / Surplus Factor means leakage, negative means surplus. Report the factor and the dollar gap together; a factor without a dollar amount is not actionable.
- Require AADT, not ADT, for any traffic count used in a verdict, with the state DOT station identifier and count year stated. Treat an undated or unsourced "traffic count" in an offering memorandum as unverified.
- Use USDA ERS distance thresholds (1 mile urban, 10 miles rural to a supermarket, supercenter, or large grocery store) as the screen for whether a grocery anchor occupies a real void, and apply the ERS exclusions (no warehouse clubs, drug stores, dollar stores, or convenience stores counted as a supermarket).
- Never mix national vacancy series. State the publisher and the metric name (availability, total vacancy, or shopping-center vacancy) every time a national figure is used as context.

Case-by-case only, no repo default:

- Retail square feet per capita, capture rate, minimum trade-area population per grocery store, and sales per square foot by category. No reviewed primary or quasi-primary source publishes a current national figure for any of these. The UW-Extension square-foot gap method depends on a sales-per-square-foot benchmark the toolbox itself sources from a 2008 publication, which is too stale to hard-code.
- Submarket vacancy and rent targets. Build them from the local report and the actual comp set; the national spread across three publishers in one quarter (4.4% to 6.0%) is larger than most underwriting tolerances.
- Drive-time bands. The trade-area literature supports drive time as a method but publishes no universal 5 / 10 / 15 minute standard that applies across urban, suburban, and rural retail.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Leakage data reliability. Esri presents the Leakage / Surplus Factor as a decision tool; Milder documents 20% to 40% error factors in business directory listings, systematic NAICS misclassification by owners, and material disagreement between two major vendors covering the same market. Resolution: the university-extension formulas and the vendor factor are both retained as hypothesis generators, and the skill requires validation against at least one harder input (state or local sales tax data by NAICS, actual reported tenant sales, a signed LOI, or a broker-confirmed comp) before a leakage figure supports a verdict. This is the same posture the Retail Benchmarks knowledge base takes toward self-reported tenant sales.
- Trade-area radius versus drive time. ICSC's radii are the only published, standards-body figures, but they are stated as typical rather than universal, and Placer.ai's visit data shows draw varies by tenant inside one center. Resolution: the ICSC radius is the starting ring and the documented default; drive time and competition adjust it; the ring is never the final answer where visit or customer-origin data exists.
- National vacancy level. CBRE (4.9% availability), JLL (4.4% total vacancy), and Cushman & Wakefield (6.0% shopping-center vacancy) disagree for Q2 2026. Resolution: none is wrong; they measure different inventories and different metrics. The skill forbids blending them and requires the metric name and publisher alongside any figure.
- Absorption direction. JLL reported 10.2 million SF of national absorption in Q2 2026 while Cushman & Wakefield reported 708,000 SF for shopping centers and -2.7 million SF year to date. Resolution: the difference is format, not measurement error. Freestanding and general retail is carrying national demand, so shop-space lease-up in a center must be built from local comps, not from the national headline.
- Aspatial capture versus spatial trade area. Deller warns that Trade Area Capture is aspatial even though the phrase "trade area" is spatial. Resolution: the skill reports Trade Area Capture as an estimated number of customers purchased for, and never presents it as a map boundary.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Tourism, seasonal, and recreational-home markets. Deller shows these inflate Pull Factors and can mask sectors that are actually losing customers. Flag any market where seasonal population, lodging, or recreational housing is material and report the Pull Factor with that caveat.
- Small and rural trade areas. Sector data is suppressed where fewer than ten entities exist, and suppressed sales land in an unclassified bucket, which manufactures apparent leakage. Do not recommend a category on a leakage figure drawn from a suppressed sector.
- Dense urban and transit-oriented retail. Ring and drive-time geometry both fail where walk-up and transit trade dominates. Use daytime population from OnTheMap, block-level residential density, and pedestrian counts instead, and say so.
- Highway and pad sites. AADT, turning movements, median cuts, signalization, and ingress / egress can matter more than the residential ring. A pad with strong counts and poor left-turn access is not a strong pad.
- STNL and single-tenant assets. Trade-area durability still matters for residual value and re-tenanting, but the controlling questions are lease term, credit, rent-to-market, and alternative use. Do not present a center-style trade-area verdict as a credit verdict.
- Cannibalization and self-competition. A grocer or big box opening a nearby store in the same banner reduces subject sales without changing trade-area demographics. Check the tenant's own fleet before concluding the trade area weakened.
- Announced-but-unbuilt competition. The Interagency Guidelines direct attention to anticipated supply of competing properties, so entitled and permitted projects belong in the competitive supply section even when not under construction. State entitlement status and expected delivery.
- Anchor definition by GLA alone. Placer.ai's visit data shows the largest box is not always the traffic anchor. If visit or customer-origin data exists, rank tenants by draw before naming the anchor.
- Any trade-area conclusion that rests on a modeled sales estimate with no local validation. Treat as unsupported.

## Open Questions

- No current primary or quasi-primary source was found for U.S. retail square feet per capita by market, or for sales per square foot by retail category, at a level that would justify a repo default. Both remain case-by-case.
- Foot-traffic panel data (Placer.ai and comparable vendors) is not independently validated in any reviewed primary source. It is used here as directional evidence about draw and cross-shopping, not as a measured trade area.
- Census Monthly State Retail Sales is labeled an experimental product and publishes year-over-year percentage changes for total ex-nonstore plus 11 subsectors at the state level only. Sub-state per capita sales benchmarks still require state sales tax data or a commercial vendor, which varies in availability by state.
- Category-level occupancy cost and sales PSF thresholds are deliberately out of scope here and are handled in the pack's tenant sales and occupancy cost material.
- Drive-time band conventions (5 / 10 / 15 minutes versus 3 / 5 / 7) are practitioner habit rather than a published standard; the skill asks the user to state the bands used rather than imposing one.
