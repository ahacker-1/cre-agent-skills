# Retail IC Memo Writer Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/retail/retail-ic-memo-writer.md`
- Intended users: acquisition, portfolio, capital markets, and asset management teams who must convert completed retail diligence into a single investment committee decision document for a U.S. retail acquisition, refinancing, recapitalization, or hold / sell review
- Secondary users: credit and lender-facing teams who need the same synthesis in a form a credit committee will recognize
- This is an educational decision-support note, not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States. Center classification, lender lanes, bankruptcy treatment, and appraisal supervision are all U.S.-specific
- Deal type: grocery-anchored, power, strip / unanchored, lifestyle, mall, mixed-use retail, pad, and single-tenant net lease (STNL) retail; fee or leasehold ownership; equity or debt decision
- Legal and market assumptions: leases, REA / OEA documents, loan documents, and organizational documents govern; committee approval is a governance act, not a legal opinion. Counsel reviews legal interpretation, and a licensed appraiser establishes value
- The memo is an internal decision artifact. Nothing in this note addresses how projected returns may be presented to outside investors, which is a securities and marketing question for counsel and compliance

## Key Findings

**A committee memo is a synthesis artifact, not a new analysis.** Every number in it should trace to one of the seven upstream retail workstreams. The composite skill's job is to reconcile those outputs, expose where they disagree, and force a decision with named conditions. Where an upstream workstream is absent, the memo carries the gap as an open item rather than filling it with an assumption.

**Institutional underwriting criteria are publicly disclosed and translate directly into memo sections.** Realty Income's FY2025 10-K states the investment must be evaluated against industry, client credit, and market conditions; expected financial returns under various scenarios including default; the value of the underlying real estate based on replacement cost, market rents, and alternative uses; and store-level profitability for retail locations where available. That is a usable four-part spine for the retail memo: demand and credit, downside-inclusive returns, real estate value independent of the lease, and unit-level economics.

**Occupancy cost is the renewal predictor a committee should see, and it has no universal threshold.** Macerich's 10-K states that a low cost of occupancy percentage shows more capacity to raise rents at renewal than a high one, and discloses consolidated occupancy cost of 11.8% of sales in 2025. The strongest practitioner treatment reviewed refuses to publish a universal healthy band, holding that the answer depends on retail type, the tenant, and how the store compares to that tenant's other locations. The memo should therefore present the ratio per tenant with its components named, plus the share of GLA above a stated internal threshold, rather than assert a national pass/fail line.

**Anchor and co-tenancy risk is a cascade, not a line item.** Co-tenancy clauses split into opening and operating varieties, can be tied simultaneously to named key tenants, a count of mini-majors, and a percentage of occupied shop space, and are constrained by replacement-tenant and condition provisions. A committee memo that reports "co-tenancy present" without the trigger, the cure period, the remedy, and whether the conditions to invoke are satisfiable has not reported the risk.

**Valuation discipline is supervised.** The Interagency Appraisal and Evaluation Guidelines require that appraisers be independent of loan production and hold no direct, indirect, or prospective interest in the property or transaction, and that for proposed or partially leased rental developments the appraiser make deductions and discounts for leasing commissions, rent loss, tenant improvements, and entrepreneurial profit, with absorption of unleased space considered. Where leases do not reflect current market conditions, the appraisal must state the interest appraised and discuss the leases in place. A memo that presents a stabilized value without those deductions is presenting a number the deal's own lender will not accept.

**The financing environment is supportive but not permissive.** The OCC's Spring 2026 Semiannual Risk Perspective calls retail a bright spot with low vacancy and little space available in most markets, while flagging that a substantial volume of CRE loans originated in a lower-rate environment must refinance at prevailing rates. The Fed's July 2026 SLOOS found a moderate net share of banks easing standards on nonfarm nonresidential loans, while its special questions found standards still at the tighter end of the post-2005 range for every category except C&I. Both readings belong in the memo's financing section as directional context with dates, not as a reason the refinance math works.

**National demand data does not underwrite a shopping center.** Cushman & Wakefield reported 708,000 SF of Q2 2026 shopping-center absorption against year-to-date absorption of -2.7 msf, with vacancy at 6.0% and rents up 2.2% year over year. Census put e-commerce at 17.1% of total retail sales in Q2 2026, growing 12.2% year over year against 6.7% for total retail. A memo should use these as context lines with sources and dates, and take its actual lease-up and rent assumptions from the trade-area study and signed LOIs.

**Exit assumptions need a comp, and STNL has one.** The Boulder Group reported Q2 2026 STNL cap rates of 6.82% overall and 6.60% for retail, with supply up 12.5% to roughly 5,800 properties and investment grade product under 10% of retail supply. That is usable evidence for a pad or STNL exit discussion and a caution that the marketed supply is mostly non-credit product. No equivalent published cap-rate matrix by shopping-center format was located, so center exit caps stay comp-driven.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults for this skill:

- The memo must show three income figures, not one: contractual in-place NOI, durable NOI after removing percentage rent, specialty and temporary income, signed-not-open rent, recovery leakage, and any rent already subject to co-tenancy relief, and stabilized NOI net of the capital required to get there. Formulas are not restated in the skill; they come from `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`.
- Occupancy cost ratio is reported per tenant with its components named, and aggregated as the share of inline GLA above a stated internal threshold. The threshold is stated by the user or the sponsor, not defaulted by the skill.
- Anchor and major-tenant rollover is measured against both loan maturity and hold-period exit, and the downside case assumes the co-tenancy trigger fires unless the clause's own conditions make that implausible.
- Risk categorization uses the nine-category framework, strategy weights, dealbreaker checklist, and recommendation values in `knowledge/risk-scoring.md`. Category 9 (multifamily regulatory) is not scored for retail; the memo states that the retail-specific escalations sit inside categories 5, 6, and 7 rather than inventing a new category.
- Conditions precedent are written as testable statements with an owner and a date, and a conditional approval that cannot name its conditions is a no-go.
- Every market figure carried into the memo is labeled directional and dated with its source.

Case-by-case only:

- Occupancy cost thresholds by category, sales PSF floors, exit cap rates for center formats, reserve amounts, and required IRR or equity multiple hurdles. None are supported by a reviewed primary source at national scope, and all are sponsor-, format-, and market-specific.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- **Retail is healthy vs shopping centers are barely absorbing space.** The OCC calls retail a bright spot with low vacancy; Cushman & Wakefield shows year-to-date shopping-center absorption negative. These are not contradictory, they are different aggregates: the regulator is describing vacancy and available space across retail property broadly, the brokerage is describing net absorption in shopping centers specifically. The memo carries both and takes lease-up from local evidence.
- **Occupancy cost thresholds.** Public filings disclose portfolio-level ratios; practitioner guidance declines to set a universal band. The filings win as evidence of what institutional portfolios run at, the practitioner position wins as the rule the skill adopts, so the skill reports the ratio and refuses to publish a national pass/fail line.
- **Value support.** Broker and seller stabilized values conflict with supervised appraisal practice. The interagency guidelines control for anything a regulated lender will finance, so the memo presents as-is value with the deductions and discounts the guidelines describe.
- **Underwriting criteria sources.** A single issuer's disclosed criteria are not an industry standard. Realty Income's list is used as a structural spine because it is disclosed in a primary filing, not as a threshold set.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Do not hard-code return hurdles, exit caps, reserve levels, or occupancy cost thresholds. These come from the sponsor's mandate and the asset's market.
- A memo assembled from upstream analyses of differing vintages is a data-quality problem. If the trade-area study predates a competing center's opening or an anchor bankruptcy, the memo says so and lowers confidence.
- Single-tenant and pad deals do not fit the center memo shape. Term, credit, rent-to-market, residual use, and the lease document itself carry the decision, and the trade area governs residual value rather than current value.
- Ground-leased anchors, REA / OEA-controlled site plans, and anchor-owned pads mean the sponsor may not control the outcome the memo is underwriting. That is a legal and structural question for counsel.
- Tenant bankruptcies inside the hold period are a live scenario, not a tail risk; Macerich's FY2025 filing names three that affected its own results. Lease treatment in bankruptcy is a matter for counsel.
- Reported tenant sales are usually unaudited and self-reported. Occupancy cost ratios built on them inherit that weakness.
- A recommendation that depends on cap-rate compression, rate cuts, or a refinance that has not been quoted is a thesis, not a plan.

## Open Questions

- No reviewed primary or standards-body source publishes a national retail investment committee memo format, approval-threshold matrix, or delegation-of-authority standard. Committee governance, quorum, and approval thresholds remain sponsor-specific and were intentionally left out of the skill.
- No reviewed source publishes durable occupancy cost bands by retail category or sales PSF floors by format outside mall and outlet disclosure. Both remain case-by-case.
- Shopping-center exit cap rates by format were not located in a citable published matrix; only STNL transaction evidence was available.
- Presentation of projected returns to outside investors raises securities and marketing questions that were deliberately not researched here and belong with counsel and compliance.
