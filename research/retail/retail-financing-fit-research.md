# Retail Financing Fit Research

## Purpose

- Supports `skills/retail/retail-financing-fit.md`
- Pairs with the existing knowledge bases `knowledge/retail-lender-criteria.md` and `knowledge/retail-benchmarks.md`
- Intended users: sponsors, mortgage brokers, credit analysts, acquisitions teams, and asset managers deciding which lender lane a U.S. retail asset belongs in, what will constrain proceeds, and what structure a lender will require before closing
- Scope is educational decision support for retail acquisitions, refinancings, and maturity reviews. It is not legal, tax, investment, accounting, or financing advice.

## U.S.-Only Assumptions

- Geography: United States. Bank concentration supervision, SBA program rules, CMBS servicing standards, and the CTL market described here are U.S. constructs and do not transfer to other jurisdictions.
- Deal type: grocery-anchored, power, strip / unanchored, lifestyle, mall, mixed-use retail, pad, and single-tenant net lease (STNL) acquisition, refinance, recapitalization, and bridge financing.
- Legal and market assumptions: retail debt is sized off lease documents and tenant sales, not off headline leverage. Anchor status and term, co-tenancy triggers, go-dark rights, exclusives, percentage rent, and the CAM recovery structure move proceeds more than LTV does.
- Any quoted term sheet, loan agreement, or lender credit policy controls over every directional range in this note.

## Key Findings

### The lane question is now "which execution," not "is retail financeable"

- The OCC's Spring 2026 Semiannual Risk Perspective states that "Retail remains a bright spot. The vacancy rate in retail properties remains low, with little space available for lease in most markets," while separately warning that CRE headwinds "have not resulted in significant credit defaults, although refinance risk merits continued attention."
- MBA reported (2026-08-06) that Q2 2026 retail originations rose 61% year over year, the largest gain of any property type, with CMBS up 68% and depositories up 61% but life companies down 27%.
- FDIC's Q2 2026 Quarterly Banking Profile shows the largest quarterly past-due-and-nonaccrual improvement in the banking system occurred in nonfarm nonresidential CRE, down $2.4 billion or 14 basis points to 1.52%, with a 1.23% noncurrent rate and NFNR balances up 4.1% year over year.
- The Fed's July 2026 SLOOS found "moderate and modest net shares of banks reported having eased standards" for NFNR loans, with demand basically unchanged and stronger at large banks than at smaller ones.
- Counterweight: Trepp data reported 2026-08-11 put July 2026 retail CMBS delinquency at 6.96% against 1.13% for industrial, with non-performing balloons making up 66% of newly delinquent balances across all property types. Maturity, not operations, is the dominant retail default mode right now.
- Implication for the skill: bank and CMBS lanes are the widest open in 2026 and life company availability cannot be assumed. But a favorable sector backdrop does not size a loan; the asset's own lease documents do.

### Published sizing bands exist, but only at practitioner grade

- No primary or standards-body source publishes retail LTV, DSCR, debt yield, or loan-per-square-foot bands. The only published parameters found are lender-broker marketing pages: life company LTV 60%-75%, DSCR 1.25x-1.35x, debt yield 8%-10%, $5 million minimum; and general retail up to 75% LTV, DSCR "about 1.25 to 1.35," $1.5 million minimum, with up to 90% for owner-user SBA execution.
- These match the ranges already carried in `knowledge/retail-lender-criteria.md`. The skill should reuse them and repeat their directional label rather than restate new numbers.
- Loan PSF has no published band at all. It stays a comparison against as-is basis, replacement cost, and local comps.

### The CREFC watchlist is the clearest published statement of what breaks a retail loan

CREFC's Investor Reporting Package v8.4 Portfolio Review Guidelines set the Servicer Watchlist triggers master servicers apply monthly. Verified thresholds relevant to retail:

- 1E fixed rate: NCF DSCR below 1.10x (1.20x healthcare and lodging), explicitly excluding CTL, co-ops, ground leases, and single tenant NNN.
- 1F informational: NCF DSCR below 1.40x and below 75% of underwritten DSCR, same exclusions.
- 1G floating rate: DSCR below 1.00x and below 90% of in-place NOI as of underwriting.
- 1B / 1C / 1D operational: non-escrowed taxes verified 60+ days past due; property or liability insurance 60+ days delinquent or force-placed; servicing advances over $10,000 aggregate and 30+ days delinquent.
- 2C: "Occurrence of Servicing Trigger Event in the Mortgage Loan Documents (for example: Springing Lockbox, Establishment of LOC, Trap Excess Cash)" - any occurrence, released on cure or on establishment of the LOC or lockbox.
- 4A occupancy: below 80% of underwritten occupancy for fixed rate loans; below 90% of in-place tenants as of underwriting for floating rate.
- 4C: single tenant, or any tenant above 30% of NRA, expiring within 12 months for loans at or above $30 million, within 6 months for loans under $30 million, or on notice of non-renewal. Fully amortizing CTL loans are excluded where the lease expires co-terminus with the loan.
- 4D: top three tenants each at least 5% of NRA and cumulatively above 30% of NRA expiring within 6 months.
- 4E: bankruptcy of a licensee, franchisor, or top-three tenant combination meeting the same 5% / 30% test.
- 4F: major tenant lease above 30% of NRA in default, terminated, or dark.
- 5A: pending maturity or ARD with a balloon balance inside 90 days.

The IRP's own retail commentary example makes the anchor-to-co-tenancy chain explicit: a 100,000 SF center where a tenant at 68% of NRA vacated early, after which "the remaining tenants at the property have notified the borrower they are exercising their co-tenancy clause and requesting a reduction in their rents."

Two design consequences. First, 4C through 4F is a pre-screen that can be run before a lender call: an asset that would trip several of them on a plausible anchor loss is not a clean permanent-loan candidate. Second, 2C confirms that springing lockbox, excess-cash trap, and letter-of-credit posting are standard loan-document mechanics, not exotic asks - the skill should specify the mechanism and the trigger, never a made-up reserve dollar amount.

### Co-tenancy is a proceeds question, and the taxonomy is disclosed in filings

Regency Centers' FY2025 Form 10-K states that loss of an anchor "can reduce rental revenues generated by the shopping center in other spaces because of the loss of the departed anchor's customer drawing power," and that co-tenancy clauses "have several variants: they may allow a tenant to postpone a store opening if certain other tenants fail to open their stores; they may allow a tenant to close its store prior to lease expiration if another tenant closes its store prior to lease expiration; or more commonly, they may allow a tenant to pay reduced levels of rent until a certain number of tenants open their stores within the same shopping center." The same filing flags shadow anchors - retailers owning space adjacent to but not within the center - whose vacancy hurts traffic without any lease remedy available to the landlord.

For financing fit this means the downside case is not "anchor rent goes to zero." It is anchor rent plus the alternative-rent haircut on every triggered inline lease, plus the co-tenancy termination options that can be exercised after a cure period lapses.

### Leasing capital, not rent, is what a retail reserve actually funds

Regency's FY2025 pro-rata leasing disclosure (weighted average PSF) verified directly from the 10-K:

| Space | Deal | Base Rent PSF | Tenant Allowance + Landlord Work PSF | Leasing Commissions PSF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor | New | $17.46 | $28.67 | $4.65 |
| Anchor | Renewal | $15.14 | $0.65 | $0.41 |
| Shop | New | $43.16 | $51.12 | $17.37 |
| Shop | Renewal | $40.89 | $1.45 | $1.30 |
| All leases | Total | $27.46 | $12.58 | $3.89 |

New anchor deal capital ran roughly 1.9x that anchor's annual base rent in FY2025, and new shop capital roughly 1.6x. Renewal capital ran a small fraction of new-deal capital. A retail loan sized without a reserve mechanism for anchor backfill is sized on the assumption that retention holds.

### Percentage rent is spendable, not borrowable

Practitioner treatment is blunt: "You can spend the percentage rent we just won't let you borrow against it." That is consistent with the disclosure record - Realty Income excludes percentage rent from its annualized base rent definition entirely, and `knowledge/retail-benchmarks.md` records percentage rent at roughly 1.0% of Regency's total real estate revenue and 0.6% of tenant sales at Macerich. The skill should treat overage rent as a tenant-health signal and equity upside, and exclude it from the NOI used for DSCR and debt yield.

### STNL and CTL are a different underwriting object

- PGIM Private Capital's published CTL program offers "Up to 100% loan-to-value" calibrated on the present value of future rental obligations, "$25 million - $100+ million per transaction," and "Maturities of 20+ years with fully customized amortization schedules," targeting investment grade corporate, not-for-profit, and government tenants with below investment grade considered case by case.
- CREFC's watchlist explicitly excludes CTL, ground leases, and single tenant NNN from the DSCR triggers, and excludes fully amortizing CTL loans from the major-tenant-rollover trigger where the lease is co-terminus with the loan. That is the mechanical reason CTL proceeds are lease-credit-driven rather than property-cash-flow-driven.
- Realty Income's FY2025 filing sets the scale reference for what "credit tenant" means at portfolio level: 32.2% of annualized base rent from investment grade clients, top 20 clients at 35.8% of ABR, WALT 8.8 years, 97.8% single-tenant.
- Practical consequence: a below-investment-grade STNL, a lease with landlord obligations, or a lease shorter than the requested loan term is a conventional retail loan wearing an STNL label. Size it that way.

### Owner-user is a structural gate, not a pricing choice

SBA's 504 page states a maximum 504 loan of $5.5 million, requires a for-profit operating business that meets SBA size standards, and lists among prohibited uses "Speculation or investment in rental real estate." Owner-occupancy percentage thresholds are administered through SBA SOP and the certified development company, not stated on the program page, so they must be confirmed with the CDC rather than assumed. An investor-owned center is disqualified from 504 as a matter of program design, not negotiation.

### Bank appetite is set upstream of the asset

OCC Bulletin 2006-46 sets the interagency screening criteria: construction, land development, and other land loans at "100 percent or more of the institution's total risk-based capital," or total CRE loans at "300 percent or more of the institution's total risk-based capital" where the CRE book "has increased by 50 percent or more during the prior 36 months." Exceeding either results in the institution being "identified for further supervisory analysis" - not a hard cap, but the reason a clean grocery-anchored center is sometimes declined by a bank that likes it.

### A maturity problem has a regulatory path that is not the same as a good refinance

FIL-23-2023 tells examiners that institutions engaged in prudent CRE loan accommodations and workouts "will not be subject to criticism for engaging in these efforts, even if these arrangements result in modified loans that have weaknesses," and that modified loans to borrowers able to repay "will not be subject to adverse classification solely because the value of the underlying collateral has declined." The skill should use this to describe what a bank can do at maturity, and should not let it substitute for a refinance test that actually clears.

### Rating agency practice is a haircut, not a value acceptance

KBRA's published methodology summary confirms the income capitalization approach: sustainable net cash flow (KNCF) "is divided by a long term, asset-specific KBRA capitalization rate." The detailed criteria sit behind registration. The takeaway usable here is directional only - expect a credit committee to underwrite to a sustainable cash flow below the broker or issuer NOI, and to a value derived from that cash flow rather than from an appraisal. `knowledge/retail-lender-criteria.md` already carries the quantified KBRA pool example; this note does not restate it.

## Benchmark and Formula Decisions

Suitable as repo defaults for this skill:

- The CREFC v8.4 watchlist triggers, used as a pre-screen for permanent-loan cleanliness. They are published, standards-body, and quantified.
- The practitioner sizing bands already in `knowledge/retail-lender-criteria.md` (LTV roughly 60%-75%, DSCR roughly 1.25x-1.35x, debt yield roughly 8%-10%), always labeled directional and dated, and always subordinate to the tightest test.
- Exclusion of percentage rent from debt-service-capacity NOI.
- Six-lane matrix (bank, life company, CMBS, debt fund / bridge, CTL for STNL, SBA 504 owner-user) matching `knowledge/retail-lender-criteria.md`.
- Structure mechanisms named without dollar amounts: upfront TI/LC reserve, springing anchor reserve on non-renewal notice, free-rent and gap-rent holdback, springing lockbox, excess-cash trap, letter of credit, milestone and extension tests.

Deliberately case-by-case, not defaulted:

- Loan per square foot by format. No published band exists.
- Reserve dollar amounts and per-unit reserve PSF. Lenders size these to the actual rent roll, lease documents, and property condition report.
- Recourse posture. Published life company material shows non-recourse, limited recourse, and full recourse all in use with springing carve-outs; the carve-out schedule is deal-specific.
- Interest rates, spreads, and index levels. Not stated in this note or the skill.
- SBA occupancy percentages. Administered through SOP and the CDC.

Formulas are not restated. DSCR, debt yield, LTV, and loan constant come from `knowledge/underwriting-calc.md`; severity weighting comes from `knowledge/risk-scoring.md`. The only arithmetic the skill introduces is the natural breakpoint check (annual minimum rent / percentage rate), used to confirm that percentage rent has been correctly excluded from sizing NOI.

## Conflicting Source Resolution

- Sector health versus loan performance: the OCC calls retail "a bright spot" while Trepp shows retail CMBS delinquency at 6.96% in July 2026. These are not in conflict once separated - the OCC is describing space-market fundamentals (vacancy and availability), and Trepp is describing legacy loan collateral, concentrated in mall and outlet product, hitting balloon maturities. The skill states both and resolves toward asset-level evidence.
- Lane availability: MBA shows life company originations down 27% year over year while a life company broker page advertises open parameters. Origination volume data was given priority over marketing material for the availability claim; the marketing material was used only for the parameter ranges it publishes.
- Sizing bands: no primary source publishes them and practitioner sources agree with each other closely. They are admitted only as directional and only because they reconcile with the ranges already accepted in `knowledge/retail-lender-criteria.md`.
- Value: appraised value versus rating agency sustainable cash flow value. Rating agency practice was given priority for describing what a credit committee will do, because KBRA publishes its method and appraisals are transaction-specific.
- Where a lender's actual term sheet or credit policy differs from anything above, the term sheet controls. The skill says so.

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

- Anchor owns its pad or tract. The anchor is then not a lease at all, and REA / OEA provisions the borrower does not control govern parking, access, and site plan. Lender diligence shifts from lease review to REA review, and no reserve fixes a control problem.
- Shadow-anchored centers. Regency's disclosure describes retailers adjacent to but not within the center. Their closure damages traffic and can trigger inline co-tenancy language while giving the landlord no remedy at all.
- Anchor dark but paying. DSCR looks fine, CREFC 4F would already flag it, inline sales fall, and co-tenancy cure clocks may be running. Do not let in-place coverage carry the verdict.
- Percentage rent-heavy NOI in mall and outlet formats. Excluding it from sizing NOI is correct and will materially cut proceeds relative to a broker pro forma.
- STNL where the lease term is shorter than the requested loan term, or where landlord obligations survive. CTL treatment does not apply; residual value and re-tenanting cost drive the loan.
- Ground-leased pads. CREFC excludes ground leases from DSCR triggers, and remaining ground lease term versus loan term becomes the binding constraint.
- Recovery leakage from CAM caps, base years, admin fee limits, gross leases, and anchor fixed-CAM deals. The billed-to-recoverable gap belongs in sizing NOI, not in an upside case.
- Owner-user requests on investment property. Structural SBA disqualification.
- Maturity default already in progress. FIL-23-2023 describes what a bank may prudently do; it is not evidence that a refinance clears. Counsel and the servicer, not this skill, drive that path.
- Any co-tenancy, exclusive, go-dark, radius, or REA interpretation question. State law specific; requires counsel.

## Open Questions

- No primary or standards-body source publishes retail loan-per-square-foot bands by format. Deferred.
